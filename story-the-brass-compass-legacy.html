The Brass Compass Legacy

Fictional story. Depicts corporate conflict, class prejudice, and dramatic confrontation.

Chapter 1: The Sanctuary of Glass and Bronze

High above the windswept harbor of Northport, the Oakhaven Heritage Auction Hall stood as a fortress of polished granite and floor-to-ceiling smoked glass. Inside, the atmosphere was strictly climate-controlled—chilled to exactly sixty-four degrees and scented with beeswax, lavender polish, and the dry, metallic tang of antique copper. Massive wrought-iron chandeliers suspended from vaulted oak beams cast warm spotlight beams over velvet-lined glass cases. Within those displays rested rare architectural artifacts: eighteenth-century brass astrolabes, hand-carved mahogany blueprints, and gilded maritime clocks designed for long-lost colonial trading ships.

At the center of the main showroom floor stood Clara Montrose, the gallery's thirty-nine-year-old managing director. Sharp-featured and relentlessly immaculate, Clara wore a tailored dark navy suit with silk lapels that mirrored the cold precision of her management style. Her high heels clicked against the polished black slate floor like a metronome marking exact time. She inspected a glass case containing a silver-plated chronometer, adjusting her thin wire-rimmed spectacles as she murmured into a micro-headset linked to her floor staff. To Clara, history was not a tapestry of human craft; it was a high-yield asset class reserved exclusively for the ultra-wealthy.
