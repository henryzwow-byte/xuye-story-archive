The Cold Beacon

Trapped in an unheated annex during a coastal ice storm, Clara is abandoned by her husband Julian for a family vanity gala. Armed with emergency telemetry logs and security data, she reclaims her life.

Fictional story. Contains depictions of structural collapse, domestic neglect, and hypothermia.

Chapter 1: The Ice on the Ridge

The gale hit Cape Perpetua just after five in the afternoon, driving freezing rain against the cedar shingles of the historic timber studio. Clara Vance wiped a layer of sanding dust from her apron and looked out at the Pacific, where gray waves smashed against the basalt cliffs two hundred feet below. At thirty-four, Clara was a heritage conservator, spending her winters restoring early twentieth-century maritime artifacts. Inside the unheated annex attached to their remote cabin, the air tasted of salt, old pine, and the sharp metallic tang of kerosene from the small space heater she had ignited an hour earlier.

In the adjacent doorway stood her husband, Julian Vance, impeccably groomed in a tailored midnight-blue tuxedo. He was fastening a pair of sterling silver cufflinks, his reflection mirrored in the dark window glass. That evening marked the golden jubilee of the Vance Architectural Foundation in Portland, an event hosted by his domineering father, Arthur Vance. For months, Julian had treated the anniversary gala as the supreme pivot of his career, a night meant to secure his elevation to senior partner. He glanced at his gold watch, then down at Clara’s paint-splattered work boots with subtle disdain.
