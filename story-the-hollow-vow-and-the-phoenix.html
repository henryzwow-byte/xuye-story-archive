FICTIONAL STORY · A-112

The Hollow Vow and the Phoenix

At his cathedral wedding, Julian rejects the mother who warned him about his bride—only for a federal investigation to reveal an international fraud scheme and force mother and son to rebuild together.

Fictional story. Contains identity fraud, arrest, family conflict, and threats from organized crime.

Chapter 1: The Hollow Vow

The Grand Cathedral of St. Jude was bathed in a light so artificial it felt like a stage production. Thousands of white lilies imported from the Netherlands lined the aisles, their scent heavy, almost medicinal. Julian Vance, the heir to the Vance logistics empire, stood at the altar, his posture stiff in a bespoke white tuxedo that cost more than most people earned in a decade.

He was sweating, though the air conditioning was set to a crisp sixty-five degrees. Beside him stood Elena, the woman he had met three months ago in the VIP lounge of a luxury hotel in Monaco. She was breathtaking—a creature of polished marble and silken hair, possessing a grace that made her seem like royalty.

But Julian’s focus wasn't on Elena. It was on the third row, where his mother, Beatrice Vance, sat alone.

Beatrice was a woman of iron and lace, the matriarch who had built the Vance fortune from the ashes of her husband’s bankruptcy. She had fought Julian on this marriage from day one. She claimed Elena was a fraud, a social climber with a history that didn't exist before she stepped onto the European circuit. Julian had called her bitter, controlling, and obsessed with maintaining her own grip on his life.

"Get out of my wedding!" Julian’s voice boomed, echoing off the stained glass. The organ music ground to a discordant halt. "You are no longer my family, Mother. You’ve humiliated Elena for the last time with your 'investigations' and your petty gossip. I’m done. Leave."

The guests—a collection of executives, politicians, and socialites—gasped. This was the social scandal of the century.

Beatrice stood up. She didn't cry. She didn't plead. She wore a midnight blue gown that made her look like a judge at an execution. She looked at Julian, her eyes holding a terrifyingly calm clarity.

"Alright, Julian," she whispered, her voice carrying in the sudden, abyssal silence of the cathedral. "Just check your phone before you say 'I do'."

She turned her back, her posture regal, and walked down the aisle. She didn't look back as the heavy oak doors swung open and then shut with a hollow, final thud.

Julian stood there, his chest heaving, adrenaline masking the sudden, cold hollow in his gut. He turned to Elena, expecting her to look hurt or perhaps grateful for his defense. Instead, Elena was staring at her own reflection in the polished wood of the altar, her face an unreadable mask of frozen panic.

Then, the ping of a notification vibrated in Julian’s pocket. Then another. Then a dozen more from every guest in the room.

The heavy doors at the back of the cathedral burst open again. This time, it wasn't Beatrice. It was a man in a maroon suit—a senior official from the Federal Department of Identity and Fraud. He marched down the aisle, the click of his heels sounding like gunfire.

"Halt the ceremony," the official commanded.

Julian’s hand flew to his pocket, his thumb swiping open his phone. His breath caught in his throat. It was an email, sent to every guest in the cathedral, from a verified government server.

Attached was a high-resolution photograph of a woman. A woman who looked exactly like Elena, but wearing an intake uniform, taken four years earlier in a facility in Prague. The name on the file wasn't Elena. It was Svetlana Volkova.
