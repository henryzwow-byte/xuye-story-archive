FICTIONAL STORY · A-254

Echoes Over Sentinel Ridge

When sound archivist Hannah Vance returns to her uncle's coastal Oregon homestead, she finds him isolated under the guise of dementia while ambitious relatives dismantle the estate. But her uncle, a former radio engineer, has engineered a mechanical failsafe hidden in plain sound.

Fictional story. Contains themes of elder exploitation, deception, and family dispute. All characters, locations, and events are fictitious.

Chapter 1: The Silence at Sentinel Ridge

The coastal fog off the Oregon Pacific ran thick against the cedar slopes of Sentinel Ridge, swallowing the headlights of my station wagon as I climbed the access road. For seven months, I had been isolated in Alaska recording low-frequency acoustic patterns of glacier fracturing for the Western Geophysical Institute. Out on the ice shelf, sound carried for miles, clear and unvarnished. Returning home to the misty timberlands was supposed to smell of wet pine, woodsmoke, and tide. Instead, an unsettled quiet hung over the tree line.

The ancestral property sat on fifty acres of oceanfront Sitka spruce, anchored by a timber home built by my great-grandfather in 1912. The jewel of the estate was the brass workshop and coastal radio tower maintained for forty years by my uncle, Arthur Vance. Arthur was a retired maritime communications technician and master horologist—a man who spent his life calibrating foghorns and repairing antique pendulum clocks. When I left in April, his workshop was a hive of ticking escapements and vacuum tubes.
