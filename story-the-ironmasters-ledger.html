The Ironmaster's Ledger

After her mentor passes away, architectural restorer Nora Vance inherits controlling interest in a century-old historic ironworks. But when her opportunistic relatives stage a safety default using a backdated agreement, Nora must rely on forensic metallurgy and her mentor's secret archive to save her heritage.

Fictional story. Contains themes of family conflict, attempted corporate fraud, and corporate inheritance disputes.

Chapter 1: The Cold Iron Foundry

The Hudson River in late October was the color of scraped slate. Inside the main workshop of Vance Heritage Ironworks, mist drifted through the high clerestory windows, carrying the sharp scent of damp coal dust, oxidized iron, and cold motor oil. Nora Vance stood at the heavy oak workbench, her leather apron stained with polishing wax as she adjusted a micrometric clamp against a section of an 1890 wrought-iron decorative gate. The gate belonged to the town's historic municipal library, and restoring its delicate cast-acorn scrollwork had consumed her evenings for three weeks.

Arthur Vance, her uncle and mentor, had built the foundry into upstate New York's premier architectural preservation studio. For fifteen years, he had taught Nora how to read the grain of forged iron, how to temper steel without brittle stress, and how to respect the integrity of old structures. Arthur had passed away eight weeks ago from sudden heart failure, leaving behind a silent workshop, a mountain of restoration blueprints, and an overwhelming stillness that Nora felt every time she turned off the main power breakers at dusk.
