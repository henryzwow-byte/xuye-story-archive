The Gilded Escapement

Nora Harlow has spent years restoring historic maritime chronometers in relative obscurity. On the morning of her fellowship evaluation, her family attempts to derail her career through a calculated act of sabotage, forcing her to rebuild her art and her life from the gears up.

Fictional story. Depicts familial sabotage, emotional abuse, workplace injury, and legal disputes. All characters and events are fictitious.

Chapter 1: The Brass Dial

The steady click of a micro-lathe was the only sound inside the small cellar workshop on Water Street. Nora Harlow adjusted her magnifying visor, her breath steady against the cool brass ring of an 1850s maritime chronometer balance wheel. For eight years, she had spent her late nights in this damp New Bedford basement, restoring vintage navigation instruments that others had long abandoned to rust.

Her workbench was an altar of small precision: velvet-lined trays of ruby jewel bearings, coiled steel mainsprings, hair-thin tweezers, and vials of whale-tooth watch oil. On the wall above her desk hung a framed blueprint of a detent escapement—a gift from her late grandfather, Samuel Harlow. Samuel had been a chief horologist for the Atlantic whaling fleet, a patient man who had placed a brass gear in six-year-old Nora’s palm and whispered, 'A clock doesn't care about your feelings, Nora. It only respects truth. If a single tooth is off by a hundredth of a millimeter, the whole machine stops.'
