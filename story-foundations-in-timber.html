Foundations in Timber

When an arrogant developer attempts to erase his wife's years of labor and reclaim his estranged birth mother at a public gala, his nineteen-year-old son steps to the podium with proof of who truly raised him and restored the landmark.

Fictional story. Contains themes of marital breakdown, public dispute, and family estrangement.

Chapter 1: The Glass Rotunda

The cold evening rain off Penobscot Bay tapped a steady, rhythmic cadence against the soaring glass dome of the newly restored Blackwood Timber & Glass Conservatory. Inside the sprawling rotunda, heavy brass oil lamps cast a deep amber glow over hand-carved white oak trusses, restored stained-glass windows, and mirror-polished slate flagstones. Nora Hayes stood near the massive granite hearth, absently rubbing the hardened calluses on her right palm—the quiet marks of twelve long years spent soldering intricate lead came, planing heavy timbers, and hauling salvaged architectural glass from abandoned coastal structures. Beside her stood nineteen-year-old Liam, immaculate in his first tailored charcoal suit, his fingers resting with nervous reverence on the leather-bound architectural portfolio that had just earned him the National Heritage Conservation Guild's prestigious Young Artisan Fellowship.

Looking at the young man, Nora felt a quiet, trembling warmth bloom in her chest. Twelve years ago, when she married Richard Hayes, Liam had been a silent, hollow-eyed seven-year-old who hid under the dining table during thunderstorms and kept a small wooden apprentice toolbox under his bed like a shield. His biological mother, Vivienne, had abandoned them to move to London, dismissing the windswept Maine coast as an unbearable provincial backwater and pursuing a lavish life among international investment circles. Richard had initially framed Vivienne’s sudden exit as a temporary family tragedy, but as months turned into years, it became painfully clear that Richard loved the performative status of fatherhood far more than its grueling daily demands.
