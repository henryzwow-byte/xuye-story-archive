FICTIONAL STORY · A-242

The Cold Breakwater

When a stormy coast leaves a quiet fourteen-year-old stranded at a dark ferry terminal, a dock mechanic intervenes before his demanding father arrives, forcing a reckoning between cold expectations and genuine care.

Fictional story. Contains themes of parental emotional distance, childhood anxiety, and family reconciliation.

Chapter 1: The Cold Breakwater

The November wind off Blackwood Bay carried the bitter bite of frozen salt and wet diesel fuel. By eight o'clock in the evening, the ferry landing was usually deserted, stripped of tourists and commuter traffic by the onset of northern winter. Clara Vance swung her heavy iron flashlight across the gravel apron, the beam cutting through gray tendrils of sea fog that hung thick over the wooden piers. At thirty-one, Clara had spent seven years maintaining the backup generators and hydraulic lifts for the municipal harbor department. She knew every creak of the timber pilings and every turn of the harbor's tide. But tonight, as she completed her final perimeter check before securing the generator shed, something unusual caught the beam of her light near the abandoned salt storehouse at the edge of the breakwater.

Tucked into the shadow of a rusted gravel hopper sat a figure so still it almost looked like discarded canvas. Clara shifted her heavy leather tool roll across her shoulder and took three deliberate steps closer. The beam illuminated a boy who could not have been older than fourteen. He was wearing dark dress slacks, a thin navy wool blazer, and wet leather oxford shoes that were coated in slushy sea mud. His knees were drawn tightly against his chest, and his hands were tucked deep into his armpits in a futile effort to stay warm. He was shivering violently, his jaw locked so hard that his teeth clicked rhythmically against the silence of the night.
