The Silent Escapement

A historic horologist in coastal Washington reclaims her family's maritime legacy when her ex-husband attempts to convert her grandfather's clocktower into a luxury resort lounge.

Fictional story. Contains themes of marital divorce, corporate conflict, and family emotional stress.

Chapter 1: The Midnight Notice

The digital clock on the workbench read 1:45 in the morning when the notification chimed. Outside the heavy timber frame of the workshop, the Pacific Northwest rain tapped against the corrugated tin roof in a steady, relentless rhythm. The harbor below Cape Alder was quiet, save for the low groan of foghorns echoing across the estuary. Tessa Vance sat motionless in her leather work apron, a magnifying loupe pushed up on her forehead, holding a polished brass gear no larger than a silver dollar between her tweezers.

She had not slept soundly since the divorce papers were signed three weeks ago. For eighteen years, she had built her life around Grant Sterling’s ascending corporate career, balancing his high-stakes real estate summits while maintaining her family's historic horological shop by the waterfront. When Grant stood in their kitchen last month and announced he was ending their marriage, he had delivered the news with the clinical detachment of a corporate board meeting. He was leaving her for Beatrice Vance-Montgomery—a thirty-two-year-old hospitality heiress whose family backed his luxury resort ventures. He had spoken smooth phrases about incompatible trajectories, personal growth, and the necessity of a clean break. Tessa had kept her posture straight and her voice calm, refusing to grant him the dramatic, tearful breakdown he seemed to expect.
