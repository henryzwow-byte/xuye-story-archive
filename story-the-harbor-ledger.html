The Harbor Ledger

When marine engineer Julian Vance finds his child crouched under oilcloth on a rainy Washington pier, he realizes the monthly support he sent was stolen by his own aunt. Confronting his past at a high-society gala, Julian uncovers forged records and a dockmaster's ledger that rewrite three bitter years.

Fictional story. Contains themes of family deception, financial betrayal, and emotional hardship.

Chapter 1: The Cold Dock

The rain over Oakhaven Bay tasted of salt and pine smoke, drifting across the timber piers in heavy, gray curtains. Inside the glass pavilion of the Oakhaven Yacht Club, warm light spilled over brass fixtures, mahogany tables, and crystal stemware. The annual gala of the Vance Maritime Historical Society was in full swing, celebrating the restoration of the sound's historic deep-water moorings.

Julian Vance adjusted his wool overcoat as he stepped out onto the upper balcony. At thirty-six, he had built a reputation across the Pacific Northwest as a meticulous structural engineer, a man who measured timber strength in stress loads and annual growth rings. Inside, his aunt Eleanor was raising a glass of sparkling cider to the Vance family legacy, praising four generations of maritime stewardship. But Julian needed air. The stifling warmth of the ballroom felt unearned.
