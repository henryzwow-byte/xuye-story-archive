The Red Box Beneath the Abyss

After a nurse comes home early and discovers her husband's betrayal, Brandon pushes her down the basement stairs, takes her phone and locks the door. Badly injured and cut off from help, she remembers the prepaid phone hidden in a red velvet box—a wedding gift from her father, Salvatore Moretti, with a single emergency number. The call brings rescuers, but she insists that the fight proceed on her terms. Recordings, altered documents and nearly fourteen million dollars in loans made with her forged signature turn a private act of violence into a public reckoning over coercion, fraud and the right to choose her own future.

Fiction. Contains domestic violence, a fall down stairs, serious leg injuries, unlawful imprisonment, denial of medical care, coercive control, infidelity, financial fraud, police intervention, courtroom proceedings and imprisonment.

Chapter One: The Fall Below

The first thing I heard after falling was my own screams hitting the concrete walls and bouncing back to my ears. The second thing was Brandon’s voice coming from above. He showed no sign of shock at what he had done.

Severe pain tore my left leg, and white light flashed in front of my eyes. My ankle twisted as I fell, and something deep in my calf pulsed violently with every heartbeat. I clung to the bottom step, gasping for air.

Three steps up from me, Brandon was wearing the same navy suit he wore to work in the morning. His tie was straight without a wrinkle. One of his shiny leather shoes clicked on the steps, as if he were casually pausing in the middle of an ordinary conversation.

Behind him, Vanessa Vale stood in the kitchen light, wearing my cream robe. The belt hung loosely around her waist. Her bare feet were still painted with crimson nail polish—she once told me Brandon’s favorite color.

“That’s exactly why I told you not to question her,” Brandon said.

There was a soft laugh in her voice.

“Your robe? Honey, he bought them all.”

The argument had started twenty minutes earlier, when I unexpectedly came home early from my nursing class at Greenwich Memorial. The supervisor sent me home before noon because a patient transfer was canceled. I felt quite lucky at the time.

Brandon and I have barely spoken over the past week. He claimed several developments were in financial trouble and the pressure forced him to stay in the office. I thought going home early would buy us both some time together.

However, when I walked into the kitchen, I found Vanessa sitting at the island counter drinking red wine. She and Brandon worked together for nearly four years. Officially, he is the director of acquisitions for his development company.

His unofficial status is the reason why his mobile phone is always held with the screen facing down. She looked at me with no trace of surprise on her face. Brandon’s white shirt was folded and placed on one of the bar stools.

My robe was on her.

“What are you doing here?” I asked.

Vanessa raised her glass.

“To make you understand completely.”

She told me that Brandon was preparing to file for divorce. She told me that the house would be his. She told me that as long as I kept quiet, I could get what he thought was reasonable alimony.

“You should be grateful that he was willing to let you go with dignity.”

Vanessa stood between me and the counter.

“You should wait until Brandon gets home.”

“I’m going to call my lawyer.”

Just then, Brandon walked in from the garage. He didn’t ask why Vanessa was wearing my robe. He looked at me as if he were an uninvited guest interrupting his meeting.

“Give me your phone,” he said.

“Celeste, don’t make this too ugly.”

“It was already ugly before I walked in.”

Vanessa walked up behind him.

“I told you to get out of my house.”

“Both of our names are on the deed.”

“So is your neglected marriage.”

I walked towards the corridor.

“You can’t even walk out of this house and call someone until we sort this out.”

I turned and walked toward the basement door, since that was the closest path around him.

“Don’t be unreasonable like a child.”

This was not an unintentional push in the middle of an argument. His palms hit my shoulders at the same time. My heel missed the first basement step. The armrest slipped from my hands. Then my leg broke at the bottom.

Now I lay on the cold concrete while the man I married watched me gasping for air in excruciating pain.

“Call an ambulance,” I said.

Brandon took two steps down. Vanessa shifted behind him.

“Brandon, maybe we should-”

He shut him up with just one look. Then he came down and squatted beside me. His hand went into my cardigan pocket. I knew it before he even pulled out his phone.

“Brenton, give me your phone back.”

“Don’t try to ruin my reputation just because you lost your balance.”

“I’m a trauma nurse. I may have a broken leg.”

“Then you should know that you can make it through the night.”

The coldness in his voice frightened me more than the fall itself. For a second, guilt flashed across her face. Then she glanced at Brandon and turned her head away. He pulled an old wool blanket out of the storage bin and threw it on top of me.

“Tomorrow morning,” he said, “you’ll tell everyone that you fell on your own.”

I pulled the blanket away from my face.

“Do you think I will sign your divorce agreement after what I’ve been through?”

Every word I said seemed to challenge his bottom line.

“I’ll make you sign whatever I put in front of you.”

“Pain changes one’s priorities.”

Vanessa whispered his name.

“Don’t embarrass me again,” he continued. “Don’t you ever question the company’s funding. Don’t call my employees behind my back. You have to accept the settlement and we can move on.”

“You mean you and Vanessa.”

“I mean everyone who is constantly interrupted by your daily routine.”

I stared hard at the man I had loved for seven years. After my mother died, he held my hand under the cold lights of a large hospital and assured me that I would never face my grief alone.

And now, he stood condescendingly over me, calculating how long a broken leg would keep me submissive.

“Do you think no one will come?” I asked.

“Your friend stopped replying to your messages months ago.”

Because he told them I was mentally unstable.

“Your co-workers thought you volunteered for the night shift.”

Because he replied to all my messages while I was asleep.

“You told me he was a retired businessman living in Chicago.”

“Then maybe he could send some flowers.”

Vanessa paused for another second.

“Celeste, just cooperate a little bit tomorrow.”

I looked straight at her.

“If you close that door, you’re choosing all of this.”

The basement door slammed shut. Darkness engulfed the entire room. I screamed for several minutes. The house is located at the end of a private road and is surrounded by trees and stone walls. Brandon bought it because he liked privacy.

And this privacy eventually became my iron cage. I lay still until the first wave of panic passed. Then, I remembered the red velvet box.
