FICTIONAL STORY · A-061

The Wounded Ring

Jewelry restorer Elise Navarro receives a damaged wedding ring from a refugee family and discovers inventory numbers engraved beneath an old repair. The markings lead to art and heirlooms displaced during wartime, forcing an auction house, a museum and the descendants of two families to confront ownership, memory and restitution without turning grief into spectacle.

Fiction. Contains wartime displacement, family loss, theft of cultural property, grief, institutional concealment and legal mediation. No graphic violence.

Chapter One: The Ring with a Split Band

Elise Navarro was asked to repair a plain gold wedding ring split nearly through the band. Its owner, eighty-two-year-old Miriam Weiss, had worn it since leaving a displaced-persons camp as a child. Miriam wanted it safe enough to give her granddaughter at an upcoming wedding.

Under magnification, Elise found a previous repair made with mismatched gold. When she removed the brittle solder, five tiny characters appeared inside the band: K-17-442. They were not a jeweler’s initials. Miriam recognized the format from labels her father once described. Before the war, her family operated a small gallery and kept a handwritten inventory. Most of the paintings and personal objects disappeared while the family was in hiding.

Elise paused the repair and documented every step. She would not polish away the numbers until Miriam decided what they meant. The ring’s wound had protected the only mark that could connect it to a larger history.
