FICTIONAL STORY · A-408

The Timber Mill Covenant

For five years, structural engineer Clara Vance funded and restored the historic Blackwood Timber Mill. But at the grand reopening banquet, when her husband and mother-in-law publicly demoted her to staff status and pledged the estate as collateral, Clara quietly pulled the plug on the entire operation.

Fictional story. Contains themes of marital financial exploitation, family manipulation, and emotional conflict.

Chapter 1: The Timber and the Iron

The heavy cedar beam overhead bore the deep, rusted marks of a nineteenth-century broadaxe, but its structural integrity was absolute. Clara Vance pulled her canvas work gloves off, ran a hand over the timber’s freshly stabilized face, and stepped back into the central gallery of the Blackwood Mill. Through the tall arched windows, the evening sun spilled across the Columbia River Valley, painting the restored fir trusses in shades of copper and warm gold. Outside, the great iron water wheel turned with a steady, rhythmic splash that vibrated pleasantly beneath her boots.

In the courtyard below, Julian Vance stood among a cluster of regional historic preservation board members and local journalists. He wore his tailored Harris tweed jacket, holding a glass of artisanal cider aloft as he gestured toward the soaring roofline. His voice carried clearly through the open double doors—confident, melodic, and polished by weeks of media rehearsal. He was telling them how he had saved the historic timber mill from collapse, how his eye for heritage craftsmanship had revived a forgotten Oregon landmark, and how the Vance family legacy was now anchored in this valley for generations to come.
