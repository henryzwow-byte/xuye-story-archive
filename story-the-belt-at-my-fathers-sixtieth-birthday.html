FICTIONAL STORY · A-023

My Father Raised His Belt at His Sixtieth Birthday Party

At Ernesto's sixtieth birthday party, his three-year-old granddaughter Camila is rushed to the hospital after he swings a belt and she strikes the kitchen floor. As relatives close ranks and try to erase the evidence, Camila's mother chooses her child's safety over family loyalty—and forces the truth into court.

Fiction. Contains child physical abuse, a head injury and hospital treatment, family intimidation, evidence tampering, victim blaming and legal proceedings.

Chapter One: The Dull Sound of Impact

The music stopped before anyone could scream. That’s the clearest part of my memory. One second, a band outside my parents’ yard was playing an old ranchera, and relatives were laughing under a white party tent.

The next second, there was only a heavy, hollow muffled sound in the kitchen. My three-year-old daughter hit the back of her head hard on the tile floor. Then there was dead silence. Camila slumped in her yellow dress next to the refrigerator, one sandal lace twisted beneath her ankle, a can of red soda gurgling away from her limp hands.

My father stood above her. Ernesto Ramírez took off his belt. The belt buckle hung near his knees. His other hand was still raised high, as if the interrupted punishment should have been completed.

I threw myself to the ground.

“Camila?”

She didn’t answer. Blood seeped out from the back of her head, first as a thin red line, and then spread into a sheet. I grabbed a clean dishcloth on the counter and pressed it firmly against the wound.

“Kami, my baby, look at mommy.”

Her eyes were closed. My husband, Diego, had already started calling emergency services.

“The three-year-old girl has a head injury,” he said, his voice trembling but precise. “She is unconscious and bleeding. Please send an ambulance.”

Outside, guests crowded around the glass patio doors. The adults were still holding paper plates with half-eaten cakes on them. My brother Raúl stood beneath the birthday banner, staring blankly. My sister Maribel came into the kitchen, her eyes flickering between Camila and the belt.

She didn’t ask if my daughter was still breathing.

“Someone has to teach her the rules,” she said.

I looked up.

“What did you say?”

My mother, Socorro, came through the doorway in a meticulously pressed beige dress. For one desperate second, I thought she would kneel beside us. Instead, she glanced at the blood, then at the guests watching from the porch.

“Your daughter asked for it.”

“She’s only three years old.”

“She took something that didn’t belong to her.”

“A can of soda.”

“Your father said not to do it.”

Camilla let out a weak choked sob. I carefully turned her onto her side while continuing to apply pressure on the wound. Diego put down his phone.

“The dispatcher said not to move her as long as she wasn’t vomiting or stopping breathing.”

My father finally lowered his arms.

“It’s time for her to have a better memory now so she won’t steal things later.”

I stared at that belt.

“You were just going to whip her.”

“She needs discipline.”

“She’s only three years old.”

“She understands the word 'no.'”

“She thought she could get a drink.”

“She was mistaken.”

I almost stood up. Almost crossing the distance between us. I almost forgot that Camila needed my sobriety now more than Ernesto being punished. So I knelt there and didn’t move. Anger is simple. Restraint is hard work.

I worked as a prosecutor for many years before transitioning into private criminal defense. I have seen too many defendants ruin their defenses by nagging them once they felt they had control. My father firmly believed that this room belonged to him.

his house. His birthday. His family. His rules. So I let him talk.

“Why did you take off your belt?” I asked.

Socorro stepped forward.

“Lucia, don’t interrogate him.”

“I’m asking my father.”

Ernesto glanced at the witnesses gathered on the porch.

“She’s too presumptuous.”

“Why are you so presumptuous?”

“She doesn’t listen to me.”

“She apologized.”

“An apology cannot erase disobedience.”

“Did you wave your belt?”

He gritted his teeth.

“She jumped out of the way before I could touch her.”

Several guests lowered their eyes. This is not a denial. This is called technical correction. He was arguing that the belt didn’t really hit her. But he also admitted that he did lift the belt.

The sirens of ambulances sounded from far to near. Camilla’s eyelashes twitched.

“Mom?”

The voice was so soft that I almost thought it was my imagination.

“I’m here.”

Her features clenched painfully.

“I have a headache.”

“I know, honey, the ambulance will be here soon.”

She tried to turn back to Ernesto. Fear flooded into her eyes.

“No belt.”

My father exhaled impatiently.

“See? She’s awake.”

Diego stood up.

“Stay away from her.”

Ernesto advanced on him.

“This is my house.”

“And she’s my daughter.”

Raul walked into the kitchen.

“Everyone, calm down.”

I looked at him.

“Take the belt away from Dad.”

Raul hesitated. That hesitation had already made the choice for him before he spoke.

“Lucia, Dad was impulsive, and Camila fell by herself. Let the doctor handle it.”

“He actually held up the belt to a toddler.”

“He won’t really hit you hard.”

My stomach turned. I won’t hit you hard. The standard of this family has never been to do anything to children. It’s about how many excuses they can make for advocating such violence afterwards. Paramedics rushed into the kitchen at 4:27 p.m.

A female paramedic named Andrea knelt next to Camilla while her partner immobilized the child’s cervical spine.

“What’s going on?”

I answered before anyone else could speak.

“My father cornered her with a belt. She backed up, slipped on the liquid and hit her head.”

Socorro interjected:

“She’s running.”

“She didn’t.”

Ernesto added:

“She fell backward on her own.”

Andrea glanced at the belt still in his hand.

“Sir, please put it on the counter.”

Ernesto tightened his fingers.

“This is mine.”

“Put it down.”

For the first time today, an authority he respected more than his daughter gave him an order. He placed the belt next to the birthday cake knife. Paramedics put a neck brace on Camila and put her on a stretcher.

I climbed into the ambulance. Diego followed in his car. My parents followed suit. Not because they’re worried. It was because they wanted to get the story under control before I conspired with others. Arriving at the hospital, Dr. Evelyn Brooks met the stretcher in the emergency corridor.

She rushed through the door pushing a mobile hospital bed as nurses cut part of Camila’s yellow dress to connect to a monitor. I ran beside them. Diego held my hand for three seconds before the medical team moved faster than we could keep up.

Behind us, Socorro shouted:

“Your daughter deserves it.”

I stopped. Turn around. Between my parents and the hospital bed that was disappearing down the hall. Ernesto came over again holding the belt. He must have picked it up again when he left home.

He pointed with his belt at the emergency room door.

“Someone has to teach her what respect is.”

I waved his hand away. His face fell. I took out my phone, turned on the recording function, and hung it by my leg.

“She’s only three years old.”

Dr. Brooks turned from the door. Her eyes fell on the belt. Then it fell on my father.

“Keep that man away from my patient.”

Socorro grabbed Ernesto’s wrist.

“Shut up.”

He jerked her away.

“She’s indulging a spoiled child.”

I raised my phone. The red recording light on the screen flashes. My father saw it. His majesty collapsed into alertness. My mother’s mouth dropped open. I dangled my phone between us.

“Say it again.”

No one moved.

“This time, be louder.”

The emergency room door closed behind my daughter with a heavy metallic thud. My father stared at the phone. Today, for the first time, fear crept onto his face.
