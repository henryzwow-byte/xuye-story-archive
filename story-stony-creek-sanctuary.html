Stony Creek Sanctuary

When a specialized craftsman monitors remote sensors during his wife's recovery from a coastal injury, he uncovers an insidious campaign of boundary violation that threatens his family's survival.

Fictional story. Contains depictions of emotional boundary violations, familial manipulation, and physical vulnerability following a medical injury.

Chapter 1: The Sanctuary at Salt-Spray Point

The Atlantic wind always rattled the northern panes of the timber cottage at Salt-Spray Point, but that autumn afternoon, the sound was muted by heavy wool drapes. Inside the ground-floor bedroom, Julian Vance adjusted the digital environmental sensor mounted beside the doorway. The device glowed with a faint green indicator, confirming that ambient light remained below twenty lux and room noise did not exceed thirty decibels.

Three weeks earlier, Julian’s wife, Clara, a marine biologist, had been caught in a severe gale while conducting offshore sampling in Penobscot Bay. A heavy deck boom had snapped, inflicting a complex vestibular concussion and spinal nerve trauma. Though released from the regional medical center, her recovery demanded total sensory containment: low light, strict physical rest, timed anti-inflammatory infusions, and absolute isolation from sudden shocks or stair climbing.
