Pressure at Blackwood Ridge

When a severe alpine freeze strikes the Blackwood Ridge Hydroelectric Facility during an off-duty engineer's quiet shuttle wait, a fatal pressure surge forces her to reveal her true expertise before a room of skeptics.

Fictional story. Depicts an emergency industrial hydro-engineering scenario, intense environmental crisis management, and technical strain without physical harm.

Chapter 1: The Gathering Frost

The lobby of the Blackwood Ridge Hydroelectric Facility smelled of ozone, machine oil, and the sharp dampness of mountain pine. Outside the floor-to-ceiling glass, an early October frost was coating the fir trees across the Cascade gorge, turning the twilight into a sheet of hammered lead. Inside, a heavy brass pendulum clock ticked against the concrete wall, its steady rhythm competing with the faint, deep thrum of the subterranean turbines four hundred feet below.

Dr. Laura Mercer sat on a low bench near the visitor entrance, wearing a faded navy fleece, scuffed leather boots, and a pair of wire-rimmed reading glasses that kept sliding down her nose. A heavy hardcover technical ledger sat on her lap, its cover worn at the corners. She turned the pages slowly, making small, neat annotations with a wooden mechanical pencil. To anyone passing through the atrium, she looked like an overworked graduate student waiting for a late shuttle down the mountain after a long week of cataloging water samples.
