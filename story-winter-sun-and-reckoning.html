FICTIONAL STORY · A-036

Winter Sun and Reckoning

At Natalie's baby shower, her own mother shoves her through a pair of doors, triggering a medical emergency and the premature birth of her daughter. What first looks like a vicious family outburst is soon exposed as a paid conspiracy: her mother and mother-in-law planned to exploit the crisis, seize control of the marriage and rewrite the family's finances. With her husband Grant beside her, Natalie follows the security footage and bank transfers toward a reckoning—and builds a safer meaning of family for her child.

Fiction. Contains an assault on a pregnant woman, a fall, premature labor and birth, medical emergencies, family abuse, coercive control, financial fraud, police investigation, imprisonment and courtroom proceedings.

Chapter One: The Push at the Baby Shower

At the baby shower before my baby girl was born, my mom pushed me so hard that even the elegant music stopped before I fell to the ground.

One second ago, I was standing in the center of the Magnolia Conservatory in Nashville, Tennessee, with a glass dome and hanging white orchids above my head, surrounded by sixty guests, with happy smiles on my face. My husband, Grant, was standing at the dessert table with a microphone, trying to read aloud the title on a baby shower game card: “Guess what this lullaby is.” A string quartet in the corner was playing a soft, expensive tune. The three-tiered cake covered in candied peonies and gold leaf shimmered in the afternoon sun as if it had just been lifted out of a bridal magazine.

Then, my mother’s palm hit me hard between my shoulders. That wasn’t a stumble. Not an accident. Not an accidental collision in a crowded room. It was a long-planned, vicious blow from the woman who gave birth to me and raised me.

I still remember the sound of air being squeezed out of my lungs. A sharp, animalistic gasp. I remember my best friend Madison’s horrified face, her champagne glass still hanging in the air. I remember the sheer terror I felt as I twisted in mid-air, the terror that burned away all thoughts in my mind.

No self-esteem. No expensive cake. There was no smooth hardwood floor approaching quickly beneath my feet. I instinctively tightened my hips and shoulders downwards, and tightly protected my eight-month-pregnant belly with my arms. I only prayed in my heart: If something is going to be broken, break it to me, and don’t hurt my child.

I hit the ground shoulder first. The pain exploded. The floor hit me so hard that my insides were shaking. The chair screeched across the floor. Someone was screaming my name. Immediately afterwards, a sudden and clear warmth surged between my legs. It soaked into my light blue maternity skirt and spread a dazzling water stain under me.

For a full second, the whole place was silent, and everyone was stunned. Then, the microphone in Grant’s hand crashed to the ground, emitting a piercing whistle.

He rushed towards me like crazy, almost tripping over himself. His face was pale, his blue eyes full of desperate fear, and he knelt down beside me with a plop. “Don’t move. Baby, don’t move. Call an ambulance!”

Women are screaming. The man is pushing away the table. The waiter rushed over with a folded napkin. Madison was hysterically speaking into the phone, calling for an ambulance. My aunt covered her mouth and retreated behind a chair. Someone knocked over a pot of white roses, and water and petals spilled all over the floor, making it look like a crime scene.

Through the dizziness caused by the severe pain, I raised my head with difficulty.

My mother, Marlene Harper, stood near the side corridor, her hands covering her mouth, performing “shock” for the entire audience. Her eyes were moist, but there was no sadness in them. I know this expression all too well. I’ve seen it for as long as I can remember - this is the way she behaves when she gets caught doing something bad and is already figuring out who to blame.

Behind her stood my mother-in-law, Celeste Wells. Celeste didn’t scream. She didn’t run over to help. She did not ask if her granddaughter was still alive. She was wearing a beige designer suit, with one hand on the pearl necklace around her neck. Her cold gray eyes were fixed on my husband, as if what fell to the ground was not a victim but a door that had just been pushed open.

A door she had been waiting for.

Another contraction came over me, so violent that my fingers dug into Grant’s sleeve. He leaned over, tears streaming down his face, and said incoherently: “Stay with me, Natalie. Stay with me. I’m here. I’m not leaving you.”

But I didn’t look at Grant. I was looking at the two women who had laid the trap with their own hands. Because in the ten minutes before my mother pushed me, I had just heard the cruelest truth in the corridor outside the bathroom.

I personally heard Celeste hiss in a cold voice: “When she enters the hospital and Grant signs, all the remaining money will be sent to you.”

I heard my mother reply with my own ears: “I raised her, and I have plenty of ways to crush her.”

I also heard the price tag on my life, casually spoken like a cold invoice. One hundred and seventy-five thousand dollars. This was the price my biological mother paid for betraying my marriage, causing medical malpractice, and pushing my husband back under the control of his family.

With another heartbreaking contraction, the emergency personnel finally burst open the door of the concert hall. Grant climbed onto the stretcher after me until one of the medical staff forced him off. His hand was still holding mine tightly. He kept calling my name, as if calling me just one more time would bring me back from hell.

Under the flashing red and blue police lights, while my body was shaking violently in the ambulance, and my daughter was forced to come into this world early, I made a vow to myself in my heart.

They wanted a premature birth that would completely wipe out my voice, my company, my marriage, and my future. If my daughter survives, I will never waste another breath begging for mercy from those who betrayed me.

I’m going to be a storm they never dreamed of.
