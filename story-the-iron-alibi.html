The Iron Alibi

A master iron restorer faces a predatory asset-strip attempt by her estranged husband, only for an archival materials test to turn the trap against him.

Fictional story. Contains depictions of financial manipulation, marital distress, and high-tension legal conflicts.

Chapter 1: The Fractured Anvil

Valerie Sterling stood in her rain-dampened workshop along the Willamette River, staring at the empty digital ledger on her laptop screen. The forge furnace was cold for the first time in nine years, casting long, muted shadows across the anvil and heavy workbenches. That morning, her commercial business account had been drained of one hundred and eighty thousand dollars—the entire equipment reserve and client deposit fund dedicated to the historic Saint Jude Cathedral dome restoration. Her operational lines of credit had been abruptly frozen by the bank, flagged for “suspected fraudulent insolvency and unauthorized asset depletion.”

Julian Mercer, her husband of eight years, had left three days earlier under the pretext of an emergency real estate acquisition conference in Seattle. Instead of returning, his departure was heralded by an urgent formal notice from the state historic preservation licensing board: an anonymous ethics complaint had been lodged, alleging that Valerie had used counterfeit, sub-standard zinc alloy fittings on the cathedral’s load-bearing arch, causing a structural failure that threatened the landmark.
