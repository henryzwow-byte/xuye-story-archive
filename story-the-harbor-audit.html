The Harbor Audit

When a widowed marine inspector discovers hidden structural flaws on a flagship cargo vessel, he is drawn into a high-stakes corporate power struggle where his son's medical future is used as leverage.

Fictional story. Contains themes of corporate integrity, workplace ethics, and family financial pressure.

Chapter 1: The Midnight Inspection

Julian Hayes had spent twelve years working the night shift as a certified non-destructive testing surveyor at Puget Maritime Logistics in Seattle. His job was quiet, meticulous, and unforgiving. Wearing steel-toed boots and carrying a heavy ultrasonic flaw detector, he spent his nights inspecting the massive steel welds and hull plates of ocean-going cargo vessels docked along Elliott Bay. While the city slept beneath a curtain of Pacific Northwest mist, Julian walked dark gantries, measuring structural tension in steel beams that carried thousands of tons of freight across the ocean.

Life had narrowed down to precise figures for Julian. Since his wife passed away four years ago, his entire world centered on his fourteen-year-old son, Leo. Leo was a bright, resilient boy with a neuromuscular condition that progressively restricted his spine and legs, requiring specialized physical therapy and custom orthotic equipment. The medical bills were a relentless current against which Julian swam every month. Rent on their modest apartment in West Seattle was twelve days past due, and the insurance co-pays for Leo's upcoming rehabilitation cycle were piled beside the microwave, waiting for a paycheck that was already stretched to its absolute limit.
