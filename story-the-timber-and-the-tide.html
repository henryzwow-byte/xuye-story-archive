FICTIONAL STORY · A-280

The Timber and the Tide

Choosing a quiet life with a humble craftsman over a wealthy marriage pushed by her family, Hannah discovers on her wedding night that her choice carried far deeper stakes than she ever imagined.

Fictional story. Contains themes of family conflict, emotional coercion, and corporate ethics.

Chapter 1: The Choice of Salt and Cedar

Cape Rowan was a town built on salt air, Douglas firs, and cold ocean fog. For generations, social standing in the bay was measured by land holdings and timber rights, and no family watched that hierarchy more closely than the household at Rowan Crest. Hannah had grown up under the authority of her stepfather, Richard Vance, a land broker whose eyes were fixed on every parcel of waterfront property along the coast. For three years, Richard and Hannah’s mother, Evelyn, pushed Hannah toward Sterling Brooks. Sterling was the polished heir to a regional resort empire, a man who wore tailored wool coats and spoke of coastal development with smooth corporate efficiency. To her family, Sterling represented security, influence, and an elevated status that few in Cape Rowan could ever reach.

Her stepfather never missed an opportunity to remind Hannah of what was at stake. During Sunday dinners overlooking the harbor, Richard detailed the grand future awaiting her. Sterling’s company planned to build the Crestline Marina, a deep-water harbor for luxury yachts that would transform the quiet bay. Evelyn reinforced the narrative, warning that turning down a man like Sterling was an act of foolish ingratitude. Her sister, Clara, insisted that men of Sterling’s wealth did not wait for indecisive women. They talked about love as if it were a high-yield contract designed to shield Hannah from the risks of an ordinary life.
