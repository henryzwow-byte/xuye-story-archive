FICTIONAL STORY · A-433

The Harbor Deed

When her husband and mother-in-law lock her and her sick son out in a freezing storm to save face at a corporate dinner, Julianne Miller turns to her uncle and an old family deed to reclaim her life.

Fictional story. Contains themes of domestic tension, familial conflict, and child endangerment.

Chapter 1: The Storm on Highcliff

The freezing rain off the Gulf of Maine did not fall in soft sheets; it blew sideways across the granite ledge of Highcliff Head, needle-sharp and relentless. Julianne Miller pressed her six-year-old son, Toby, tight against her heavy wool coat, desperately trying to shield his face from the bitter spray. Toby’s breathing had already turned shallow and strained, the telltale dry whistle in his chest signaling an asthma flare triggered by the sudden, brutal drop in temperature. His small hands were trembling inside damp cotton mittens, his cheeks flushed with icy moisture.

Only twenty minutes earlier, Julianne had been standing inside the cedar-beamed dining room of Highcliff Lodge, watching her husband, Marcus Drake, raise a crystal glass of cabernet to his mother, Evelyn, and his newly hired lead developer, Corinne Vance. The gathering was an exclusive, black-tie dinner to celebrate the official launch of Drake Coastal Properties—a commercial venture built almost entirely on Julianne’s architectural restoration blueprints and structural surveys. But when Julianne quietly questioned Marcus in the side hallway about missing environmental permit documents, Evelyn had immediately stepped between them with practiced malice. With sharp efficiency, Evelyn declared that Julianne was "emotionally unstable" and actively sabotaging a multi-million-dollar investment opportunity. Before Julianne could return to the sideboard to retrieve her handbag, her car keys, or Toby’s vital emergency asthma nebulizer, Evelyn pushed her out onto the rear terrace and slammed the heavy iron latch shut.
