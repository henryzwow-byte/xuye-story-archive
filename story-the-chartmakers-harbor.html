The Chartmaker's Harbor

After his wife Eleanor passes away, Garrett Thorne uncovers a concealed attic chest containing decades of hand-drawn harbor charts and wire transfers—unraveling a secret child she protected from afar.

Fictional story. Contains sensitive themes related to historical adoption, family secrets, and grief.

Chapter 1: The Silence of the Tide

Four days after Eleanor Thorne’s funeral, the rain over Port Meridian turned into a steady, gray mist that hung heavy over the fir trees along the bluff. Garrett Thorne stood at the edge of his wrap-around porch, staring down at the turbulent gray waters of Puget Sound. The house smelled of damp wool, cedar oil, and the lingering fragrance of white freesia that well-meaning neighbors had left on the kitchen counter.

Eleanor had been a master bookbinder and cartography archivist for forty years, spending her days preserving fragile eighteenth-century maritime charts and hand-bound town registries. Now her indigo canvas apron hung motionless behind the kitchen door, and her reading spectacles sat beside an unfinished mug of Earl Grey tea on the window sill. Garrett, a retired harbor pilot who had spent forty-five years navigating giant container ships through treacherous Pacific channels, felt completely marooned in the silent, seventy-year-old clapboard house.
