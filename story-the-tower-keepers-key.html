The Tower Keeper's Key

When a retired horologist travels to Boston for his daughter's landmark museum dedication, high-society arrogance threatens to push him into the shadows—until a decades-old emergency secret comes to light.

Fictional story. Includes themes of social class prejudice, subtle family alienation, and non-graphic historical emergency rescue memories.

Chapter 1: The Midnight Express

The 1:15 a.m. regional train from the Berkshire Hills swayed gently over the steel tracks, its engine humming a low, steady rhythm against the dark New England countryside. Outside the rain-streaked window, yellow streetlamps flickered across sleepy mill towns before dissolving back into black pine forests. Julian Vance sat near the middle of the passenger car, his knees stiff from hours of immobility, his calloused thumb tracing the worn leather strap of an antique satchel tucked securely between his ankles.

At fifty-eight, Julian carried the quiet, unhurried posture of a man who measured his life not in calendar years, but in vibrations, teeth, and escapement tolerances. For thirty-five years in his small workshop in North Adams, he had restored antique tower clocks, grandfather movements, and marine chronometers. His palms bore the permanent signature of his craft: faint indigo stains from gear-polishing compound, tiny pale nick scars near his knuckles, and a thick, silvered burn mark running across his right forearm like a hardened ribbon of birch bark.
