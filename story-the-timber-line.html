The Timber Line

Nine years after being turned away from her childhood home, Nora returns to the rainy timber town of Mistwood Bay with her eight-year-old son and a leather field ledger that unspools the truth about the collapse that changed everything.

Fictional story. Contains depictions of family estrangement, grief over a past accidental death, and emotional reconciliation.

Chapter 1: The Gravel and the Fog

The rain in Mistwood Bay did not fall in sudden bursts; it drifted sideways off the Pacific, soaking the hemlock needles and turning the driveway gravel into a slick dark slurry. Nine years ago, when Nora was nineteen, that gravel had crunched under her boots as she carried a canvas duffel bag toward the county road. Her jacket was damp at the collar, and inside her pocket rested a handwritten lab result from a clinic two towns over, showing two blue lines beneath a faded stamp.

Inside the craftsman house on Cedar Ridge Road, the air had smelled of cedar smoke and dried lavender. Her father, Walter Miller, had sat behind his heavy oak desk in the study, surrounded by rolled topographic maps and county land ledgers. For thirty years, Walter had served as the chief land surveyor for the Skagit Watershed District. Order was not merely his profession; it was his religion. Every boundary line he drew was sharp, every elevation metric checked against federal standards.
