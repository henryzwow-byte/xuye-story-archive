Chapter 1: The Pendulum and the Fern

For forty-four years, Arthur Vance measured his life in beat-seconds and hairspring tension. In his converted timber workshop outside Cedar Ridge, Oregon, the rhythmic ticking of twenty-eight antique regulators was not noise; it was evidence of order. At seventy-two, Arthur lived alone with his brass lathes, magnifying loupes, and a cast-iron stove that burned oak logs through the damp Pacific Northwest autumns. He did not dislike solitude. Solitude was predictable, precise, and entirely under his own control.

On a rainy Tuesday morning, his calm was broken by his daughter, Meredith. She arrived unannounced, her rain jacket smelling of damp wool and roasted espresso. Behind her stood Beatrice Sterling, a woman of sixty-nine with crisp silver hair, a dark rain-cape, and a leather press box used for preserving botanical specimens. Beatrice lived two miles down the valley in a slate-roofed cottage. Arthur knew her only by sight at the farmers' market.
