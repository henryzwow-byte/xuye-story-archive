The Silent Timber

When eleven-year-old Nora develops mysterious tremors on an isolated Oregon timber estate, her mother Clara must confront family coercion and hidden environmental sabotage to save her daughter.

Fictional story. Contains themes of domestic coercion, child illness, and environmental tampering.

Chapter 1: The Quiet in the Timber

For three long years following my husband Arthur’s fatal logging accident in the high woods, our timber-frame home on Mistwood Ridge had been sustained by a single comforting sound: the resonant, oak-deep voice of my eleven-year-old daughter Nora’s cello. Every afternoon after completing her correspondence school lessons, she would pull the dark instrument from its velvet-lined case beside the high parlor window. The old spruce floorboards would hum beneath her feet as scale after scale echoed out into the damp Oregon pines. She played with an unstudied, radiant joy that made our isolated mountain homestead feel bright and invulnerable.

Then, near the middle of October, the music simply dissolved. It did not end with a sudden crisis, but faded away in small, creeping increments that I initially struggled to comprehend. First came a heavy, unnatural lethargy. Nora would sit at the kitchen table staring vacantly through the window while grey autumn mist clung to the timber line, leaving her morning porridge completely untouched. By the second week, her handwriting—usually crisp and precise—slanted into shaky, disjointed scrawls across her exercise pages. When she attempted to lift a light porcelain teacup at breakfast, her hands trembled so violently that warm tea sloshed over the pine counter and onto her apron.
