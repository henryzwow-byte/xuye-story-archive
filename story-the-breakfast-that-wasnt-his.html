The Breakfast That Wasn't His

After her adult son slaps her for refusing to hand over forty thousand dollars, Margaret prepares breakfast and invites her brother Michael to the table. What Ethan mistakes for another surrender becomes the beginning of frozen loans, exposed forgeries and a mother's fight to reclaim her home, her money and the right to decide who may enter her life.

Fiction. Contains domestic violence against a parent, financial exploitation, identity theft, forged documents, coercive control, family betrayal, legal proceedings and emotional distress.

Chapter One: Access Revoked

I set breakfast on the table, covered with my best tablecloth, and the eggs, bacon, and pancakes on the plate were steaming like a festive morning. Yet just a few hours earlier, my son had slapped me across the face.

As Ethan walked down the stairs smiling, he was convinced that he had finally got me. Little did he know that another man was already sitting at my table. Little did he know that everything he thought he controlled would soon be wiped out.

We live in a small town outside of Austin, Texas. For years, I comforted myself with the idea that my son was just going through a rebellious phase. Every mother would like to think so.

Every mother is racking her brain to find excuses for behaviors that hurt her. Ethan gave me so many excuses. His father abandoned us when he was very young. Didn’t finish college. Work never lasts long.

Love always ends in breakup. I don’t know when the disappointment turned into resentment. Resentment condensed into violence. The sweet boy who used to chase fireflies in the backyard disappeared. In his place is a man who blames everyone for all his failures.

Still, I defended him. When he was rude, I made excuses for him. When he asked for money, I responded. When he slammed the door and roared, I told myself that he just had a hard life.

I believe things will get better. But it didn’t. That evening, I came home exhausted from a long day at the medical billing office. My feet ached. The pain in my back is unbearable. The only thing I longed for was a moment of peace.

Ethan is waiting in the kitchen.

“I need money.”

No greetings. Didn’t ask me how my day was. Asking again.

“How much?”

“Forty thousand.”

I stared at him.

“What are you going to do?”

“A business opportunity.”

“What business?”

“You don’t understand even if I tell you.”

This sentence has become his mantra since he turned thirty. You don’t know how to invest. You don’t understand modern business. You don’t understand what men our age face. But I know very well that just six months ago, he just borrowed US$180,000, and there has been no news yet.

I know very well that his last company only lasted eleven weeks.

I also knew very well that every “emergency” he had ended up with me signing a payment slip.

“No.”

His expression changed.

“What did you just say?”

“I said no.”

A cold sneer crept onto the corner of his mouth.

“Who do you think you are?”

Those words lit a fuse inside me. Perhaps years of forbearance have finally come to an end. Maybe it’s due to overwork. Maybe just to survive.

“I’m the one who pays the bills,” I started. “I’m the one who keeps this family going. And now, I’ve had enough.”

A deathly silence fell in the kitchen. Then, his hand rose. The slap fell hard before I could react. My head jerked to the side. There was no blood. No dramatic slumping. There is only silence.

Suffocatingly heavy. I looked at him. He looked at me too. Then he turned and walked away. As if slapping his mother was no big deal at all. As if it were natural. As if I deserved this slap.

I sat alone in the kitchen for hours. No crying. No shouting. Just thinking. At 1:20 in the morning, I called my brother Michael. He answered on the second ring.

“What happened?”

I could barely make any sound out of my throat.

“Ethan hit me.”

Dead silence. Then there was the screeching sound of chairs being dragged across the floor.

“I’ll be there right away.”

Twenty-nine minutes later, Michael arrived. He was sixty-seven, four years older than me, with broad shoulders, steady eyes, and the calmness of a man who had spent a lifetime working in financial fraud investigations in the state comptroller’s office.

He looked at my cheek. Then look towards the stairs.

“Where is he?”

“Sleep.”

“Did you call the police?”

“Not yet.”

“Why?”

“Because I have to tell you something first.”

I took him to the small desk next to the tea room. I’ve been getting some weird notifications for a few months now. A letter thanking me for applying for business financing. A confirmation of tax registration from a company I had never heard of.

A Home Insurance Questionnaire asking me about running a short term business on my land. Each time, Ethan was able to give a reasonable explanation. Spam. Clerical error. Marketing letters. Just three days before he slapped me, a bank clerk left a voicemail asking me to confirm an asset valuation appointment.

The mortgage address is my home. Michael listened to the voice message twice.

“Have you applied for a loan?”

“No.”

“Did you give Ethan a power of attorney?”

“Never.”

“Do you still have that envelope?”

I gave him everything I had. At three o’clock in the morning, he found the registration information of a company online. Hale Ridge Ventures LLC. Owner: Margaret Elaine Hale. That’s my name. Registered Manager: Ethan Hale.

Company address: my home. The registration document was signed with an electronic signature that looked exactly like mine. That’s not what I signed at all. Michael immediately called my attorney, Rebecca Sloan. She instructed me to take pictures of my face, keep my clothes, save every message, and never confront Ethan alone.

At 4:15 a.m., a sheriff’s deputy quietly walked in through the back door. He documented the attack and took photos of the redness and swelling on my cheeks. The kitchen voice assistant captured part of the altercation as Ethan was standing near the equipment and yelling.

The cloud recording completely preserved the process of him asking me for money. My rejection. He questioned. Who do you think you are? Then there was a round of applause. The adjutant listened to the recording.

“This will be documented.”

Before dawn, I made a decision. The best tablecloths were taken out of the cupboard. I fried eggs, baked bacon, and made biscuits and pancakes. The aroma of coffee filled the whole house with warmth.

The restaurant looked like it was celebrating a holiday. Not really. This is a full stop. Michael sat across from me.

“Today it all ends,” he said.

“Okay.”

Then, there were footsteps on the stairs. Slowly. Confident. Ethan walked into the kitchen in sweatpants and the same smile he had on his face after slapping me.

“So,” he said, pulling up a chair, “you finally figured it out—”

His words died when he saw Michael. Doubt crossed his face, followed by wariness and then fear. Ethan finally understood that I was no longer alone. I stood and removed a folded document from my bag.

All the color drained from his face.

“What’s that?”

I lay it flat between us.

“Revoke any authority you think you have.”

He unfolded the document. Page One officially revoked his authority to conduct financial activities on my behalf. Page Two terminated his permission to conduct commercial activities from my property. The third page requires that all company, bank, and real estate records bearing my name be sealed.

Ethan looked up.

“You can’t do this.”

“I’ve already done it.”

“What does this mean?”

“It means that the bank has frozen the loan.”

His hands froze in mid-air. Michael stared at him sharply.

“What loan?” Ethan asked.

I looked directly into the same eyes I had watched since he was a child.

“The three hundred and eighty thousand dollar loan.”
