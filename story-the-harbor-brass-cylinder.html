The Timber Line

When her daughter unexpectedly chooses her wealthy father in a county guardianship hearing, a coastal wood restorer discovers a hidden message that reveals a dangerous plot of municipal extortion.

Fictional story. Contains mild themes of family conflict, emotional distress, and legal dispute.

Chapter 1: The Hearing at Oakhaven

The high arched windows of Hearing Room B in the Oakhaven County Courthouse looked out across a misty Pacific harbor, but inside, the air was dense with the smell of floor wax and damp wool coats. Nora Vance sat at the edge of the polished oak counsel table, her palms resting on a scuffed leather portfolio. Inside were timber restoration certificates, maritime property deeds, five years of tax returns, and receipts for her daughter’s school books and violin lessons. Nora had spent three days organizing every document in chronological order, foolishly believing that methodical honesty could protect a mother who had no legal firm on retainer.

Across the carpeted aisle sat Arthur Vance. He wore a dark charcoal suit made of imported wool, his gold cuff links catching the cold overhead fluorescent light. Arthur was a prominent commercial resort developer along the coast, a man who viewed human relationships through the lens of municipal leverage and square footage. Seated beside him was eleven-year-old Maya Vance. Her dark hair had been slicked back into a rigid braid that looked far too formal for a girl who usually spent her afternoons combing tide pools for sea glass. Maya kept her chin ducked toward her chest, her eyes fixed on the metal buckle of her shoe, refusing to make eye contact with Nora.
