The Tide-Land Covenant

Locked out of her coastal boat restoration workshop during a autumn squall, master craftswoman Nora Hayes seeks refuge at her father's harbor watch station. Together, they use state trust records and land covenants to dismantle her husband's scheme.

Fictional story. Contains themes of emotional betrayal, financial deception, and cold exposure during a maritime storm.

Chapter 1: The Cold Gale at Blackwood Pier

Rain whipped across the timber planking of Blackwood Pier, driving cold Pacific runoff under the collar of Nora Hayes's heavy oilskin jacket. The autumn storm had rolled down the Oregon coast three hours earlier than forecast, churning the estuary into gray, foam-capped swells that slapped violently against the pilings below. Nora knocked on the solid oak door of the historic shipwright workshop, her knuckles red and aching from the bitter dampness. Through the rain-streaked window pane, warm interior lights glowed against polished cedar beams, illuminating a catered buffet table laden with roasted meats and crystal wine decanters.

When the heavy iron latch finally turned, it was not Nora’s apprentice who opened the door, but her husband, Victor Sterling. He stood in the entryway wearing a tailored cashmere sweater, a glass of dark Pinot Noir balanced effortlessly between his fingers. Behind him, his sister Evelyn adjusted a bouquet of imported lilies on the main drafting table where Nora’s boat blueprints usually lay spread out. Further back near the roaring wood stove sat Vivienne Vance, the managing director of a Portland private equity firm whose investment Victor had been chasing for six months.
