The Blackwood Bequest

When archival surveyor Julian Vance inherits a desolate granite light station on the Oregon coast, an encrypted journal left by his long-lost aunt reveals an industrial cover-up buried deep beneath the waves.

Fictional story. Contains mild themes of corporate malfeasance, legal tension, and family mystery suitable for adult readers.

Chapter 1: The Blackwood Deed

Julian Vance believed that history was safest when bound in leather and filed away under temperature-controlled lights. At forty-one, he worked as a senior archival surveyor for the Oregon Historical Land Registry in Portland. His days were measured in yellowed parchment, property lines drawn in iron gall ink, and the comforting predictability of public deeds. He lived alone in a restored craftsman bungalow on Belmont Street, drove a fifteen-year-old Volvo, and took quiet pride in identifying boundary errors that county clerks had overlooked during the land rushes of the late nineteenth century.

On a drizzle-soaked Thursday afternoon, that predictable life was interrupted by a courier in a grease-stained uniform. The package required a physical signature, a thumbprint scan, and a verified state photo ID. Inside lay a heavily sealed courier envelope containing an original 1894 title deed for Blackwood Sentinel—a remote granite light station resting on an isolated headland near Cape Perpetua—along with a rusted brass skeleton key and a water-damaged surveyor’s logbook wrapped carefully in oiled silk.
