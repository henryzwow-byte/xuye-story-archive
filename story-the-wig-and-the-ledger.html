The Wig and the Ledger

At her wedding, a forensic accountant recovering from lymphoma is publicly humiliated when her future mother-in-law tears away her wig. But the spectacle was never only about illness: it was meant to destroy the bride's credibility before she could expose a charity fraud hidden in the Mercer family's accounts. With her partner Ethan beside her, she follows the ledger from a ruined ceremony to a courtroom—and learns that survival is more than keeping a secret.

Fiction. Contains cancer treatment, hair loss, public humiliation, harassment, family conflict, financial fraud, bereavement, police questioning and courtroom proceedings.

Chapter One: The Tug at the Wedding

The entire cathedral seemed to hold its breath as Vivian Mercer ripped the wig from my head.

Three hundred guests all stared at my bare scalp.

My mother-in-law stood at the front bench, holding the blonde wig aloft like a trophy she had won.

“Look at her!” she screamed. “She fooled everyone here!”

My knees went weak.

Before I hit the ground, Ethan grabbed me from my waist.

His mother smiled.

It’s not loud.

But that’s what makes it even more disgusting.

It was a dark, satisfied laugh, belonging to a woman who watched her carefully planned plot come to fruition.

I battled lymphoma for six months.

Six months of biopsies, scans and chemotherapy, canker sores, countless sleepless nights, and those early mornings when I tried to lift my heavy head off the pillow that felt like a desperate debate with gravity.

I didn’t hide my illness from Ethan.

Whenever his schedule allowed, he came with me to every chemotherapy session and appointment.

He even memorized the names of the drugs that made me nauseous just hearing them.

Whenever I was vomiting in the bathroom, he would sit with me on the cold floor, distracting me with trivial everyday things until the nausea passed.

My brother Nathan knew.

My attending physician knew.

My boss knew about it and even adjusted my work schedule for it.

Only the wedding guests were kept in the dark.

I only wanted one day when people would look at me and see a beautiful bride before they saw a patient.

In Vivian’s eyes, this is called deception.

My hands instinctively moved to protect my head.

There was nothing there, no shelter.

Camera flashes erupted from the back of the cathedral.

Someone whispered my name.

My mother had burst into tears.

Ethan whipped off his dress jacket and draped it over my shoulders with brute strength.

Then, he pulled me into his arms.

“I hid my illness,” I whispered against his chest, “but I never hid my love for you.”

His eyes were moist.

“I know.”

Vivian took a step closer, still holding the wig tightly in her hand.

“She traps you with compassion.”

Ethan turned away.

I had never seen him look so furious. For a moment, even the man protecting me seemed like a stranger.

“I love her,” he said. “Get out of here.”

“You cannot begin to know what she did.”

“I just know what you just did.”

“She lied to you for months.”

“She has the right to choose to maintain privacy during her treatment.”

“She’s sick, Ethan.”

“I know.”

Vivian suddenly stopped.

Those words struck her harder than any shout could have.

“You know?”

“I know.”

“Then you still want to marry her?”

These words echoed in the empty cathedral for a long time.

Ethan put his body between us.

“Call security.”

Two ushers came quickly from the side aisle.

Vivian poked the wig almost up Ethan’s nose.

“Your family will regret choosing her!”

No one moved to obey her.

When the cathedral’s heavy doors closed, a deathly silence settled over the room again.

The priest looked towards us.

“We can take a break.”

I glanced at the wig that had fallen next to the crushed white rose.

My first impulse was to run away.

Find a restroom.

Hide yourself.

Wait until all the guests have dispersed.

Then I looked down at Ethan’s suit jacket draped over my shoulders.

I saw Nathan standing by the front bench, tears flowing freely on his cheeks.

I looked at the hundreds of people who had just watched Vivian turn my illness into a weapon.

I pulled Ethan’s jacket away from my head but kept it draped over my shoulders.

“No,” I said.

The priest leaned forward.

“No need?”

“Please go ahead.”

Ethan studied my face carefully.

“You don’t have to prove anything to anyone.”

“I didn’t.”

I held his hand.

“I’m here to marry you.”

The ceremony continued.

My voice was still shaking slightly when I read the first paragraph of the oath.

But by the last sentence, he had become extremely firm.

When Ethan kissed me, the whole room stood up.

The applause was not immediate.

Some people haven’t recovered from the shock just now.

Then, someone started clapping.

A second man joined in.

The applause gathered into a torrent and echoed under the dome of the church.

Across the street, Vivian sat in her car, frantically making call after call.

She thought she had exposed my secret.

But in fact, she gave everyone present a reason to remember this moment forever - from that moment on, she was afraid of me.
