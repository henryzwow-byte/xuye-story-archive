FICTIONAL STORY · A-172

The Timber Framer's Ledger

A master coastal artisan turned away from his own conservatory gala uses historical land titles and quiet resolve to expose corporate deceit and protect his grandson's future.

Fictional story. Contains themes of familial exclusion, corporate governance disputes, and legal asset protection.

Chapter 1: The Lantern on the Pier

The salt fog had settled heavy across Penobscot Bay when Arthur Vance finished polishing the brass hinge of the handmade compass box. At seventy-three, Arthur’s hands bore the deep scars of fifty years in timber framing—calluses built from shaping white oak, cedar joists, and mortise-and-tenon joints that held coastal structures against Atlantic gales. He blew away a whisper of fine mahogany sawdust, admiring the smooth curve of the lid. Inside lay a hand-calibrated tide dial and a key carved from polished walnut, a personal token for his twenty-four-year-old grandson, Leo. Tonight was the inauguration of the Oakhaven Marine Conservatory, a state-of-the-art ecological research hall built upon the historic boatyard Arthur had restored from dilapidated ruins over three decades.

Leo had spent two years designing the conservatory’s tidal filtration lab for his graduate thesis, and Arthur had personally financed every steel beam, timber truss, and glass pane. To mark the grand opening, Arthur wore his dark wool overcoat and dark gray tailored suit, pinning a modest silver anchor badge to his lapel—a gift from his late wife, Martha.
