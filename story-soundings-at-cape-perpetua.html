Soundings at Cape Perpetua

Exhausted after a thirty-six-hour shift, boat restorer Nora Vance steps into the wrong vehicle during a Pacific Northwest storm, sparking an unexpected partnership to save a historic maritime slipway.

Fictional story. Depicts work exhaustion, fatigue, and workplace stress. Free of graphic violence, illegal conduct, or medical misconduct.

Chapter 1: Midnight on the Slipway

Nora Vance wiped salt spray and machine grease from her forehead with the damp cuff of her canvas jacket. The motorized winch at Cape Perpetua’s historical salvage dock had jammed twice during the midnight high tide, forcing her to crank the heavy steel drum by hand until her arms felt like worn hemp rope. As the head conservator for the local wooden vessel trust, she spent her weeks pulling thirty-foot cedar hulls out of freezing Pacific saltwater before seasonal gale winds could smash their frames against the shoreline riprap. Tonight, thirty-six straight hours of emergency storm preparations had hollowed out her judgment, leaving only a dull, rhythmic ache between her shoulder blades and eyes that burned under the pier’s amber sodium lights.

The gale was coming down in thick, blinding sheets across the gravel parking lot. Through the rain-streaked darkness, Nora spotted the soft interior glow of an idling vehicle parked beside the harbormaster’s winch shed. It was a dark, retrofitted sprinter van with solar arrays mounted on the roof rack—the exact sort of heavy-duty utility van the harbor commission periodically rented for night inspectors. Assuming her assistant had driven over to take over the graveyard watch, Nora grabbed the side door handle, slid the heavy door open, stepped into the dry warmth, and pulled it shut against the howling ocean wind.
