The Unbroken Span

When a municipal ribbon-cutting erases the memory of five fallen riggers to appease corporate sponsors, an aging welder and his granddaughter unveil buried archives to reclaim the truth.

Fictional story. Contains themes of institutional injustice, workplace safety history, and historical erasure.

Chapter 1: The Erased Plaque

The sun rose cold over the rusted iron arches of the old Blackwood Viaduct, casting long geometric shadows across the granite walls of Pine Gorge. High in the Colorado mountains, the air in the town of Oakhaven carried the sharp bite of late autumn pine and distant river spray. Nora Vance, a thirty-one-year-old museum curator for the county historical society, adjusted her heavy wool coat against the mountain wind as she stepped onto the newly constructed timber overlook. Today marked the ribbon-cutting for a ten-million-dollar municipal project designed to restore the historic canyon overlook and showcase the valley's industrial heritage to regional tourists.

Carrying a heavy canvas bag packed with digitized archival materials and framed blue-print reproductions requested by the municipal planning committee, Nora walked toward the reserved seating area near the main podium. Rows of crisp white folding chairs faced a wooden stage draped in official state bunting and pine garlands. She scanned the printed name tags taped to the backs of the front-row seats. Mayor Harlan, the regional transit authorities, and Commissioner Richard Sterling had prime locations beneath the heated canvas canopy. However, when Nora reached the outer edge where her seventy-six-year-old grandfather, Warren Vance, was scheduled to sit, she found an empty gap between two silver metal stanchions.
