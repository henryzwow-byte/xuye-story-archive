FICTIONAL STORY · A-454

The Salt and Timber Ledger

Recovering from a severe workshop injury at her coastal Maine boatyard, antique conservator Nora Vance realizes her business partner isn't trying to protect her recovery—he is isolating her to seize her family's historic timberland.

Fictional story. Contains themes of emotional manipulation, unlawful confinement, and medical neglect.

Chapter 1: The Cold Return

The salt air of Heron Bay was sharp enough to bite through the collar of Nora Vance’s heavy woolen coat when the truck pulled up to the boatyard. Five days in the county hospital had left her skin smelling of antiseptic soap and synthetic bandages, a clean industrial scent that felt utterly alien against the backdrop of cured cedar, pine resin, and damp sea fog. Her left forearm was encased in a thick fiberglass cast, held rigid from hand to elbow after a scaffold brace had snapped in the main restoration bay. Below her ribs, a tight elastic binder constrained every breath, reminding her with a dull throb that recovery would be measured in months, not days.

Julian Cross cut the engine and stepped out into the gravel driveway. He moved with the smooth, unhurried efficiency of a man who managed commercial marine contracts, his heavy boots crushing the frosted stones. He opened Nora’s door and reached in to assist her, his grip firm beneath her right elbow. For a moment, his gesture looked like the attentiveness of a dedicated business partner. But as Nora swung her feet out onto the cold ground, she saw Vance Sterling waiting near the double doors of the timber workshop. Vance was their senior financial consultant, wearing a tailored topcoat that seemed absurdly out of place among the stacked oak knees and seasoning timber.
