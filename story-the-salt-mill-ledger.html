The Salt Mill Ledger

Returning home after ten months at sea, Julian Vance finds his uncle locked in a loft and framed for dementia by an ambitious brother-in-law seeking to sell the family timber mill. Armed with structural telemetry and forensic engineering, Julian sets out to prove the truth.

Fictional story. Contains themes of familial deception, corporate greed, and psychological manipulation.

Chapter 1: The Sound of the Salt Mill

When Julian Vance disembarked from the coastal ferry at Oakhaven Cove, he carried ten months of high-seas acoustic engineering work in his calloused hands and the heavy salt spray of offshore oil rigs behind his eyes. He had spent forty-eight hours navigating choppy shuttle boats, regional transit hubs, and stale diner coffee, fueled entirely by the mental image of his home on the estuary: the creaking cedar pilings of Vance Timber & Marine, the sharp tang of freshly cut Douglas fir, and his uncle Arthur working at the restoration workbench with his brass calipers tucked behind his ear.

Instead of quiet sawdust and the familiar rhythmic whine of the water-driven timber saw, Julian stepped onto the gravel driveway to find three sleek black sedans parked outside the office. Standing on the pier beside a group of slick-suited investors was Marcus Sterling, Julian’s brother-in-law. Marcus wore a tailored waterproof coat and spoke in the smooth, practiced cadence of a corporate liquidator addressing a grieving family.
