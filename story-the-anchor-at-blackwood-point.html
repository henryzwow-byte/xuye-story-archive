FICTIONAL STORY · A-178

The Anchor at Blackwood Point

When a high-society developer mocks his past and threatens a historic Maine wharf, a timber specialist uses physical core samples to expose his deceit.

Fictional story. Contains themes of emotional conflict, marital abandonment, and corporate fraud investigation.

Chapter 1: The Glass Gallery

The wind coming off Penobscot Bay carried the sharp tang of salt brine and damp cedar, but inside the glass pavilion of the Blackwood Harbor Club, the air smelled only of white lilies, expensive cologne, and imported pinot noir. I stood in the shadow of the heavy brass-trimmed entry doors, holding seven-year-old Leo’s hand. His small fingers were tightly wrapped around mine, his thumb absently rubbing the worn cuff of my canvas jacket. Inside the high-ceilinged hall, two hundred sea-resort investors, city council members, and coastal developers clinked crystal glasses under soft recessed spotlights.

At the raised podium stood Julian Vance. He wore a tailored charcoal suit that fit his tall frame perfectly, his dark hair immaculately groomed, holding a polished microphone with the effortless grace of a man who believed the tide itself waited on his schedule. Beside him stood a digital projection screen displaying renderings of "Vance Luxury Marinas"—a proposed multi-million-dollar private yacht basin designed to replace Blackwood Point’s historic nineteenth-century timber wharf.
