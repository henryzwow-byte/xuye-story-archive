Ash and Iron Verdict

When manifest clerk Nora Hayes uncovers a toxic waste smuggling ring disguised as lumber shipments at her family's coastal dock, her corrupt corporate partner frames her for arson. Pursued through the sea fog, she flees on a midnight freight train, teaming up with a veteran signalman to expose the forgery before the maritime board.

Fictional story. Depicts corporate betrayal, workplace intimidation, and legal proceedings.

Chapter 1: The Fog over Port Sterling

November brought a wet, clinging salt frost to the timber docks of Port Sterling, Oregon. Twenty-six-year-old Nora Hayes sat at her narrow wooden desk inside the upper gallery of Hayes & Sterling Freight Logistics, the oil lamp flickering against rows of black-bound ledgers. For three generations, the Hayes family had overseen the timber shipments leaving the rain-soaked bay, but since her father's death two autumns ago, the quiet authority of the office had soured into something sharp and evasive. Her father's surviving partner, Gareth Sterling, had systematically moved Nora away from client contracts and relegated her to late-night manifest auditing. Outside, the tide pounded against the Douglas fir pilings, and the screech of iron wheels echoing from the railway yard sounded like a warning she had ignored for months.

Tonight, the building was supposed to be empty. The night-shift dockworkers had been dismissed early under Sterling's personal orders, leaving only the dull thrum of the diesel generator running in the yard below. Nora had stayed behind to locate missing bills of lading for a timber consignment bound for San Francisco. While searching the lower drawers of the heavy mahogany roll-top desk in Sterling’s private office, her fingers caught on a hidden wooden latch behind the paneling. Sliding it back revealed a compartment stuffed with unfiled blue carbon invoices and sealed brass ledger cylinders that bore neither company stamps nor customs approval marks.
