The Salt Harbor Ledger

In a coastal Maine town, historic timber conservator Clara Thorne faces corporate framing after a disastrous arson fire. Relying on digital access logs, chemical core analysis, and financial records, she untangles a greedy developer's conspiracy to seize her family's heritage waterfront.

Fictional story. Depicts corporate betrayal, financial fraud, and arson framing resolved through lawful forensic accounting and non-violent investigative action.

Chapter 1: Whispers in the Timber

The air inside the salt-crusted sanctuary of Oakhaven Cove smelled of scorched cedar, damp sea fog, and cold ashes. Clara Thorne stood beneath the skeletal rafters of the historic lighthouse storehouse, her leather work boots caked in black soot. For three generations, the Thorne family had preserved the nineteenth-century timber archive—a sanctuary of hand-carved oak beams, maritime ledgers, and rare shipbuilder blueprints that defined the identity of the small Maine coastal town. Now, four days after the midnight blaze, the historic structure lay hollowed out, its iron-ribbed roof open to the gloomy grey Atlantic sky.

At the evening fundraising memorial hosted in the historic town hall, Julian Vance stood before the podium beneath glittering brass chandeliers. As the chief executive officer of Vance Maritime Holdings, Julian possessed a smooth, velvet charm that had easily swayed the town council for years. Draped in a tailored charcoal suit, he spoke with practiced sorrow about the tragedy of the lost heritage. Yet as his gaze settled on Clara sitting silently in the back row, his tone subtly shifted. He suggested to the gathered reporters and townspeople that the destruction was not an unpredictable act of nature, but the tragic outcome of improper oversight and reckless storage of volatile timber finishes.
