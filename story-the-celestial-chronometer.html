The Celestial Chronometer

Returning early from a grueling deep-water audit along the Olympic Peninsula, marine engineer Sam Hayes visits her husband's heritage trust gala, only to find another woman introduced as his wife—wearing a twin brass pocket chronometer Sam believed was one-of-a-kind.

Fictional story. Contains themes of marital infidelity, emotional betrayal, deceit, and legal disputes.

Chapter 1: The Coastal Highway

The salt spray from the Pacific still coated the windshield of my heavy-duty pickup truck as I drove south along U.S. Route 101. For twenty-two long days, I had been submerged in the damp, freezing gloom beneath the deep-water piers of the Olympic Peninsula, conducting rigorous structural integrity audits on timber pilings, steel trusses, and reinforced concrete caissons. As a senior marine structural engineer, my life was measured in acoustic sonar scans, concrete core samples, and grueling hard-hat shifts that began hours before dawn. When the state transport authority cleared our final underwater inspection three days ahead of schedule, I did not call home. I wanted to surprise Julian.

After twenty-eight years of marriage, spontaneous gestures were rare between us. Julian ran Hayes Maritime Engineering & Heritage Trust out of a meticulously restored nineteenth-century granite custom house overlooking the Columbia River estuary in Astoria. While my work took me to remote deep-water harbors, offshore wind farms, and storm-damaged bridges across the Pacific Northwest, Julian was the smooth, public face of the firm—managing federal grants, hosting historic preservation galas, and directing municipal land trust negotiations. In my passenger seat sat a heavy brass transit case containing our engineering team’s preliminary safety certification, along with a bottle of small-batch coastal rye I had purchased at a small distillery in Port Townsend.
