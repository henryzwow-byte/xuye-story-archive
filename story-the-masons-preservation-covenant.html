The Mason's Preservation Covenant

When a retired master stonemason is humiliated by his daughter's ambitious fiancé at a historic pavilion gala, he answers not with anger, but with an ironclad preservation covenant that exposes a web of deceit.

Fictional story. Contains depictions of family conflict, emotional distress, and public humiliation.

Chapter 1: The Pavilion Gala

The evening mist had barely settled over the Columbia River Gorge when Arthur Vance arrived at the Pinecrest Pavilion. At sixty-four, Arthur moved with the deliberate, unhurried posture of a man who had spent forty years laying granite blocks and mortising massive douglas fir timbers. His navy wool blazer was clean and freshly pressed, though the fabric along the cuffs was worn thin enough to reveal the subtle weave of past decades. He had come early because punctuality was the quietest form of respect he knew.

Four decades ago, Arthur and his late wife, Evelyn, had spent an entire rain-soaked summer crafting the soaring timber trusses that anchored the pavilion to the basalt cliff face. Every mortise-and-tenon joint had been cut by hand; every stone foundation block had been squared with chisel and sledge. Tonight, the pavilion was host to a high-society rededication gala organized by Sterling Heritage Holdings, a prominent regional real estate firm headed by thirty-five-year-old Julian Sterling.
