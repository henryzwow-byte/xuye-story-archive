FICTIONAL STORY · A-040

Four Seconds of Breath

Retired rescue veteran Thomas Avery receives a four-second call from his daughter Emily and hears enough to know she is in danger. He reaches her penthouse to find her feverish, drugged and trapped while her husband Preston and mother-in-law Lorraine stage a psychiatric breakdown meant to strip her of control over a valuable medical patent trust. A deputy's careful investigation, a hidden archive left by Emily's mother and the testimony of a doctor who never examined her expose a calculated scheme of coercion, fraud and attempted erasure. Father and daughter must then carry the truth into court without letting rage distort the evidence that can finally set her free.

Fiction. Contains domestic abuse, coercive control, drugging, unlawful confinement, physical assault, a medical emergency, fabricated evidence, financial fraud and courtroom proceedings.

Chapter One: The Scissors

At 10:17 on that cold Saturday morning, the world that surrounded Thomas Avery consisted only of wet soil, bare branches, and the stubborn roses that his late wife had planted along the backyard fence.

In this quiet Pennsylvania town, neighbors only knew him as a retired school janitor who lived alone, drove an old pickup truck, and spent a lot of time repairing garden tools that could be replaced.

They noticed his silver hair, his stiff right leg, and the faded flannel shirt he wore no matter the season. But they didn’t know that Thomas had served as an Army medical evacuation specialist for thirty-three years and later trained specifically for emergency rescue teams in hostile environment rescues.

His disabled leg was injured when a plane landed during an overseas evacuation operation. People mistakenly think that it is the calmness of emotional apathy, but it is actually the tempered experience of decades of making decisions in smoke, darkness and panic.

After retiring, his emergencies were downgraded to repairing water pipes and frozen pipes. Until his cell phone rang. Thomas took off one glove.

“Hello?”

His daughter’s weak voice reached his ears, as thin as a gnat.

“Dad, please come pick me up.”

What followed was a harsh, sharp sound. The call is interrupted. Just four seconds. Thomas called back again. Voicemail. He called again, then opened the live location-sharing software the two had activated together after Emily’s car broke down a few months ago.

Her cell phone location showed it was still parked at the Whitmore family estate, twenty-seven miles away. Thomas doesn’t look for excuses to make himself feel better. He only believes in the objective facts he observes.

Emily’s voice sounded weak. She spoke softly, as if she were someone standing next to her. The call ended abruptly. And she never answered. Before walking into the house, he contacted emergency dispatch.

“My adult daughter just called from a residence in Westbridge County requesting immediate assistance,” he said. “The call was disconnected and she is no longer answering. She recently reported feeling unwell and has escalated a conflict with her husband’s family.”

The dispatcher asked for an address and advised him not to break in if he saw anyone armed.

“Deputies are already on the scene, but the nearest patrol car is about twenty minutes away.”

Thomas looked at his truck.

“I understand.”

He put on a pair of boots, took Emily’s medical records, stuffed a first aid kit in the cab, and locked all the guns securely in the locked gun cabinet. Years of rescue experience have taught him that breaking into a highly tense home with weapons often does not end the crisis, but creates another.

He sent the truck model, route and estimated time of arrival to dispatch. Next, he called Emily’s doctor. Dr. Hannah Lee confirmed that Emily had been diagnosed with the flu two days ago and had been given clear instructions to seek emergency medical help immediately if her fever worsened or she became confused.

Thomas quickly drove to the scene while remaining calm. Along the way, he recalled every red flag he had chosen to ignore as ordinary marital friction. Emily’s husband, Preston Whitmore, is always picky about her clothes, monitors the family’s expenses, and even complains whenever she visits her father without permission.

Preston’s mother, Lorraine, controlled the family calendar and reduced Emily to nothing. And Emily always insisted that they were just difficult, not dangerous. Thomas once chose to respect her marriage. But now, he began to wonder whether his restraint back then was just a disguised form of indifference.

At Whitmore Hills, the main entrance to the private community was closed. The security guard said Preston had given orders not to let any visitors in.

“My daughter is requesting emergency assistance,” Thomas said. “The county deputy sheriff is on his way. Please put my visitor request on record.”

The security guard hesitated. Thomas gave the dispatch center number. The door slowly opened. He arrived at Whitmore’s mansion nine minutes before the first patrol car arrived. Preston is standing on the stone steps holding an aluminum alloy baseball bat.

His shirt was pressed to perfection. The hair is carefully styled. There was a tense alertness on his face that was ready for a fight.

“You need to leave,” Preston yelled, “Emily is sick and emotionally unstable. My mom and I are dealing with it.”

Thomas stopped a few yards away.

“My daughter asked to leave.”

“She had no idea what she was thinking. The fever was making her fussy.”

“Stay away from the door.”

Preston tightened his grip on the bat.

“This is a private matter.”

“Emergency crews are on the way.”

“Cancel them.”

“No.”

The bat was raised high. Thomas raised his hands above his head to make sure the external security camera could see clearly.

“Put the baseball bat down, Preston.”

“Do you think the stories you heard from the army can scare me?”

“My background is why I’m giving you one last chance to make a safe choice.”

Preston charged in with his bat. Thomas nimbly dodged the arc, grabbed Preston’s wrist, and used the opponent’s momentum to bring him down to the wet grass. The bat clattered a few feet away. Thomas put one knee on Preston’s shoulder and didn’t even throw an extra punch.

“Stay still until the deputy arrives.”

Emily’s screams came from inside the house. Thomas let go of Preston and rushed through the open door. The foyer was empty. A chair by the stairs was overturned. Emily’s broken cell phone lay alone in the corner.

He heard movement upstairs. Then came Lorraine’s voice:

“Be honest, otherwise you will suffer.”

Thomas rushed to the second floor. The bedroom door is wide open. Emily was wearing a thin nightgown and sitting weakly on the floor next to the dresser. Her face was pale with fever. There is a bruise on the wrist.

A few strands of dark hair lay scattered on the carpet. Lorraine stood behind her, holding a pair of tailoring scissors in one hand and her phone in the other.

“Stay away from her,” Thomas said.

Lorraine turned around. Her expression switched from annoyed to pompous innocence.

“Thank God you came. She was just going crazy and hurting herself.”

Emily shook her head weakly.

“No.”

Lorraine held up her phone.

“I’m just documenting her condition.”

Thomas looked at the scissors. Looked at the hair again. Finally looking at Emily’s hands resting calmly on her knees.

“Put down the scissors.”

“You have no idea-”

“I understand very well.”

Lorraine did not put down the scissors, but retreated towards the bed. Thomas strode over and stood between her and Emily.

“Get down. Now.”

The pressure in his voice finally calmed her down completely. The scissors clattered to the ground. Thomas kicked the scissors under the dresser, knelt on one knee, and put his hand on Emily’s forehead. Hot skin burned his palms.

“Dad,” she murmured.

“I’m here.”

“They gave me something to eat.”

“What?”

“In the tea.”

Heavy footsteps rushed up the hall. The first deputy downstairs had identified himself. Lorraine’s face changed color.

“She was delirious.”

Deputy Marisol Grant looked at her coldly.

“You’ve been warned not to talk to her.”

Thomas looked at Lorraine.

“Then you don’t mind handing over the complete video to the police.”

Lorraine stared at him. The phone screen is on. That’s the camera app open. But there was no sign of it being recorded. There are only a few still photos. Emily collapsed on the ground.

Hair around her. The scissors lay not far from her hand. It was a carefully choreographed drama before Thomas arrived. They were not documenting a breakdown at all. They were manufacturing one.
