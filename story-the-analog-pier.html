The Analog Pier

On a rain-swept bridge over the Columbia River, a night technician discovers the transit director manually inspecting structural flaws under a secret brace. Together, they confront falsified telemetry that puts the public at risk.

Fictional story. Contains themes of workplace vulnerability, corporate corruption, and chronic illness.

Chapter 1: Vibration on the Catwalk

Marcus Brody had spent nine years managing the night shift for the Sentinel Narrows Toll Bridge, high above the churning waters of the Columbia River basin. He knew the bridge not by its painted iron girders or its sodium streetlights, but by its frequencies. When three fully loaded timber trucks crossed at forty miles per hour, the lower maintenance catwalk hummed in low D-minor. When the midnight river winds blew from the west, the steel cables vibrated like tuned wire.

Tonight, rain hammered the steel superstructure, turning the gorge into a blur of dark mist and rushing water. Marcus walked the lower cat-deck with a heavy industrial flashlight in one hand and a torque wrench hanging from his belt. At thirty-eight, his life was narrowed to precise measurements and strict domestic duties. Since his sister passed away two years ago, he had been raising his eight-year-old niece, Maya. Maya wore specialized custom leg braces to correct a developmental neuromuscular condition—braces that required quarterly adjustments, specialized physical therapy, and an out-of-pocket co-pay that ate half of Marcus’s monthly wages.
