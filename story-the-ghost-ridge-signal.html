Chapter 1: The Echoes of Cape Mercy

At the Cape Mercy Maritime Watch on the rugged coast of Maine, Hester Vance had mastered the art of quiet presence. She did not raise her voice, nor did she demand attention from the younger engineers who swept through the station with bright tablets and sleek uniforms. At fifty-nine, with her steel-gray hair gathered in a practical knot and her durable woolen cardigan smelling faintly of salt rain and ink, she seemed to the casual observer like part of the building’s old wooden trim—harmless, outdated, and easily overlooked.

Her official title was Senior Station Archivist. Working the midnight-to-eight shift in the lower control tower, Hester was responsible for maintaining the station’s historical logs, physical barometer scrolls, and legacy hydrophone charts. While the day shift relied entirely on high-frequency satellite telemetry, Hester understood that the Atlantic Ocean around Cape Mercy cared very little for digital vanity. The sea had a rhythm after midnight that software frequently missed: the low shudder of deep-water swells, the metallic hiss of spray against copper window frames, and the subtle pressure drops that signaled trouble long before an automated siren blared.
