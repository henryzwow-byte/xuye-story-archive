Voices in the Timber

Left for dead after an orchestrated avalanche at a remote timber dam, acoustic engineer Nora Vance holds fast to her heavy brass surveying transit—unaware it contains mechanical proof of intentional corporate destruction.

Fictional story. Contains depictions of extreme cold survival, corporate corruption, and peril.

Chapter 1: The Mountain Below Zero

The men who tried to bury me beneath forty feet of frozen timber and mountain runoff made a single fatal miscalculation. They assumed the blizzard would finish what their dynamite started, and that two days in the black crevasses of Blackhorn Pass would freeze my heart, swallow my body, and erase every record I had compiled on Sterling Hydro-Grid. From a heated board office four hundred miles away in Denver, Julian Sterling’s executives likely watched satellite infrared updates of the avalanche, marking my disappearance as a clean asset write-off. But when the Forest Service snow-tracked patrol found me wedged inside a hollow cedar drift, the heavy brass surveying transit was still locked against my ribcage. The truth was preserved inside its mechanical gears, and I was still drawing breath.

"Dead technicians don't sign safety affidavits," was the first coherent thought that returned to me through the haze of hypothermia. It did not come when my frostbitten fingers throbbed back to agony, but when the twin rotors of the rescue chopper beat against the mountain gale above the tree line. The thunder of the blades reached my ears like iron striking stone. When you spend six years auditing alpine infrastructure for wealthy energy conglomerates, you learn that rescue rarely arrives without conditions.
