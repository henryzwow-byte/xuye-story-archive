FICTIONAL STORY · A-044

Broken Ribs and the Kingsley Empire

Structural engineer Lena Ortiz discovers eight fractured steel ribs beneath the Kingsley railway terminal weeks before its grand reopening. When executives pressure her to approve cosmetic repairs, inspection photographs, altered calculations and a night test expose a corporate cover-up. Saving the landmark means confronting the empire that built it without turning the public into collateral.

Fiction. Contains structural danger, workplace intimidation, corporate misconduct, an emergency evacuation and legal investigation. No graphic injury.

Chapter One: Eight Broken Ribs

Lena Ortiz first saw the fractures at 2:17 in the morning, forty feet above the empty concourse of Kingsley Central Terminal. Her flashlight crossed one steel arch, then another, revealing pale lines beside old rivets. By dawn she had marked eight structural ribs with red tape.

The terminal was scheduled to reopen in nineteen days after a two-billion-dollar restoration led by Kingsley Infrastructure. Invitations had gone to governors, investors and television crews. Chairman Adrian Kingsley called Lena’s report preliminary and asked whether the lines might be old paint.

They were not paint. Ultrasonic readings showed fatigue spreading beneath the surface where replacement brackets had been welded to century-old steel. Lena recommended closing the site, removing temporary loads and testing every arch.

Project director Paul Danner told her a delay would trigger penalties large enough to destroy smaller subcontractors. He offered a compromise: inject resin into the visible cracks, repaint the arches and conduct full tests after the ceremony. Lena wrote one word across the approval form—Rejected.
