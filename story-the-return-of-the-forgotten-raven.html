The Return of the Forgotten Raven

A child carrying a carved raven leads an aging motorcycle charter into the badlands, where its missing founder is being held by an old criminal enemy.

Fiction. Contains abduction, organized crime, fire, armed confrontations, physical violence, injuries, gunfire, and death.

Chapter One: The Ghost in the Wood

The silence that fell over the dusty roadside truck stop was heavier than the sweltering August heat. It was the breathless, electric stillness that always came before a violent storm.

Cyrus—a weathered veteran of the underground with iron-gray hair and a gaze forged in a thousand midnight brawls—stared at the crude wooden toy resting in his palm. It was a simple semi-truck, hastily carved from pine, but its undercarriage carried a hidden signature.

A meticulously carved raven.

For twenty years, that symbol had been buried beneath blood, silence, and ash. It belonged to Elias, the legendary founder of their original charter, a man who had vanished two decades earlier to shield the brothers now standing around Cyrus from a federal purge.

“They found him,” Cyrus repeated. His low, gravelly voice made the surrounding riders stiffen. He lowered himself to one knee, bringing his face level with the trembling child. “What’s your name, son?”

“Leo,” the boy whispered, his knuckles white around the straps of a worn backpack. “My uncle Elias told me to run. He said if I brought this to the men wearing silver skulls on their leather, you would understand.”

Cyrus stood slowly, his massive frame casting a long shadow across the cracked asphalt. He looked at his brothers. No vote was called and no debate followed. The raven was more than a club emblem; it represented an oath none of them had forgotten.

“Mount up,” Cyrus ordered, his voice cutting through the afternoon air.

The truck stop erupted. Heavy boots struck kickstands, and twenty custom engines shattered the stillness, throwing exhaust and gravel into the sky. Cyrus lifted Leo into the sidecar of his cruiser and draped a leather jacket around the boy’s small shoulders.

“Hold on tight,” Cyrus called over the engines. “We’re bringing your uncle home.”

The convoy raced down Route 66 as the desert blurred into streaks of gold and dark crimson. Leo clutched the sidecar’s steel rim and guided Cyrus through forgotten dirt tracks leading deep into the jagged canyons of the badlands.

As the sun fell below the horizon, painting the sky purple and orange, the acrid smell of burning timber drifted over the ridge.

Cyrus raised a clenched fist. The convoy skidded to a stop in a cloud of dust.

A mile down the canyon floor, an isolated cabin was engulfed in flames. Black smoke rolled into the twilight. Four heavy black SUVs stood in a tactical perimeter around the burning structure.

The Iron Syndicate had arrived.

“They’re still here,” Jax, Cyrus’s second-in-command, said as he pulled a steel wrench from his saddlebag.

Cyrus’s eyes hardened. He glanced at Leo, whose face had gone pale. “Jax, stay with the boy. The rest of you—let’s show these men what happens when they wake the dead.”
