Shadows Over Timber Creek

When his teenage niece arrives during a mountain deluge with a locked antique transit box, a reclusive clockmaker is forced to confront his past and a predatory land developer to avert a disaster.

Fictional story. Contains themes of family estrangement, legal suspense, and mild peril in a storm environment.

Chapter 1: The Echo of the Pendulum

Arthur Vance did not measure time by second hands or wall calendars, but by the steady, resonant drop of copper escapements inside his workshop. Perched on a granite outcrop above Timber Creek in the Cascade foothills, his stone-and-timber sanctuary felt far removed from the bustling valley towns below. Tonight, however, the rhythmic ticking was drowned out by an unseasonable autumn torrent. Rain lashed the high clerestory windows, and the creek below was rising fast, churning muddy foam against the bridge piers.

At fifty-two, Arthur was a craftsman of rare patience and exactitude. He had spent twenty years restoring historic turret clocks, delicate pocket watches, and mechanical barographs for collectors across the Pacific Northwest. People in the county called him hermit-like, but Arthur preferred the predictable logic of brass gears to the volatile motives of human behavior. Six years earlier, a bitter argument with his younger sister, Clara, had severed his last tie to family. He had accused her new husband, civil engineering executive Julian Sterling, of ruthless financial ambition; Clara had called Arthur a self-righteous recluse who hid from life behind locked iron gates. Neither had reached across the silence since.
