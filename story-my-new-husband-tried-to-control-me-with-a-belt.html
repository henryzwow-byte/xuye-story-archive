My New Husband Tried to Control Me with a Belt

The morning after her honeymoon, Maya Bennett watches her new husband unbuckle a belt and announce that he will teach her how to be a proper wife. A recording, a police call and her late father's sealed instructions reveal that the threatened assault is tied to a false identity, a forged trust plan and a hidden warehouse her husband's family has been trying to reach for years.

Fiction. Contains coercive control, threatened domestic violence with a belt, bruising, financial abuse, deceptive identity, police response, stalking or unlawful entry, grief and family conspiracy.

Chapter One: The Belt After the Honeymoon

On the morning after our honeymoon ended, my new husband calmly unbuckled his belt, wrapped it around his fist and smiled as if he had waited his whole life for this moment. “It is time you learned how to be a proper wife,” he said.

He expected me to tremble and surrender to his control. What he did not know was that he had chosen the wrong woman. After Ethan and I returned from our honeymoon in Hawaii, the first thing he did when he walked in was neither to kiss me goodnight nor to help pack my luggage.

He unbuckled his belt.

The thick metal buckle hit the bedside table lamp with a harsh metal crunch, echoing in the bedroom, making the silence that followed even more suffocating. However, there was no trace of anger on his face, but instead a kind of contentment and arrogance, as if all this had been carefully planned by him from the moment we exchanged vows.

“Now that the honeymoon is over,” he said as he slowly wrapped the leather around his hand, “it’s time for you to learn how to be a wife.”

We had just gotten off the plane just a few hours ago. My suitcase was still open and slumped next to the bed, stuffed with brightly colored summer clothes, sunscreen, and two smiling photos pretending to have a perfect marriage.

Looking back now, all the warning signs were there. He finds fault with my attire. He corrected my tone in speaking to the waiter. He insisted on having the passwords to all my bank accounts.

I convinced myself it was just because he was insecure and mistook control for deep love. But the belt in his hand completely shattered this illusion. I didn’t cry. Nor did he beg for mercy.

Instead, I silently unbuttoned my baggy travel shirt and draped it over the chair next to me. His smile deepened.

“Good,” he said. “Being obedient makes things easier.”

Underneath the shirt, I wore a black compression vest and boxer shorts. Without looking away, I took out a pair of frayed red boxing gloves from the suitcase, clenched the straps with my teeth, and put them neatly on my hands.

Then, I laughed.

“Perfect,” I whispered, “I’m just in need of a sparring partner.”

Ethan was stunned for a moment. Then he burst out laughing so much that he almost dropped his belt. He knew I worked at a community gym, but he never bothered to care about what exactly I did. He thought I was just greeting guests, wiping down equipment, and handling paperwork.

He never wondered why I had scars on my knuckles that would never go away. He never noticed the framed photo of me lifting the national boxing championship trophy that sits in my office. He moved first.

The belt broke through the air and struck. I ducked sideways and hit his chest with a clean, straight punch, watching the confidence on his face collapse. Shock suddenly replaced arrogance. He pounced again.

There is no structure. Furious. And predictable. I grabbed his wrist, spun around, swept his legs, and swept him down to the carpet. His lungs gasped in pain as the air was squeezed out. I could have ended the fight right there. But I stepped back, pulled out my phone, and pressed the emergency button.

“Get out,” I said.

His face was twisted in humiliation.

“You hit me,” he yelled, “and I’m going to tell everyone you attacked me.”

I glanced at the tiny security camera hidden in the smoke detector.

“In that case,” I replied calmly, “the story would be wonderful.”

For the first time fear appeared on his face. But it was fleeting. What replaced it was a colder calculation. He stood up, grabbed his phone, and without even taking his eyes off me, dialed his mother’s number.

“Mom,” he said, “she’s crazy.”

His mother’s voice immediately came from the receiver.

“Then go with the plan,” she said, “before she realizes why you want to marry her.”

I didn’t move. My heart fell completely silent. Our engagement was strangely quick, and it happened after I told Ethan that my late father had left several valuable properties in Chicago. He thought sadness made me vulnerable, that loneliness made me highly manageable.

Without saying a word, I pressed the record button. His mother’s voice was low.

“I’ll sign it tomorrow,” she said. “As long as the property is transferred to your name, no one will care what happens to this family in the future...”

My fingers were hanging on the save button, and Ethan slowly turned around. That look in his eyes made the blood in my body run cold.
