Chapter 1: The Sanctuary at Cedar Ridge

The scent of fresh-shaved red cedar, linseed oil, and crisp mountain air filled the workshop as sunset spilled across the Columbia River Gorge. For Arthur Vance, sixty-two years old and built like the old timber beams he framed, the quiet of Cedar Ridge Studio was a sacred inheritance earned through decades of relentless toil. Three months had passed since he finalized the sale of Vance Architectural Joinery, the custom millwork firm he built from a lone table saw in a drafty garage into a respected regional powerhouse.

The acquisition had netted him three million dollars after taxes and debt liquidations. He didn't want yacht clubs or velvet curtains. Arthur bought forty acres of old-growth timber in Hood River, Oregon, along with a timber-framed studio barn that he personally spent four years restoring. His dream was simple: to spend his remaining healthy years crafting bespoke studio furniture, drinking black coffee on his west-facing terrace, and waking up without the weight of three dozen payroll accounts pressing on his chest.
