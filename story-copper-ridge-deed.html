The Copper Ridge Deed

After receiving a prestigious maritime craft fellowship, Nora Vance faces demands from her stepfather and cousin to forfeit her career. Locked out of her family's timber yard, Nora uncovers a hidden land covenant that changes everything.

Fictional story. Depicts family emotional conflict, eviction threats, and legal disputes over inherited property.

Chapter 1: The Fellowship Seal

The salt fog off Blackwood Bay always smelled of pitch pine and wet cedar. At twenty-one, Nora Vance had spent six winters in the drafty loft above her family’s timber workshop, shaping ash oars and recording timber grain density by oil lamp. On a damp Tuesday evening in October, the mail carrier brought a thick manila envelope sealed with red wax. Inside was her formal admission to the Northwest Maritime Preservation Fellowship—a funded master apprenticeship in Puget Sound that came with a ten-thousand-dollar tool stipend and a place at the historic shipwright harbor.

Nora held the parchment against her chest, her thumb tracing the embossed anchor crest. For three years she had submitted her drafting portfolios, working extra night shifts at the sawyer's yard to buy her own calipers, chisels, and copper rivets. She ran down the narrow wooden stairs to the ground floor, where the scent of freshly planed Douglas fir hung heavy in the air. Her stepfather, Arthur Vance, was standing by the workbench, wiping grease off an old hydraulic press while his sister’s son, Lyle, slumped across a pile of oak timbers scrolling through his phone.
