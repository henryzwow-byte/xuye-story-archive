FICTIONAL STORY · A-255

The Covenant at Greywater Point

Following the passing of Captain Arthur Vance, his estranged son Julian arrives at Saltwind Manor for the reading of the will. Expecting to take everything, Arthur's second wife Victoria presents an older testament—unaware that Arthur left behind a maritime trust with a self-executing forfeiture clause.

Fictional story. Contains themes of testamentary disputes, emotional deception, and family estrangement.

Chapter 1: The Saltwind Covenant

Rain hammered against the diamond-paned glass of Saltwind Manor, a grey-stone estate perched above the roaring swells of Penobscot Bay. Inside the grand library, the air smelled of damp cedar, brass polish, and cold Earl Grey tea that nobody had touched. Julian Vance stood by the high hearth, his hands shoved deep into the pockets of a worn waxed-canvas coat. The jacket was damp around the cuffs, carrying the bitter scent of ocean brine, a stark contrast to the immaculate velvet slippers worn by his stepbrother sitting near the fire.

Julian had spent six years far from Penobscot Bay, tracking marine acoustic patterns aboard oceanographic research vessels. Returning to the manor felt like stepping into a house designed to erase him. Opposite him sat Victoria Vance, his late father’s second wife, clad in tailored charcoal silk that looked less like mourning attire and more like armor selected for a boardroom takeover. Her posture was flawless, her pearl earrings catching the pale grey light filtering through the storm clouds outside.
