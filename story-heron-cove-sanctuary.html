Heron Cove Sanctuary

After years of working in a Seattle shipyard to buy her mother a coastal botanical nursery, Clara returns unannounced to find local relatives exploiting the estate.

Fictional story. Depicts family financial boundary disputes and recovery of elder dignity. Contains no real-world legal advice.

Chapter 1: The Glasshouse in the Rain

For seven long years, the image of Heron Cove Nursery had been the single anchor keeping me steady through double shifts at the shipyards in Puget Sound. I had spent cold damp mornings restoring timber frames and welding steel hulls, saving every spare dollar so my widowed mother, Eleanor, could finally have a peaceful place of her own. She was a master botanist whose life's passion was cultivating rare Pacific heritage flora, and the historic glasshouse nestled on the Oregon coast was supposed to be her sanctuary after decades of hard labor.

When I turned off the coastal highway that foggy October afternoon, the salt mist hung low over the spruce trees. I expected to hear the soft clink of my mother's glass watering globes or see her sitting comfortably under the patio awning with a warm mug of herbal tea. Instead, as my truck pulled onto the gravel driveway, I heard the brutal, metallic scrape of heavy wooden crates dragging across wet concrete.
