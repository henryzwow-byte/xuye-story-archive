The Salt Pine Ledger

Dismissed by her father's traditional maritime auditing firm as uncredentialed, Nora Miller leaves home to found an innovative eco-timber marine engineering company. Years later, a public industry crisis forces her family to confront their prejudice and acknowledge her true worth.

Fictional story. Depicts past emotional family strain, professional disagreement, and eventual personal reconciliation.

Chapter 1: The Silver Anniversary

The night my father publicly declared that my work was nothing more than unaccredited carpentry fit for scrap heaps, I did not drop my glass. I stood near the edge of the crystal chandelier light at the Maritime Exchange Guild in Portland, watching sixty guests in evening wear freeze over their cedar-planked salmon and vintage wines. My father, Julian Miller, senior partner of Miller Marine Audits, pointed his index finger toward the oak doors of the banquet hall. His voice carried the sharp, practiced timber of a man who spent forty years issuing violations to ship captains.

"You are still a drop-out playing with driftwood," Julian said, ensuring the board members heard every syllable. "Leave this hall, Nora. Do not embarrass this family by pretending your informal yard work belongs on an official engineering ledger."
