The Chronometer at Cape Sentinel

Returning from an eleven-month oceanographic expedition, horologist Julian Vance discovers his coastal Oregon workshop under the hostile control of an ambitious speculator. Guided by secret evidence hidden within an antique marine chronometer, Julian uncovers a web of forgery, extortion, and psychological coercion.

Fictional story. Depicts themes of corporate forgery, psychological coercion, and emotional stress. Reader discretion is advised.

Chapter 1: The Cold Workshop

Julian Vance stepped off the coastal shuttle at Cape Sentinel under a veil of misty Pacific rain, carrying his heavy tool canvas and cedar storage case. For eleven months, he had worked in the damp steel hold of an oceanographic survey vessel off the Alaskan shelf, repairing deep-sea compasses and antique brass chronometers. Every night spent listening to the ocean’s rhythm had been sustained by a single grounding image: his cliffside workshop overlooking the cove, filled with the warmth of brass polish, drying varnish, and the comforting ticking of century-old escapements.

The workshop light was burning as he walked up the gravel path, but the silhouette framed in the broad bay window was not his wife, Clara. It belonged to Gareth Hayes, a prominent board member of the regional maritime heritage trust, standing over the workbench with an unrolled blueprint. Beside him stood Sienna Brooks, a private estate appraiser whose sleek leather briefcase rested atop Julian’s personal drafting table.
