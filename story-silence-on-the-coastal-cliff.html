Silence on the Coastal Cliff

In a foggy Oregon coastal town, a mother's precision records and an urgent search for her estranged husband's hidden maritime dive archives unlock the key to her daughter's sudden, mystifying neurological illness.

Fictional story. Depicts pediatric neurological testing, hospital diagnostics, and family separation. All characters, settings, and medical conditions are entirely fictionalized for creative storytelling.

Chapter 1: The Tremor in the Strings

The damp mist of Cape Tillamook always smelled of crushed pine needles and cold tide pools. In the timber-framed workshop overlooking the Pacific breakers, Clara Vance spent her afternoons restoring nineteenth-century brass chronometers and fresnel lighthouse lenses. The work demanded absolute stillness—a steady hand, a jeweler’s loupe, and an ear attuned to the faint, mechanical heartbeat of brass gears. Across the rug, her eleven-year-old daughter Iris sat near the bay window, practicing Bach’s Cello Suite No. 1. The low, resonant hum of the wooden body usually anchored the workshop, blending seamlessly with the rain against the glass.

But at four o'clock on a Tuesday afternoon, the music stopped mid-measure with a sharp, discordant screech of horsehair on steel. Clara paused her magnifying glass over a pendulum escape wheel and turned around. Iris had dropped her bow onto the braided rug. Her left hand hung uselessly beside the fingerboard, her fingers curled slightly as if frozen mid-chord. She was pressing her right forehead against the polished maple rib of the instrument, her breathing fast and shallow.
