FICTIONAL STORY · A-053

He Always Comes

Fifteen-year-old Eli has learned not to trust promises, except for one: public defender David Hale always comes to his foster-care hearings. When David misses a crucial morning, a delayed train, altered case notes and a hidden billing scheme reveal why. Their reunion becomes a test of chosen family, patient advocacy and the systems adults are supposed to protect.

Fiction. Contains foster-care instability, institutional neglect, financial misconduct, anxiety, a transport accident without graphic injury and family-court proceedings.

Chapter One: The Empty Chair

Eli Morgan arrived at family court carrying everything he owned in a school backpack. At fifteen, he had lived in seven placements and learned to distrust sentences beginning with I promise. The exception was David Hale, the public-interest lawyer who had attended every one of Eli’s hearings for three years.

At nine thirty, David’s chair was empty. The agency asked the judge to approve Eli’s transfer to Northfield Residence, two counties away. Its report claimed Eli needed intensive supervision and had repeatedly run away. Eli had left one placement without permission—but only to reach a library after staff locked away his school laptop. The report omitted that detail. It also described him as aggressive because he had refused to sign a blank behavior plan.

The judge postponed the transfer for four hours and appointed a temporary advocate. Eli stared at the empty chair and tried not to feel foolish for having believed David always came.
