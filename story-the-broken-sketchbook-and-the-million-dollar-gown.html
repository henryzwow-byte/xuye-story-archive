FICTIONAL STORY · A-091

The Broken Sketchbook and the Million-Dollar Gown

After a fashion executive destroys a cleaner’s secret portfolio, a ruined million-dollar gown gives the quiet young artist ninety seconds to reveal her extraordinary gift.

Fiction. Contains workplace bullying, class prejudice, verbal humiliation, destruction of personal property, and emotional distress.

Chapter One: The Apex of Cruelty

The red-carpet tent smelled of expensive lilies, French champagne, and fear. Crystal chandeliers hung from the ceiling like frozen tears, illuminating a room full of corporate lawyers, international investors, and prominent business leaders who had gathered for Vanguard Prêt-à-Porter’s most important gala of the year.

Regina stood at both the literal and social center of the room. Her immaculate, triple-tailored white suit cost more than many employees earned in a year, and she carried the terrifying confidence of a woman accustomed to breaking careers without consequence. As Vanguard’s senior executive, her word functioned as law. She wore that authority like impenetrable armor.

“You thought you could do what?” Her voice sliced through the conversation and silenced the tent.

Maya stood before her in a faded gray cleaning uniform. At twenty-two, she had spent six months polishing the showroom’s marble floors, but her hands knew more than the rough handle of a mop. They also knew the fluid language of charcoal and ink. In her left hand she held a battered, leather-bound sketchbook—her only refuge in a world that treated her as background noise.

“I asked you a question,” Regina said, stepping closer as her stilettos struck the marble. “Did you believe that because I allow you to breathe the same air as my clients, you could leave this trash on a designer’s table?”

“It isn’t trash, Ms. Regina,” Maya replied, her voice steadier than her knees. “It is my portfolio. I left it by accident while cleaning the mahogany desk before the gala, and I came back to retrieve it.”

Regina laughed loudly enough to invite her subordinates to join her. “The woman who cleans our restrooms thinks she is a designer.” A few junior executives offered nervous chuckles.

She snatched the sketchbook from Maya and opened it. Page after page revealed intricate avant-garde gowns—work more daring and coherent than anything in Vanguard’s current collection. The discovery of rare talent in someone she considered inferior did not soften Regina. It threatened the hierarchy on which her identity depended.

“This is an insult to fashion,” Regina declared. Then she gripped the heavy paper and tore the first page in two. Maya reached forward. “Please, Ms. Regina. Don’t.”

Regina tore out page after page and scattered the pieces across the red carpet. She threw the mangled leather cover at Maya’s feet, then planted one heel on a charcoal drawing of a structured geometric dress. “Know your place. You scrub floors. Gather this rubbish, empty your locker, and leave. You are fired.”

The guests watched with shallow amusement or careful indifference. To them, Maya was merely another casualty of Regina’s authority. Maya looked down at the ruins of her private dream, breathing hard. She did not cry. Instead, a cold and focused stillness settled into her eyes.
