The Foundry Ledger

When architectural restorer Harlan Croft attends his father's funeral in a rust-belt town, a cryptic casting token leads him to a submerged quarry vault containing evidence of decades of toxic dumping and corporate murder.

Fictional story. Contains themes of family secrets, corporate corruption, and corporate crime investigation.

Chapter 1: The Broken Anvil

The autumn rain over Iron Mill, Pennsylvania, had turned the gravel driveway of St. Jude’s chapel into a slick bed of dark gray slurry. Standing beneath the dripping slate eaves of the rectory, Harlan Croft adjusted the heavy wool collar of his coat while the last mourners hurried toward their shivering sedans. His father, Jonathan Croft, had spent forty-two uninterrupted years managing the casting floor at the historic Blackwood Iron Works—a quiet man known throughout the valley for his leather-bound logbooks, grease-stained cuffs, and an unyielding silence regarding the town’s ruling family. When Jonathan collapsed beside his workbench three nights ago, the county coroner had recorded heart failure without a second thought. It was presented as a clean, peaceful ending for a hard-working craftsman, or so Harlan had tried desperately to convince himself during the three days of hurried funeral arrangements.

As Harlan turned to walk toward his truck, a calloused, heavy hand clamped firmly onto his upper arm. Samuel Vance, a retired foundry machinist with silver stubble lining his jaw and oil-stained knuckles, stepped out from behind a weeping willow tree. His eyes darted nervously toward a polished black sedan idling near the chapel gates, where County Magistrate Arthur Pendelton sat watching the crowd behind dark, tinted glass. "Do not sign those probate waiver papers tomorrow morning, Harlan," Samuel rasped, his voice barely rising above the drum of rain on the chapel roof. "Your father didn’t die of a tired heart, and that foundry explosion back in ninety-six was never an industrial accident."
