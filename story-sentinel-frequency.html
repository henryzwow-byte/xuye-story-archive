FICTIONAL STORY · A-352

The Sentinel Frequency

After receiving a distress call on her ham radio scanner, a retired estate auditor travels into a mountain pass to rescue her nephew—and exposes a paper trail of deliberate neglect.

Fictional story. Contains themes of child neglect, family deception, and legal investigation.

Chapter 1: Static on the Frequency

Clara Thorne had spent thirty-four years as a senior county estate auditor in eastern Washington, a quiet career built on unearthing missing property titles, phantom shell companies, and subtle arithmetic discrepancies that cunning people assumed no one would ever take the time to notice. At sixty-four, living in a handcrafted timber-frame house nestled among the pine foothills of Spokane Valley, her retirement was structured around quiet, purposeful routines: brewing dark chicory coffee at dawn, pruning her heirloom apple orchard, and monitoring local amateur radio frequencies on an old, meticulously calibrated base station transceiver.

Her eleven-year-old grand-nephew, Julian Vance, shared her deep fascination with radio signals and electronics. Julian was an introverted boy with extraordinary analytical focus, often overwhelmed by chaotic crowds and sensory noise, but remarkably calm and capable when assembling circuit boards, solar telemetry sensors, and shortwave antennas. His mother, Evelyn—Clara’s niece—had long regarded Julian’s quiet, observant nature as a social inconvenience, particularly after her marriage to Marcus Reed, an ambitious regional real estate developer who treated family life as an extension of his corporate public relations image.
