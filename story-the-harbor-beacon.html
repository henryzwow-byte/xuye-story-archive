The Harbor Beacon

A master boat restorer uncovers a hidden betrayal at her coastal Maine heritage drydock and uses public transparency to reclaim her legacy.

Fictional story. Contains themes of marital infidelity, emotional distress, and financial deceit.

Chapter 1: The Discrepancy on the Ledger

The first thing Nora Vance noticed was the irregular cadence of the harbor water against the granite seawall. It was not the crisp, rhythmic slap of high tide, but a sluggish sloshing caused by a vessel idling where no ship was cleared to anchor. At thirty-eight, Nora had spent twenty years managing Vance Heritage Maritime, a historic timber drydock and navigational chronometer restoration workshop tucked into the jagged cove of Sentinel Bay, Maine. The business was her father’s legacy, built on hand-turned brass fittings, reclaimed white oak, and an uncompromising reputation for precision.

She pulled her utility wagon into the workshop gravel driveway at 4:15 p.m. on a Thursday. Her last client meeting in town had ended early, leaving her an unexpected hour before the evening’s opening reception for the annual Coastal Heritage Gala. She carried a heavy canvas bag packed with brass polishes, mahogany oil, a fresh loaf of sourdough bread, and two crisp winter pears she had bought at the farm stand. The air smelled of salt spray, cold iron, wet cedar, and the sharp tang of wild bayberry bushes lining the shore.
