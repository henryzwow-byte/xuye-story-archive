The Resonance at Deadwood Gorge

A gripping tale of alpine transit, acoustic telemetry, and an observant fourteen-year-old girl who must convince skeptical adults before a mountain passenger train crosses a compromised trestle.

Fictional story. Depicts mild transit peril, extreme winter conditions, and engineering tension without graphic violence or non-fictional risk instructions.

Chapter 1: The Mountain Corridor

The passenger car smelled of roasted dark coffee, damp wool coats, and the faint metallic tang of forced electric heat. Outside the double-paned reinforced windows of the Cascadia Alpine Limited, a midnight blizzard tore across the timberline of the Cascade Range. Pine branches bent double under wet white curtains, snapping into the dark as the mountain express climbed steadily toward Ridge Notch Cut.

Fourteen-year-old Nora Vance sat near the rear of Car 4, tucked into an upholstered window booth. She wore a dark blue knit sweater with the sleeves rolled up to her forearms. In her lap lay a scuffed, leather-bound notebook filled with hand-drawn structural cross-sections, shear-stress formulas, and acoustic resonance tables. Resting against the page was a pair of weathered brass drafting calipers—a tool her mother had used for fifteen years while inspecting high-altitude rail foundations for the Pacific North Transit Authority.
