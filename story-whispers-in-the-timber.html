Whispers in the Timber

When architectural historian Clara Vance investigates forged inspection stamps on a coastal restoration project, she uncovers a decades-long financial scheme orchestrated by her own husband.

Fictional story. Depicts marital betrayal, corporate financial fraud, and non-fatal structural sabotage. All characters and settings are entirely fictional.

Chapter 1: The Broken Grain

The salt fog had settled thick over Blackwood Harbor by seven in the morning, sticking to the rough oak pilings of the nineteenth-century maritime station. Clara Vance ran her gloved thumb across a hand-carved support beam inside the dry dock loft. As a senior architectural historian hired by the coastal preservation trust, she knew the feel of honest timber—the slow growth rings of old-growth pine, dense and heavy under a steel caliper. But beneath her touch, the beam groaned under light pressure, its surface painted over with dark tar to mimic aged heartwood.

She scraped a thumbnail of paint away, revealing soft, fast-grown yellow pine underneath—a cheap commercial grade completely unfit for load-bearing marine structures. Stamped into the end grain was a heavy iron mark: a crowned anchor inside a geometric double circle. It was supposed to be the official quality certification from the State Maritime Inspection Bureau. But Clara knew the stamp intimately. The inner radius was off by two millimeters, the anchor crown lacked its traditional bevel, and the ink used was synthetic polyurethane, not the classic oil compound phased out fifteen years ago.
