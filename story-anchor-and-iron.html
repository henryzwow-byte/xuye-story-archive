FICTIONAL STORY · A-210

Anchor and Iron

When naval draftsman Hannah Mercer is invited to the high-society opening of a maritime museum, she brings more than historic blueprints—she brings the undeniable truth of her work and her sons' bloodline.

Fictional story. Contains themes of past corporate deception, high-society pressure, and single parenthood.

Chapter 1: The Wax Seal of Puget Sound

The late afternoon rain along the waterfront of Port Townsend had a quiet, methodical cadence, tapping against the high glass panes of the drafting loft like small wooden pegs. Outside, the tide was pulling out of Puget Sound, turning the gray seawater into long strips of foam against the weathered cedar pilings. The cold Pacific wind hummed against the eaves, carrying the clean scent of wet pine and distant salt marshes.

Inside the workshop, the atmosphere was warm and inviting—a comforting blend of freshly planed red cedar, linseed oil, brass polish, and wood smoke from the cast-iron stove in the corner. Hannah Mercer stood at her long oak drafting table, adjusting a heavy brass T-square across a sheet of high-density vellum paper. Below her work table, on a woven jute rug, her twin seven-year-old sons were engrossed in their own quiet engineering project. Caleb was carefully aligning brass gear wheels salvaged from an old ship's chronometer, while Silas narrated the imaginary journey of a miniature wooden cargo crane with total, solemn concentration.
