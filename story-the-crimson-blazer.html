FICTIONAL STORY · A-101

The Crimson Blazer

An heiress trapped by forged transfer papers finds one surviving clue to her father's investigation—and a federal prosecutor determined to preserve the truth before the company erases it.

Fiction. Contains coercion, physical assault, threats, corporate fraud, surveillance, firearms, and legal proceedings.

Chapter 1: The Broken Archive

The executive office had gone silent except for Elena Vale's uneven breathing and the faint mechanical pulse of the security camera above the bookshelves. Marcus Thorne, her late father's former protégé and the company's acting chief executive, stood over her with a transfer agreement open on the desk. His polished shoe held her right hand against the floor while he pressed a fountain pen toward her left.

"Sign it," Marcus said. "The board has already approved the restructuring. Your signature only makes the record look orderly." Elena knew the document did far more than reorganize the company. It assigned her voting shares to a trust controlled by Marcus and released him from liability for decisions made during her father's final year.

When Marcus shifted his weight, a folded strip of archival paper slipped from his inside pocket. Elena recognized the blue fibers used in her father's private records and the impression of his original seal. She dragged the fragment beneath her sleeve before Marcus noticed. It contained a date, a sequence of letters, and the handwritten words: MIRROR LEDGER.

The oak doors opened. Assistant United States Attorney Julianna Vance entered in a crimson blazer with two federal agents and the company's outside counsel. The agents carried a signed search warrant; counsel carried a board resolution suspending Marcus pending an independent investigation. Julianna did not announce a conviction or stage a spectacle. She ordered Marcus away from Elena, documented the position of every object in the room, and called for medical assistance.

Marcus insisted Elena had attacked him and that the document on the desk was legitimate. Julianna pointed to the camera. The building's internal system had been disabled nine minutes earlier, but the outside law firm had preserved a mirrored feed under a litigation hold. Several directors had watched Marcus demand the signature. The recording did not decide the case, Julianna warned them; it established why the room and its contents could not be altered.
