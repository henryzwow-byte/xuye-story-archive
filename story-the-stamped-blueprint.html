The Stamped Blueprint

At a gala celebrating her brother's architectural awards, a master timber-framer quietly revokes her engineering stamp, exposing the secret financial retainers and stolen designs that built his career.

Fictional story. Contains mild themes of family conflict, emotional betrayal, and financial misconduct.

Chapter 1: The Timber and the Shadow

The October rain had settled thick over the coastal ridges of Pinecrest, beating a soft, rhythmic pulse against the ironwood beams of the Vance Carriage House. Inside, the air smelled of cured cedar, spiced cider, and polished beeswax. String lights cast golden halos across the timber-framed trusses overhead—trusses that I had spent three weeks cut-fitting with a broadaxe and hand chisel while my brother stood in the warmth of his Portland showroom, explaining design philosophy to architectural magazines.

I arrived at six in the evening with sawdust still caught in the cuffs of my wool jacket. My right wrist throbbed with the deep, dull ache that comes from fifteen years of timber joinery, a gift from heavy Douglas fir logs and cold mornings on scaffolding. As I hung my coat near the hearth, my mother, Evelyn, stepped out of the dining hall, wearing her silk scarf and a look of practiced disappointment.
