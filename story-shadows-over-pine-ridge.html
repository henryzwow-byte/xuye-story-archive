Shadows Over Pine Ridge

High above the New Mexico high desert, a high-altitude cable car stalls over an abyss during an unpredicted storm. Mountain technician Julian Vance braves fraying steel and hidden environmental hazards to bring two trapped passengers to safety.

Fictional story. Depicts high-altitude rescue hazards, storm turbulence, and severe weather tension.

Chapter 1: The Summit Station

The sky above the Pine Ridge Escarpment turned the color of bruised slate an hour before twilight. Julian Vance stood on the open steel gantry of Relay Station Nine, his heavy work gloves gripping the cold iron guardrail while freezing desert air whistled through the structural lattice. Down in the basin, six thousand feet below, the arid floor of eastern New Mexico stretched out like a vast, tawny sea broken only by dry riverbeds, chalky gullies, and scrub juniper.

Julian was forty-two, a former structural mechanic whose life was defined by mountain maintenance and quiet discipline. Since the passing of his wife five years ago, he had chosen the solitude of the high ridge stations, keeping the county’s emergency radio repeaters, mountain weather sensors, and public relay towers operational. His fifteen-year-old daughter, Clara, spent her late afternoons at the valley observatory office down at the base, logging atmospheric data and coordinating telemetry while her father worked the isolated crests.
