FICTIONAL STORY · A-074

The Final Account

Daniel returns home before his wedding with an expensive plan and a fiancée who measures his parents by their money. As demands, insults and old sacrifices surface, Thomas and Margaret must decide whether love means paying one more bill—or finally closing the account.

Fiction. Contains family conflict, financial pressure, class prejudice, emotional manipulation, illness and grief.

Chapter One: The Returned Son

On a rainy night three months before the wedding, Daniel Hale returned to his parents’ home outside Boston with two suitcases and an air of expectation. Thomas, a builder with forty years in the trade, had constructed the house himself. It was comfortable rather than grand, filled with the old furniture and family photographs Margaret loved.

Daniel had once loved the house. As a boy, he raced through the hallway, hid behind the kitchen curtains, and waited faithfully at the front window for Thomas to come home from work. Margaret saved every picture he drew, even the ones made of nothing but crooked circles and lines.

Now thirty-two, Daniel walked in without knocking.

“Mom?”

Margaret hurried from the kitchen to greet him. For an instant she saw the boy he had been; then she noticed the expensive wool coat, the designer watch, and the impatience in his expression.

“You’re early,” she smiled and hugged him.

“The road was smoother than expected.”

Thomas came out of the study and said hello with a smile.

“Our protagonist is back.”

Daniel shook hands with his father and then gave his mother a brief hug. Following him was his fiancée Vanessa. She wore a slim-fitting ivory coat and carried her handbag high in her hands, as if she was afraid of contaminating the dust of the house.

“Margaret,” she blew a gentle kiss, “this place hasn’t changed at all.”

Margaret chose to take this as a compliment.

“Glad you could come.”

The dinner started in a peaceful atmosphere. Margaret specially prepared Daniel’s favorite roast chicken, rosemary potatoes and apple pie. However, just after dessert, Daniel placed a folder on the table.

“We have set a date,” he announced.

Margaret immediately smiled with relief.

“Great.”

“October 18th,” Vanessa said quickly, “at Bellweather Manor.”

Thomas raised his eyebrows. Bellwether Manor is one of the region’s leading luxury wedding venues. Its gardens overlook a private lake, and its ballroom has hosted countless dignitaries, athletes and prominent families.

“It’s not a cheap place,” Thomas said.

Vanessa leaned forward.

“It was immaculate. There was a lakeside ceremony, a glass ballroom, big enough for three hundred guests.”

Margaret glanced at Daniel.

“Three hundred people?”

“We all have our own social circles,” he replied.

Daniel is an investment adviser, but he has changed jobs twice in the past four years. Vanessa has accumulated a good number of fans by recording high-end restaurants, big-name clothing and luxury travel on social networks.

Margaret had tried to understand Vanessa’s work. She even watched her videos and left supportive comments until Vanessa asked her to stop because the comments were “too motherly and didn’t fit with her personal brand.”

Thomas opened the folder. Inside is a page-by-page breakdown of estimated costs. Venue Rental. FOOD. band. flowers. photography. transportation. Guest accommodation. At the bottom, the total amount was approaching US$200,000. Thomas closed the folder.

“Are you asking for advice or money?”

Daniel smiled, but the smile didn’t reach his eyes.

“We’re hoping to get some funding.”

“How much funding is needed?” Thomas asked.

Vanessa answered on her behalf: “The Cole family will be responsible for my wedding dress and rehearsal dinner. We feel that the Hale family can cover the main ceremony and reception expenses.”

Margaret looked at Thomas. They had actually privately discussed funding Daniel in the future, but never imagined it would be on this scale. Their retirement funds need to be managed carefully. In addition, Margaret also inherited an estate from her deceased sister, who had no children.

Thomas leaned back in his chair.

“With a fraction of this money, you can have an beautiful wedding.”

Vanessa’s back straightened.

“We don’t want a scratch wedding.”

“Vanessa,” Daniel whispered to stop.

“No, it doesn’t matter,” she smiled at Thomas and said, “I understand that some people don’t value the pomp of weddings. But Daniel’s clients will all attend. This wedding is directly related to his image in the workplace.”

Thomas studied his son.

“Is this what you really think?”

Daniel hesitated.

“This is important.”

Margaret sensed something was wrong in the atmosphere and reached for the folder.

“I’ll discuss this with your dad.”

Daniel breathed a sigh of relief.

“Of course.”

Vanessa refused to give up.

“The manor requires the deposit to be paid before Friday.”

Thomas' face fell.

“You didn’t even know where the money came from, so you booked the wedding?”

“We are investing in the future,” Vanessa replied confidently, “Families should support each other.”

Margaret put her hand on Thomas’s.

“We’ll talk.”

Late that night, after Daniel and Vanessa had left, Thomas stood alone at the kitchen window.

“You know what they’re planning,” he said.

“They’re just so excited.”

“They’re manipulating you.”

Margaret silently cleared away the dessert plates.

“He is our biological son.”

“But that doesn’t mean we should pay for his irresponsibility.”

“He’s been working hard.”

Thomas turned around.

“Really?”

Margaret stopped what she was doing. Daniel has borrowed money twice in the past three years. The first time, he said he needed money to move closer to the company; the second time, he said an investment temporarily froze his savings. These two sums of money have never been paid back once.

Margaret always defended him unconditionally.

“He was looking for direction in life,” she always said.

But Thomas clearly saw that Daniel was subtly deteriorating. After meeting Vanessa, their son became more and more vain, thinking only about appearance. However, Margaret still had another Daniel in her mind. She remembered that when he was eight years old, he sat beside her hospital bed and stayed with her after she had just had knee surgery and refused to go home.

She remembers him giving his lunch to a classmate who couldn’t afford a meal. She remembered the little boy who burst into tears after accidentally stepping on a beetle.

“I don’t want to lose him,” she whispered.

Thomas' anger turned into distress.

“Saying 'no' doesn’t mean losing him.”

“What if he thinks we don’t support him?”

“Support does not mean unconditional compromise.”

Margaret looked toward the hallway where Daniel’s childhood photos hung.

“My inheritance from Elaine is bigger than I thought. We didn’t have to dip into our pension to afford the wedding.”

“That’s your money.”

“I just want to spend it on our son.”

Thomas was silent. Margaret took his hand.

“One day, we will all leave. I don’t want to look back when I get older and regret that I didn’t celebrate him in time.”

Thomas stared into her face. He knew only too well how much she loved Daniel. He also knew that no argument could shake her determination. Finally, he sighed.

“If we want to help, we have to set rules.”

Margaret nodded repeatedly.

“Every major expenditure must be approved by us. There are no empty promises. No surprise attacks are allowed.”

“It’s a deal.”

“And Daniel must understand that this is your gift, not our obligation.”

“He’ll understand.”

Thomas wished he could believe this. The next morning, Margaret called Daniel. She put it on speakerphone so Thomas could hear.

“We’ve decided to help you,” she said. “We’ve covered all the basic expenses for the venue, catering and reception.”

Daniel breathed out a sigh of relief.

“Mom, thank you.”

Vanessa’s voice came faintly through the receiver.

“What about the flowers and the band?”

Thomas looked at Margaret. Margaret’s smile stiffened slightly.

“We will review the full budget together.”

Daniel quickly interrupted.

“That’s fair. Mom, we really appreciate it, really.”

Margaret smiled the most she had in months.

“I just want your wedding to be perfect.”

“It’ll be perfect,” Daniel promised.

After the call ended, Thomas looked at the silent phone. Margaret kissed him on the cheek.

“You’ll see. It’ll bring us closer.”

However, on the other side of the city, Vanessa had opened a spreadsheet. She typed a few words at the top: 【Haier Wedding Fund】 Then she started adding items to it that Margaret had never agreed to buy.
