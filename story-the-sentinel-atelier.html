The Sentinel Atelier

When Clara's husband dies suddenly, his estranged brother attempts to confiscate their historic cliffside workshop. But foresight and a quiet legacy stand in the way of betrayal.

Fictional story. Contains themes of family conflict, grief, and estate disputes.

Chapter 1: The Cold Ember

The ocean fog rolled over the granite cliffs of Port Sentinel long before the memorial service ended. It was not the kind of mist that washed the town clean; it was cold, heavy, and saturated with salt, clinging to the wool coats of the mourners like an unwanted embrace. By the time the last church bell echoed down the ravine, my boots were thoroughly soaked, and my daughter Teresa's fingers felt like damp marble in my palm. Friends and acquaintances pressed close near the chapel gates, murmuring soft platitudes about resilience and strength. I found myself cringing at the word strong. It felt less like a compliment and more like a polite shield strangers used to insulate themselves from our catastrophic ruin.

My seventeen-year-old stepson, Eli, stood at my right side. His posture was unnaturally rigid inside the dark tailored blazer his father, Julian, had helped him choose for his high school debrief only six months prior. Eli was over six feet tall, easily towering over most of the neighbors standing on the gravel path, yet in the pale Pacific light, he looked younger and more fragile than I had ever seen him. Teresa, just fifteen, kept her hand tucked deep inside my coat pocket, clutching a linen rag Julian used to wipe brass filings off his workbench. She had refused to bring paper tissues, telling me quiet logic matters when your world collapses.
