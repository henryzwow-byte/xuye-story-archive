The Red Tag at Vance Orchard

When architectural conservator Evelyn Vance returns from a project to find her timber barn workshop illegally gutted for a commercial venue, she uncovers forged land records and takes a decisive legal stand against her family.

Fictional story. All characters and events are purely fictional. Content involves family financial conflict and real estate property disputes.

Chapter 1: The Cedar Vault

For eight years, my sanctuary had been a mortise-and-tenon timber barn overlooking the wind-swept bluffs of Hood River, Oregon. I am an architectural conservator, specializing in restoring late nineteenth-century Pacific Northwest millwork and heavy timber joinery. The barn was my private workshop. It was built from clear-grain Douglas fir in 1912 by my grandfather, perched on a ten-acre orchard lot that had been deeded solely to me when I turned twenty-five. Inside, the air was dense with the scent of aged cedar shavings, tung oil, and dry autumn earth. Centered under the high timber rafters sat my crowning pride: a twenty-foot workbench hand-planed from a single slab of reclaimed white oak, anchored to the foundation with forged iron brackets.

My family, however, never viewed the workshop as a place of craftsmanship. To my stepmother, Nora, it was an oversized storage shed that I stubbornly refused to turn into an income asset. To my older half-brother, Julian—a charismatic, perpetually debt-ridden boutique event planner—it was a wasted venue sitting idle on prime real estate overlooking the river gorge. For years, I had maintained a cautious distance from their financial entanglements. I paid my own property taxes, financed my own timber equipment, and earned a quiet, comfortable living restoring historic library interiors across the West Coast. When Julian crashed through his second commercial catering enterprise in three years, I offered sympathy and loaned him three thousand dollars for his daughter's school tuition, explicitly refusing to co-sign his loans.
