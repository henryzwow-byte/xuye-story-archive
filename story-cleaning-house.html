FICTIONAL STORY · A-003

Cleaning House

A young heir pushed to the edge of his own family waits three years to reveal the ledger that proves who stole his inheritance and dismantled his father’s company.

Fiction. Contains guardianship abuse, coercion and family conflict.

Chapter One: The Empty Chair

I stood beside the dining table while my uncle deliberately kept the final chair empty, making it clear that I was not welcome in my own family home. Above me, his family of three drank the wine my father had loved. Tonight it was their victory bottle, opened to celebrate removing me from my own inheritance.

“Call me Dad,” my uncle said with a cold smile. “Do that and perhaps I will let you remain at this table. Your father is no longer here to protect your place.”

I stared back without tears. Since the court placed me in their care, I had been treated like an unwanted guest. My aunt cut her food with perfect elegance and never once looked at me.

“When your mother left, you became our responsibility,” she said. “And those assets your father left? We consider them payment for everything we have done.”

The empty chair had belonged to my father. My uncle kept it visible because every meal was meant to teach me that memory, property and belonging were privileges he could ration. I said nothing, but I recorded the date of the conversation in a small notebook. It joined bank notices, board minutes and copies of letters my uncle believed had disappeared.
