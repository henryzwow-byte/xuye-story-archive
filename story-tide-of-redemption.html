Tide of Redemption

When Port Director Arthur Sterling tries to humiliate an unranked technician on a stormy harbor pier, he triggers an audit that unearths a suppressed disaster report and clears an innocent engineer's name.

Fictional story. Contains themes of workplace intimidation, administrative corruption, structural disaster investigation, and systemic justice.

Chapter 1: Salt and Iron

The rain over Blackwood Bay did not fall so much as cling, wrapping the concrete piers of the Oregon freight terminal in a cold, grey haze. Silver drops trickled down the yellow gantry cranes, pooling around piles of rusted iron chain and heavy timber pallets. Diesel fumes from idling tugboats drifted low across the water, mixing with the sharp scent of wet asphalt and brine.

Director Arthur Sterling liked mornings that felt heavy. It gave him an excuse to walk fast and keep his subordinates trailing half a pace behind. He moved along Pier 4 with four senior port executives and two chief structural inspectors. His boots were polished Italian leather, entirely unsuited for grease-stained docks, but Sterling did not dress for docks; he dressed for boardrooms that happened to sit beside salt water.
