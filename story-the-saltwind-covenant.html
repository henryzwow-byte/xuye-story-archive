The Saltwind Covenant

After her mother passes away in a quiet Maine fishing town, Nora Vance discovers a hidden maritime escrow certificate that exposes a corporate titan's past betrayal—and her mother's lifelong plan for justice.

Fictional story. Contains themes of parental loss, past corporate wrongdoing, and emotional strain.

Chapter 1: The Brass Compass in the Wall

The morning after the funeral, the damp salt fog of Alder Bay hung thick against the windowpanes of my mother’s saltbox cottage. The smell of cold hearth ash, lemon varnish, and dry cedar filled the rooms—fragrances that had defined Clara Vance for as long as I could remember. Outside, the tide crashed rhythmically against the granite footings of the old timber piers, a steady heartbeat that had accompanied every quiet year of our lives.

I wasn't searching for secrets when I brought a toolbox into the workshop behind the kitchen. I only wanted to fix the sticky brass latch on her workbench, where she had spent forty years repairing ship chronometers and sorting coastal freight receipts for local fishermen. My mother had lived with a rigorous, almost desperate thrift. She saved tallow candles, patched her oilskin coats with heavy sail thread, and carefully measured oil for the stove by the tablespoon. When her hands grew too unsteady from arthritis to handle delicate clockwork springs, her small pension barely covered kerosene, bread, and the winter coal bill.
