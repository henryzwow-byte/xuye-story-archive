Bound by Shadow Lines

A timber restoration consultant facing emergency eye surgery discovers her sister forged her signature on a massive commercial guarantee. Using wilderness GPS telemetry as an unassailable alibi, she reclaims her life, financial sovereignty, and self-worth.

Fictional story. Contains themes of familial financial breach, emergency medical procedures, and legal disputes. All characters and events are fictional.

Chapter 1: The Pressure in the Grain

For ten years, my life was measured in grain lines, moisture percentages, and structural load calculations. As a historic timber restoration consultant based in Portland, Oregon, I evaluated Douglas-fir trusses and old-growth cedar beams that had stood for a century. I knew how wood held weight under quiet tension. I also believed, with an unexamined devotion, that supporting family meant holding up whatever load was placed upon you without complaining about the creaks in your own frame. That belief cost me my savings, my peace of mind, and ultimately the safety of my own health.

Every month for two years, $1,500 left my business operating account to back an artisan lumber credit authorization for my older sister, Diana Thorne. Diana ran a boutique interior staging firm in Seattle, catering to luxury high-rise developments along Puget Sound. She possessed an immaculate sense of presentation—sharp silk blouses, minimalist studio lighting, and a voice that softened into helpless vulnerability right before she requested a financial favor. She had convinced our parents that her firm was on the verge of international prestige, needing only temporary capital bridging. I convinced myself that helping her build her dream was an act of sisterly duty.
