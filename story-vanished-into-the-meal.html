FICTIONAL STORY · A-060

Vanished Into the Meal

A feared sergeant rules the base dining hall through humiliation and threats, certain that every witness will keep looking down. A badge, preserved files and a camera hidden in plain sight give the soldiers living under his shadow a way to make the room speak.

Fiction. Contains military hazing, physical assault, threats, abuse of authority, workplace intimidation and disciplinary proceedings.

Chapter One: King of the Dining Hall

“As long as I think about it, I have plenty of ways to make you 'disappear' in the food without even realizing it, without even leaving a trace of oil stains.”

Sergeant Major Robert Jensen nudged Private Lucas Reed’s cheek viciously, then gave the young soldier a shove that knocked the young soldier’s back straight against the stainless steel plate counter. A tray crashed to the ground.

The mashed potatoes left a trail on the polished terrazzo floor. A plastic cup rolled under a table. The whole cafeteria was silent. Not quiet. It’s dead silence. A dangerous silence. The kind of dead silence where everyone has a clear view of the scene just now, but tacitly believes that the only way to survive is to pretend to be blind.

Jensen stood in the center of the canteen at the United Medien Base, as if even the air flowing inside was his private property. Forty-eight years old. Broad shoulders. A neat crew cut. A chest full of medals.

A weather-beaten face that says “must obey absolutely” written all over it.

In this base, he is the kind of senior sergeant major who can make recruits tremble before they even remember their names. And here, no one doesn’t know his name. Private Reed straightened up in panic.

“Yes, Sergeant Major.”

His voice was shaking badly. Jensen smiled. Not out of joy. It’s because he enjoys seeing fear do its standard conditioned reflexes.

“You think wasting food is fun?”

“No, Master Sergeant.”

“You think this is your mother’s kitchen?”

“No, Master Sergeant.”

“Do you think I don’t know who is tipping off the Inspector General privately?”

The last sentence caused the temperature of the entire cafeteria to suddenly drop to freezing point. Reed’s eyes flickered to the side. Just half a second. But that’s enough. Jensen approached step by step.

“Be careful, kid. In this base, those who can’t control their mouths will eventually be assigned to a place where no one can hear their complaints.”

At the other end of the cafeteria, a woman in a gray hoodie sat alone at a corner table. There was a paper coffee cup in front of her. Untouched. Half-bitten sandwich. A base visitor’s card tucked crookedly in his pocket.

Her hair was tucked under a navy blue baseball cap. She looked ordinary. There is no sense of existence. Almost invisible among the swarms of military uniforms. This is her purpose. Lieutenant Maya Delaney stared at Jensen silently, not moving.

Her right hand rests near the zipper of her hoodie, where a camera no bigger than a coin is hidden, streaming live video, audio and biometric timestamps to an encrypted server. She had been sitting there for forty-three minutes.

She witnessed Jensen humiliating two cooks, threatening a corporal over a seat problem, and forcing a limping specialist to stand at attention with a tray in both hands until the soup was completely cold. But this time is different.

Behind the threat to Reed just now was a specific target of the report—the Inspector General. Maya’s eyes dropped slightly and fell on the phone screen.

The live broadcast of Operation “Safe Haven” is ongoing.

NCIS (Naval Criminal Investigative Service) agent Malik Hayes is listening in a van behind the maintenance department. Captain Elena Ross and two investigators stood guard outside the north entrance. They had expected to be intimidated.

But they never expected that Jensen would take the initiative to provide such a clean handle before lunch. Jensen stepped back and stared down at Reid.

“Clean up.”

“Yes, Sergeant Major.”

Reed knelt down to pick up the fallen tray. His hands were shaking so much that he couldn’t hold the napkin steady. No one helped him. It’s not because everyone doesn’t want to help. But because everyone knows that Jensen will not even let the witnesses go.

Maya stood up. The movement is slow. The chair legs scraped against the floor tiles. Jensen turned his head. His eyes fell on her hoodie. Her casual clothes. Her calm face. A beast planning how to drive uninvited guests away from its territory.

“What are you looking at?” he asked sharply.

Maya picked up the paper coffee cup.

“That’s enough.”

Only two words. The voice is very soft. But very clear. The entire cafeteria seemed to have stopped breathing for an instant. Jensen stared at her. Then laugh out loud. It was a low, ugly laugh meant to remind the room that they could laugh too now.

Several soldiers smiled nervously. Most people didn’t laugh. Jensen strode towards Maya. A heavy step. A deliberate sense of oppression. Full of performative provocation. He stopped at her table and looked down at her badge.

“Civilian Support Contractor?”

Maya said nothing.

“Lost, sweetheart?”

Reed, still kneeling on the ground, looked up in horror. Maya could even see that he was silently begging her not to make him the next target. Jensen leaned forward.

“This is a military canteen, not a place for coffee for those who are weak and full of nonsense.”

Maya glanced at his hands. Another look at his face.

“You threatened that soldier.”

The smile disappeared from Jensen’s face.

“I’m correcting him.”

“You pushed him.”

“He tripped over himself.”

“You tell him and he will disappear into thin air.”

Jensen’s jaw tightened.

“That sentence probably sounds pretty impressive in your head before you say it.”

Then, as he passed her, he deliberately bumped her hard with his shoulder. The force was so strong that the coffee spilled out of the cup. It was so big that a hidden camera clearly recorded the moment of impact.

It was so big that the three soldiers around him shrank subconsciously. Maya didn’t move.

She just turned her head and said lightly: “That’s enough, Sergeant Major.”

Jensen stopped. slowly. He turned around.

“You have no idea who I am.”

For the first time, Maya looked directly at him without any disguise, no more pretense in her eyes.

“No,” she said, “I know.”

The door to the cafeteria opened in response. Two men and one woman came in wearing casual clothes. The ID he showed was only long enough for the nearest tables to see it clearly. NCIS.

Jensen’s phone vibrated in his pocket. For a moment. Two times. Three times. He didn’t reach for it. His eyes slowly moved down and fell on Maya’s hoodie. Falling on the miniature lens next to the gap.

The arrogance peeled off his face bit by bit. Maya reached into her pocket, took out the visitor’s pass, and pulled it off. What was exposed underneath was a military ID card. Lieutenant Maya Delaney.

Naval Criminal Investigative Service Mission Liaison Officer. Jensen’s mouth dropped open. But he couldn’t make any sound. Maya pulled out a tissue and wiped the coffee stains from her hands.

Then he said softly: “You’d better find a lawyer.”
