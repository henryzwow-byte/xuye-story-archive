Salvage and Strain

Working in his family's historic coastal shipyard, craftsman Leo Vance has spent years bearing the blame for equipment failures engineered by his domineering brother. When a catastrophic winch failure reveals a deeper web of forged safety logs and structural fraud, Leo must choose between family silence and standing up for the truth.

Fictional story. Depicts workplace psychological pressure, family conflict, and non-fatal mechanical hazards.

Chapter 1: The Splintered Beam

The tension inside the timber yard did not begin with the sharp crack of the steel cable; it had been settling into the cedar dust for years. Cold October rain lashed against the corrugated iron roof of Vance & Sons Shipyard, sending rhythmic drips down into the rusted oil pans along the bay floor. In the center of the main shop, a three-ton beam of reclaimed white oak hung suspended above the saw pit, swaying gently in the damp draft from Penobscot Bay.

My brother, Gareth, stood on the elevated catwalk with his arms crossed over his oilskin jacket, his voice booming over the low hum of the diesel generator. At thirty-three, Gareth carried himself like a man who owned not just the shipyard, but the tide that rose beneath it. He had spent his entire adulthood cultivating an aura of ruthless authority, collecting praise from municipal inspectors and wealthy yacht owners while ensuring everyone remembered that Vance & Sons stayed afloat solely because of his leadership.
