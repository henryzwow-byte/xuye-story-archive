The Brass Sounder

Returning home after six months at sea, harbor mechanic Caleb Vance finds his wife exiled to a damp coastal shack and blamed for a harbor disaster. To clear her name, he must unearth tampered telemetry records hidden deep within the bay's historic foghorn tower.

Fictional story. Contains mild themes of family conflict, financial deception, and coastal storm hazard.

Chapter 1: The Fog over Blackwood Bay

The sea fog rolled off the Gulf of Maine in thick wool-gray waves, muffling the acoustic horn at Point Sentinel until it sounded like a dying beast trapped under the breakwater. Caleb Vance adjusted the heavy canvas strap of his sea bag, his steel-toed boots crunching against the wet gravel of the harbor road. Six months on a deep-sea cable laying vessel off the Grand Banks had accustomed him to freezing spray and diesel fumes, but the sharp tang of woodsmoke and rotting sea kelp anchored him back in Harbor Creek. He walked past the silent shipyard, where the rusted iron hulls of trawlers loomed like ribcages in the mist. He expected to see welcoming lights in the high gables of the Vance family residence on the bluff—the house built by his grandfather when their salvage company dominated the county shoreline.

Instead, the front windows were dark, and heavy iron padlocks he had never seen before secured the storm doors of the main workshop. Caleb climbed the granite porch steps, his knuckles knocking hard against the oak door. After a long minute, a latch slid back, and the door opened just a few inches. His cousin Arthur Cole stood in the frame, wearing a crisp wool sweater and holding a silver fountain pen. Arthur did not step aside. His eyes met Caleb’s with a polished, unyielding coldness that belonged in a corporate board room rather than a family hallway.
