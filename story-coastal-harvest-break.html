FICTIONAL STORY · A-309

The Unbroken Light

When a severe coastal storm hits her family’s heirloom apple farm, Clara Vance is left injured to secure the harvest alone while her husband pursues luxury real estate deals in Portland. When he returns demanding she mortgage her inheritance to cover his bad debts, Clara draws on family support and meticulous documentation to reclaim her independence.

Fictional story. Contains depictions of emotional distress, financial manipulation, and marital separation.

Chapter 1: The Cold Tide

Five miles down the rocky coast of Coldwater Cove, the sea had a way of revealing what was already broken long before the storm hit. The October gale arrived at dusk, driving salt spray against the high glass windows of High Point Farm, the coastal cider press and heirloom apple orchard my grandfather had built sixty years ago. I stood in the dark holding a battery lantern, my right ankle tightly bandaged after taking a hard twist while trying to latch the heavy timber doors of the pressing room. Water was pouring through a loose seam in the roof, splashing dangerously close to our newly harvested barrels of winter cider.

Across the yard, the main house was empty. Julian had left four hours earlier for Portland, wearing his tailored slate-gray suit and smelling of sandalwood cologne. He was attending a real estate launch party hosted by regional luxury developers—investors he had been chasing for months in his bid to turn our quiet agricultural parcel into a boutique coastal resort. When the coastal weather advisory was broadcast at noon, warning of sixty-mile-per-hour gusts and localized flooding, I had asked him to stay and help reinforce the eastern barn doors.
