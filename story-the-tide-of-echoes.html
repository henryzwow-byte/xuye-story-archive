The Tide of Echoes

A quiet coastal dispatcher with a scarred past takes remote command of a deep-sea salvage operation during a catastrophic storm, uncovering the corporate cover-up that derailed her career.

Fictional story. Depicts maritime peril, atmospheric storm conditions, and workplace conflicts. Contains no graphic violence or real-world instructions.

Chapter 1: The Quiet Wire

By seven o'clock on Thursday evening, the signal tracking room at Blackwood Maritime Station on the outer jagged rim of Penobscot Bay had stopped feeling like an automated watch house and begun to resemble a storm bunker. The November nor'easter pounded against the reinforced glass windows until the iron frames groaned. Dark rain tore across the cliffs, blurring the revolving red beam of the harbor lighthouse into a smear of crimson light. Inside, the air smelled of ozone, damp wool coats, and stale espresso from the breakroom pot.

Hanna Vance sat at console four, her left hand moving over the weather logging keys while her right arm remained resting stiffly on her lap. Four years of working the junior night shift had taught her to keep her head down and her entries flawless. She recorded swell heights, barometric trends, and commercial radio check-ins with deliberate precision. To the twelve operators in the room, she was simply Vance: the soft-spoken logistics clerk who took the graveyard shifts nobody else wanted and never complained about the cold draft leaking through the eastern wall.
