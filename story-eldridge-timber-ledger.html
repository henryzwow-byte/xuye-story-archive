FICTIONAL STORY · A-263

The Cedar and the Ledger

After two years of silently bankrolling the historic restoration of a Puget Sound maritime heritage center for her grandniece's debut exhibition, Evelyn Mercer is denied entry at the opening gala by her ambitious nephew. Armed with notarized deeds and audited timber ledgers, Evelyn demands accountability while protecting the true legacy of her craft.

Fictional story. Contains themes of familial exclusion, financial deceit, and emotional conflict.

Chapter 1: The Lanterns on the Sound

The salt air off Puget Sound always carried the scent of wet red cedar, but on the evening of the autumn solstice, it smelled of varnish, fresh timber, and rain. From the floor-to-ceiling windows of her loft above the harbor, Evelyn Mercer watched the yellow beam of the lighthouse sweep across the dark water. Down on the rocky shoreline stood the Eldridge Heritage Center, its copper roof gleaming under the newly installed exterior lanterns. For two years, the historic nineteenth-century boatyard had been little more than a collapsing timber ruin filled with dry rot and forgotten maritime blueprints. Tonight, it was opening its doors as the town’s premier coastal museum and historical archive.

Evelyn smoothed the lapels of her dark teal velvet blazer and pinned her late husband’s gold compass brooch near her collar. At seventy-two, her hands bore the faint calluses of a woman who had spent forty years working as a restoration archivist and master timber joiner. She had not built the museum single-handedly, but she had saved it. When the local historical society faced bankruptcy and the city threatened to demolish the structure for a luxury condo development, Evelyn stepped into the gap. She quietly liquidated her commercial real estate holdings, covered three years of back property taxes, and bankrolled the complete structural overhaul.
