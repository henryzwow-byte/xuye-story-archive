The Copperstone Boundary

When cartographer Clara Vance arrives in Copperstone, Colorado, she finds her employer imprisoned and accused of destroying the local timber dam. Armed with a hidden brass transit telescope and high-station telegraph logs, she uncovers a corrupt magistrate's illegal aquifer diversion and fights to restore the truth.

Fictional story. Contains themes of regional corruption, false imprisonment, and resource disputes.

Chapter 1: The Mountain Stage

The six-horse stagecoach rattled to a halt in the high mountain dust of Copperstone, Colorado, its iron axles groaning against the incline. Clara Vance unlatched the heavy wooden door, her boots meeting pine needles and sun-baked granite. The crisp mountain air carried the resinous scent of raw timber, coal grease from the mill boilers, and the sharp bite of impending autumn frost. In her lap sat a leather cylinder containing a territorial surveying license and a signed land-audit contract from Denver.

She had traveled three hundred miles from Philadelphia to meet Julian Hayes, an independent cartographer who had commissioned her firm to map the upper basin’s timber boundaries. His letters had described a thriving valley community built on honest logging, clear water rights, and shared timber trusts. Clara needed this employment to satisfy her late father’s estate debts, but more than that, she relied on the certainty of topographical numbers—lines on vellum that could not be twisted by smooth-tongued speculators.
