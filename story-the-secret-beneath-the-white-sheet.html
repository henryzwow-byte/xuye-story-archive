The Secret Beneath the White Sheet

When Alexander lifts the hospital sheet covering his pregnant wife, he uncovers a campaign of abuse, forged medical evidence and a five-billion-dollar family trust plot.

Fiction. Contains domestic abuse, injuries to a pregnant woman, unlawful confinement, coercion, drugging, medical fraud and family trauma.

Chapter One: The White Sheet and the Mask

When Alexander tore the white sheet from the hospital bed, he finally saw what his mother had called “an accidental fall.”

His wife, six months pregnant, had deep purple marks across both legs from repeated blows with a strap and a blunt object. Some bruises had already yellowed; others were still bleeding. Parallel welts crossed the sides of her thighs, and distinct leather restraint marks remained on her wrists.

On the other side of the bed, Beatrice stood in a Chanel couture suit, holding a cup of Earl Grey tea with perfect composure.

“Alexander,” Beatrice said, blowing lightly across the tea, “don’t be fooled by her performance. She was walking down the stairs in velvet slippers and simply slipped.”

Two hours earlier, Alexander Sterling had been in Boston signing a merger agreement. Although he came from an old-money family, he had built his venture-capital empire without taking a dollar from the Sterling trust. Emma, a young maid at the estate, risked everything to call 911. Hoping to define the incident before the hospital could discover the truth, Beatrice called her son first and tried to seize control of the story.

Alexander took a private helicopter back to Westchester County, New York, and found this scene waiting for him. Clara looked tiny inside the pale hospital gown. When Alexander approached, she recoiled on instinct. Once she recognized her husband, she gripped his sleeve and stared past his shoulder at Beatrice in terror.

“Clara,” Alexander said hoarsely, fighting to contain his anger, “tell me what happened.”

Clara clenched her teeth and pushed a small, cold object into Alexander’s palm with a trembling hand. It was an encrypted memory card from the local backup slot of the wine-cellar security camera.

“Emma gave it to me. She helped inspect the security system and had a spare key to the equipment room.” A tear traced Clara’s cheek, but her faint voice was perfectly clear. “If she couldn’t get out of the estate, she told me to make sure you received it. I didn’t sign the trust agreement. She wants our child.”
