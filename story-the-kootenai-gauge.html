The Kootenai Gauge

When newcomer Evelyn Mercer reports to an Idaho river dam depot wearing a master diver's acoustic endorsement, her supervisor suspects forged credentials. But an emergency spillway test and a regional audit reveal the heroic truth behind her sealed past.

Fictional story. Contains themes of occupational stress, underwater industrial hazards, and administrative safety audits.

Chapter 1: The Brass Stamp on the Harness

The autumn rain had washed the dust from the heavy spruce timber along the riverbank, leaving the cold mountain air smelling of pine pitch and wet rock. When Evelyn Mercer stepped out of her pickup truck at the Kootenai River Hydroelectric Depot, she carried her life in a worn canvas gear bag. She was twenty-eight years old, dressed in standard canvas work trousers, oil-stained steel-toe boots, and a faded flannel jacket that offered little protection against the damp chill of northern Idaho. To the six-man maintenance crew unloading timber cribbing near the spillway gate, she looked like any other entry-level apprentice surveyor reporting for her first seasonal contract.

She walked past the heavy timber cribbing where the river thundered through the intake gates, her gait steady and deliberate. She checked in at the tool shed, where chief rigger Caleb Thorne handed her an inspection harness. As Evelyn adjusted the heavy shoulder strap, the morning sun cut through the cloud cover and caught a small brass stamp riveted to the leather above her left collarbone. It was an oval emblem, dark with oxidation, bearing an engraved anchor wrapped in turbine blades and a six-digit registration serial number.
