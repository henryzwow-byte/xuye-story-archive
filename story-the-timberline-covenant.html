FICTIONAL STORY · A-324

The Timberline Covenant

Clara Vance spent twenty-four years building her mountain pottery studio and heirloom apple grove. When her daughter-in-law attempts to convert the property into a lucrative event venue, Clara uses quiet documentation and a binding land covenant to reclaim her life's work.

Fictional story. Contains mild themes of family boundary disputes, property conflicts, and emotional estrangement.

Chapter 1: The Distant Smoke

At sixty-seven, I turned my dusty pickup truck off the highway into the narrow gravel pass that climbs toward Cedar Ridge Craftworks, expecting nothing more than the cold fragrance of damp pine needles and the quiet murmur of the river below. For twenty-four years, this mountain hollow had been my quiet fortress. I had cleared the thick brush with my own hands, planted three acres of heirloom dwarf apple trees, and built a timber-frame pottery studio using mortise-and-tenon joints that my late husband, Arthur, had spent an entire summer chiseling into shape.

As the truck rounded the final bend of blue spruce, however, the familiar solitude was shattered. The gravel turn-off was jammed with slick black SUVs, commercial delivery vans, and high-end sedans spilling onto the rain-softened shoulders. A white valet sign had been hammered directly into the mossy wooden border my husband had built out of mountain basalt.
