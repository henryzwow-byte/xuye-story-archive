The Iron Oak

When an arrogant heritage foundation publicly humbles a retired master shipwright, his daughter-in-law uncovers a decades-old timber trust that turns the tables.

Fictional story. Contains themes of family conflict, emotional distress, and corporate legal disputes.

Chapter 1: The Silver Chisel

The annual charity gala for Vance Heritage Maritime was held inside the glass conservatory overlooking Blackwood Bay. The air smelled of expensive lilies, salted breeze, and cured mahogany. Brass lanterns cast warm amber reflections across the polished parquet floor, where wealthy donors in evening wear mingled over champagne flutes. Standing near the central display case, Arthur Vance—my seventy-two-year-old father-in-law and a retired master shipwright—adjusted his worn tweed jacket. His hands, calloused from fifty years of shaping ship timber, trembled slightly as he leaned over the pedestal.

On that pedestal sat his life’s masterpiece: a meticulous, hand-carved scale replica of the 1928 schooner Northern Star, crafted with brass rivets and steam-bent oak. Beside it lay his father’s silver-plated carving chisel, a relic from the historic Harbor Pointe drydock. Arthur had spent three months restoring the piece specifically for the foundation’s heritage exhibition.
