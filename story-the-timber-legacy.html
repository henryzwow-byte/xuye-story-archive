The Timber Legacy

When Nora discovers her son Julian secretly laboring at night to pay an arbitrary family fee, she unearths years of hidden exploitation and reclaims what was rightfully his.

Fictional story. Depicts themes of familial financial exploitation, psychological pressure, and boundary enforcement. All characters and events are entirely fictional.

Chapter 1: The Brass Gear

The autumn mist had settled low across the Hood River valley, wrapping the Douglas fir ridges in a heavy, cold shroud. Inside our garage workshop, the sharp smell of cedar shavings usually carried a familiar warmth, but tonight the air felt brittle. My fourteen-year-old son, Julian, was sitting at his workbench, his back unusually rigid as he scraped a fine steel file over a miniature brass gear. For months, Julian had spent his after-school hours crafting intricate mechanical wooden clocks and music boxes, a delicate trade he had inherited from my late father. But over the past three weeks, his steady rhythm had fractured. He had stopped coming upstairs for dinner on time, his eyes bore dark circles, and his hands—normally meticulous and carefully scrubbed—were constantly stained with dark, foul-smelling marine grease.

I stood in the doorway for a long moment, watching the nervous tension in his small shoulders. When he dropped the file, it clattered against the iron vise, making him flinch as though he had been caught doing something forbidden. 'Julian,' I said softly, stepping into the yellow light of the workbench lamp. 'You've been skipping meals, and your hands are raw. What is happening?'
