The Saltwood Archive

Following the death of his beloved wife Clara, retired boatbuilder Arthur discovers that his ambitious relatives are angling to claim his coastal workshop under the guise of elder care—unaware that Clara left behind an airtight legacy of her own.

Fictional story. Contains mature themes of grief, family conflict, financial deception, and bereavement.

Chapter 1: The Fog over Heron Cove

The tide in Heron Cove never asked for permission before it took something away. For forty-two years, Arthur Vance had measured his life by the steady rhythm of cedar shavings falling onto his workshop floor and the soft, deliberate footsteps of his wife, Clara, coming down the gravel path with tea. Now, the workshop smelled only of dried varnish and damp tide-flats. Clara had been buried three days prior under the weeping white pines at St. Jude’s Cemetery, leaving behind a house that felt too vast and a silence that settled into the floorboards like frost.

At the reception in the old town hall, neighbors had brought hot casseroles and pressed Arthur’s weathered hands with hushed condolences. But Arthur had spent the afternoon watching his nephew, Derek, and Derek’s wife, Vanessa. They had arrived late in a sleek grey sedan that looked out of place among the battered pickup trucks of the cove. Derek had barely looked at Clara’s portrait by the memorial book. Instead, his eyes wandered across the exposed ceiling beams, the wide plank floors, and the floor-to-ceiling windows looking out over the harbor.
