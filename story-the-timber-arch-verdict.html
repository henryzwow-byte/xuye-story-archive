The Timber Arch Verdict

Framed for structural failure on a flagship coastal pavilion, Nora Vance spent five quiet years analyzing raw stress telemetry. When her former partners attempt to coerce her into patching their dangerous flaws, she delivers a crushing audit at an architectural gala.

Fictional story. Contains themes of professional betrayal, corporate coercion, and legal disputes.

Chapter 1: The Fracture at Sentinel Point

I still remember the precise creak of heavy Douglas fir under forty tons of hydraulic tension at the Sentinel Point shipyard. It was not the heroic sound people associate with massive wooden architecture. It was a high-pitched, metallic groan that resonated through the concrete foundation while rain lashed against the corrugated glass walls. The storm outside was howling off the Oregon coast, throwing salt spray against the harbor windows.

Five years ago, I had spent eighteen months calculating every stress vector for the Pacific Maritime Pavilion, an ambitious cross-laminated timber arch designed to withstand coastal earthquakes. I lived on black coffee, cold salt air, and calculation spreadsheets. My late uncle had built Vance Structural Engineering on honesty and craftsmanship, but after his death, his senior partner, Richard Vance, took the helm. Beside him was my lead associate, Julian Thorne—the polished, charismatic designer who could charm corporate boards while barely knowing how to calculate bending moments.
