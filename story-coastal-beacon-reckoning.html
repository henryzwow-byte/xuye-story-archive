Coastal Beacon Reckoning

A meticulous archivist at a historic Pacific Northwest boatyard survives a fall from a gantry catwalk. Labeled as clumsy and unstable by her influential relatives, she uncovers automated marine telemetry and load-cell sensor logs that expose deliberate sabotage and systematic fraud.

Fictional story. Contains depictions of family emotional manipulation, severe physical injury from a workplace fall, structural tampering, and legal accountability. All characters, organizations, and events are entirely fictional.

Chapter 1: The Timber Loft

High above the grey waters of Puget Sound, the Vance Heritage Shipyard smelled of cured yellow cedar, salt spray, and decades of boiled linseed oil. For thirty-two-year-old Clara Vance, the timber loft was the only place where numbers and measurements had fixed meanings. A ten-inch beam was ten inches; a dovetail joint either held its weight or collapsed under stress. In the rest of her family’s world, reality was whatever her uncle Donald decreed at Sunday dinner, and truth was whatever her cousin Marcus spun into a joke.

Marcus Vance was thirty-four, charismatic, and groomed to inherit the family’s maritime restoration business. He had a booming laugh that filled boardrooms and a habit of standing just close enough to invade Clara’s personal space without technically crossing a physical boundary. To the port authority board and wealthy historical foundation donors, Marcus was a visionary preservationist. To Clara, he was the boy who had spent twenty years sabotaging her work tools, whispering threats during family gatherings, and claiming her structural blueprints as his own.
