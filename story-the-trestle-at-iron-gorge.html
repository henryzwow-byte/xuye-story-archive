The Trestle at Iron Gorge

When a runaway caboose becomes trapped on a failing mountain trestle, a solitary signal inspector and his estranged niece fight a rainstorm and a corrupt timber foreman to execute a daring rescue.

Fictional story. Contains depictions of structural danger, high-altitude rescue, and rail machinery risks.

Chapter 1: The Lantern on the Switch Line

The autumn deluge had turned the steep clay cuts of Iron Gorge into rivers of rust-colored slurry. For three days, rain had beaten against the heavy cedar shingles of the switch station, filling the valley with the roar of the swollen Rogue River three hundred feet below. Julian Vance stood at the workbench of the signal house, the steady tick of a disassembled brass chronometer buried under the pounding rhythm of water on glass. He adjusted his magnifying loupe, his calloused fingers placing a hairspring with the quiet precision of a man who measured his life by fractions of a second. At forty-two, Julian was the line inspector for the timber rail spur, a solitary figure whose duty was to keep the signals clear and the mechanical switches functional across sixteen miles of treacherous mountain track.

The door latched open with a wet thud, admitting a gust of cold mountain air and a girl wrapped in an oversized oilskin coat. Hester stood on the threshold, carrying a battered leather valise held together by twine. At sixteen, she possessed her late father’s sharp jawline and watchful, unblinking eyes. A boiler explosion at the upper logging camp two weeks prior had left her without family or home, prompting the rail company to send her down-line on the evening supply handcar to her estranged uncle’s door. Julian set down his tweezers and looked up, the silence between them weighted by years of unwritten letters and unspoken regret.
