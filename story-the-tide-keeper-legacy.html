The Tide Keeper's Legacy

A maritime surveyor investigates his late grandfather's secretive coastal holdings, unlocking a forty-year-old hydrographic mystery that reframes his family's bitter estate battle.

Fictional story. Contains depictions of family estate disputes, historical maritime accidents, and environmental hazards.

Chapter 1: Salt and Cedar

The fog over Cape Cypress didn't rise; it simply rested against the damp Douglas firs and turned the salt water of the bay into cold lead. I stood on the rotting cedar planks of the tide-station pier while cold spray misted my wool coat, listening to the low, rhythmic groan of the harbor bell. My grandfather, Captain Harrison Vance, had been buried two hours earlier in the mariners' plot on the hill, but down here on the waterline, his presence still hovered in the smell of pine pitch, creosote, and iron rust.

For over forty years, Harrison had operated the Cape Cypress tide watchtower, recording water depths, barometric trends, and shoreline shifts while the rest of the county transitioned to automated satellite radar. To the townspeople, he was an eccentric relic of the timber boom. To my family, he was a stubborn old man sitting on forty acres of prime Pacific Northwest coastal shelf. Aunt Nora stood near the boat ramp with her black umbrella tilted against the wind, her leather boots carefully avoiding the slick kelp. Beside her, my cousin Marcus was already pointing at a digital land survey on his tablet, talking in low, urgent tones to a commercial real estate assessor.
