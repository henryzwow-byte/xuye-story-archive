The Fractured Lead

A young glass artisan in Seattle protects her aging mentor from an aggressive director, losing her job only to expose a high-stakes historical restoration scam.

Fictional story. Contains depictions of workplace confrontation, intimidation, and corporate fraud.

Chapter 1: The Fractured Lead

The morning rain over Seattle’s industrial waterfront always smelled of salt, creosote, and cold iron. Inside Puget Sound Restoration Works, the air was even colder, thick with linseed oil, polishing rouge, and the sharp metallic tang of melted lead solder. Clara Vance sat at the long pine workbench, her fingers stained dark as she carefully cleaned the outer glass border tiles of a nineteenth-century cathedral window panel. Beside her stood Arthur Pendelton, his seventy-year-old hands moving with quiet, incredible precision as he re-leaded a shattered sapphire pane. Arthur was the last master conservator in the Pacific Northwest who could still determine the exact origin of historic mouth-blown glass merely by its ring against a wooden mallet.

At quarter past eight, heavy leather boots rattled down the iron staircase leading from the executive offices above. Julian Thorne, the studio’s managing director, stepped onto the concrete floor with a tailored charcoal coat slung over his arm and a digital tablet clamped in his grip. Julian had assumed control of the workshop through marriage two years prior, rapidly transforming a world-renowned preservation laboratory into a high-speed commercial assembly line.
