FICTIONAL STORY · A-388

The Coldwater Ridge Ledger

When timber inspector Harlan Miller attends his late uncle’s mill auction in the Cascade Mountains, an unexpected clue leads him to a remote fire lookout, uncovering a vast watershed conspiracy.

Fictional story. Contains themes of family estrangement, corporate fraud, and psychological suspense.

Chapter 1: The Auction at Mill Nine

The heavy smell of damp cedar, machine grease, and diesel oil still hung thick over the lower yard of Mill Nine, even after the auctioneer’s iron gavel began to fall. Coldwater Ridge was a rugged mountain town built entirely on old timber and bitter winters, tucked deep into the western foothills of the Cascade Range. For three grueling weeks, everyone in the valley had accepted as fact that my uncle, Vance Miller, had died when the sawmill’s central boiler room collapsed in a sudden midnight blaze. I stood quietly among thirty grim-faced buyers near the rusted head-rig saws, watching outside investors in heavy canvas work coats place bid after bid on flatbed trucks, crane cables, and industrial planners.

Vance had taken me in when my parents died in a highway storm, raising me in his timber house and teaching me how to read growth rings, assess grain density, and respect the ancient forests. He was a silver-haired, soft-spoken man who logged every single board foot of lumber in meticulous hand-bound leather ledgers and never left an equipment gate unbolted. Watching his sixty years of honest labor auctioned off for pennies on the dollar felt like being forced to attend a second, far more painful funeral.
