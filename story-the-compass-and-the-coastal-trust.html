The Compass and the Coastal Trust

After being turned away from the inaugurating gala of a coastal observatory she funded, an archival conservator uncovers her son's role in a fraudulent structural violation scheme—and uses undeniable paper trails to enforce accountability.

Fictional story. Contains themes of family emotional conflict, financial boundary setting, and legal accountability.

Chapter 1: The Glass Horizon

The autumn wind off the Columbia River carried the sharp scent of tide pools and rain when Helen Vance adjusted the collar of her wool coat outside the Columbia Observatory. For thirty-four years, Helen had worked in quiet precision as a master parchment conservator, restoring nineteenth-century nautical charts in her workshop three miles down the coast. Tonight, however, she stood on the bluestone terrace as a guest of honor—or so she had believed when she purchased her ticket and packed her late father’s 1880 brass mariner’s chronometer into a cushioned leather case.

The Observatory was an architectural landmark, a historic timber-and-stone structure overlooking the roaring Pacific waves. Helen had spent three years establishing the initial preservation trust that saved the building from demolition, pouring her life savings and endless hours of grant writing into its foundation so her son, Julian, could realize his dream of converting the site into a world-class maritime archive and boutique research lodge.
