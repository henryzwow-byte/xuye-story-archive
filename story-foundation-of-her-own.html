FICTIONAL STORY · A-163

A Foundation of Her Own

Set against the rugged gorge country of the Pacific Northwest, an architectural consultant stands her ground against a domineering investor and an enabling partner during a high-stakes commercial project unveiling.

Fictional story. Contains themes of professional workplace conflict, emotional boundary setting, and commercial pressure.

Chapter 1: The Draft on the River

The high wind off the Columbia Gorge always smelled of pine pitch and wet iron. At thirty-two, Nora Vance had spent four years earning her reputation as the sharpest historic preservation consultant in the region, but standing on the half-finished terrace of Blackwood Mill, she felt the familiar, low-grade tension that had defined the past eighteen months.

Below her, the river churned gray against the granite piers. Beside her, Julian Sterling was adjusting his navy blazer, his gold signet ring catching the late evening light. Julian was thirty-five, smooth-spoken, and the principal partner of Sterling & Vance Architecture. He was also the man who had spent three years telling Nora that compromising on minor details was simply how business got done in commercial restoration.
