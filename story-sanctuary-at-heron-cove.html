FICTIONAL STORY · A-342

Sanctuary at Heron Cove

When Clara Miller’s son and his partner move into her Oregon coastal greenhouse cottage, what begins as temporary support shifts into a stealthy takeover of her life's work—until a hidden file reveals the truth.

Fictional story. Contains themes of family conflict, emotional boundaries, and property disputes.

Chapter 1: The Salt Air and the Draft

Rain off the Pacific hit the double-paned glass of the greenhouse studio with the relentless rhythm of a tide coming in fast. Inside, the room smelled of damp potting soil, wild rosemary, wet cedar shingles, and the bitter dark roast Clara had brewed twice that morning. The storm had stripped the last yellow leaves from the Sitka spruces outside, plastering them flat against the stone foundation. Clara Miller, sixty-one, held her watercolor brush suspended above a half-finished plate of coastal maidenhair fern, listening to the floorboards creak near the doorway.

Marcus stood under the timber archway wearing his crisp technical rain shell and polished leather boots. He had his silver fountain pen clipped to his collar, his shoulders angled into the effortless posture of a man who believed every room was waiting for his architectural critique. For seven weeks, since he and Clara’s son, Julian, had moved into the south guest wing after their Portland loft lease expired, Marcus had spoken with a soft, calculated cadence designed to sound like professional concern.
