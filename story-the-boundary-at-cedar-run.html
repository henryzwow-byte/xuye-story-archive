FICTIONAL STORY · A-235

The Boundary at Cedar Run

When Nora Sterling welcomed her daughter and son-in-law into her historic Maine timber workshop after her husband's passing, she believed they were preserving family heritage. But when subtle microaggressions escalate into a hidden commercial rezoning scheme, Nora relies on her engineer's eye to audit the truth and draw an unyielding line.

Fictional story. Contains depictions of family tension, quiet financial manipulation, and property disputes.

Chapter 1: The Cedar Dust

Nora Sterling blew a thin film of white pine shavings from the edge of her mortise chisel, the steel gleaming under the high clerestory windows of her coastal Maine workshop. At sixty-seven, her stance remained as solid as the load-bearing king posts she had spent thirty-five years inspecting as a chief structural preservation engineer. She knew how wood aged under stress. She knew how grain bent before it fractured, and how damp rot could hollow out a beam long before the surface showed a single crack.

For four decades, this timber-frame shop in Heron Bay had been her sanctuary. She and her late husband, Thomas, a master boatwright, had raised its heavy oak bents by hand in the spring of 1984. They had hammered the black locust pegs into place together, building a space that smelled permanently of cedar dust, boiled linseed oil, and the sharp brine of the tide flats. When Thomas passed away six years ago, Nora kept the shop alive, restoring historic covered bridges for the state historical commission and crafting custom white-oak timber joints for local shipbuilders.
