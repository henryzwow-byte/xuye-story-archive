Salt Water Proof

A shipwright daughter uncovers her brother's systematic campaign to sabotage her heritage trust stewardship, leading to a dramatic solstice confrontation solved by an unexpected witness.

Fictional story. Contains themes of sibling deception, family trust conflict, and emotional resolution.

Chapter 1: The Driftwood Ledger

The salt wind blowing across the Columbia River estuary carried the scent of cedar shavings, damp tide-flats, and fresh varnish. From the elevated deck of the Vance Marine Heritage Pavilion in Astoria, Oregon, the summer solstice twilight stretched like spun gold across the water. Below us, the forty-foot wooden schooner Albatross rested in her drydock cradle, her Douglas fir planking gleaming under three fresh coats of tung oil. I wiped my hands on my canvas apron, feeling the familiar, grounding texture of cured timber. For twelve years, I had managed the physical restoration of the fleet my parents founded, ensuring that every frame, knee, and transom met state maritime heritage standards. Yet as the first guests arrived for the trust's golden anniversary banquet, the air inside the boathouse felt heavier than a rain-soaked mainsail.

My younger brother, Julian, arrived precisely forty minutes early, wearing an unwrinkled cream linen jacket that belonged in a Portland real estate high-rise rather than a working shipyard. He didn't come to help set out the cedar tables or adjust the vintage brass lanterns. Instead, he glided straight across the apron toward my parents, dropping his designer sunglasses into his breast pocket with practiced grace. Before I could even hang up my apron, Julian had stepped toward my husband, Mark, resting a hand on Mark's shoulder with a sigh of soft, performance-grade sympathy. "You two are looking so worn down," Julian said, his voice carrying just enough carrying power to drift toward my mother, Clara, who was arranging coastal wildflowers nearby. "Hannah, are the oakum suppliers pressing you again? You really don't have to carry this burden alone if it's getting past your depth."
