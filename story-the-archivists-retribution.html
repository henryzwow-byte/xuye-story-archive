FICTIONAL STORY · A-220

The Archivist's Retribution

When forensic architectural conservator Evelyn Vance returns to Boston after years of hazardous restoration work in Europe, she discovers her uncle has stolen her mother's trust, erased her studio, and branded her incompetent. At a high-society museum gala, a legendary master engineer recognizes her unseen mark on the building's structural timber, shattering the family's carefully crafted lies.

Fictional story. Contains themes of family betrayal, financial manipulation, defamation, and high-stress professional conflict.

Chapter 1: The Erased Studio

The salt mist of Boston Harbor carried the damp chill of late October. I stood on the worn granite steps of Vance Architectural Conservation, my leather duffel slung over my right shoulder while my thumb rubbed the rigid ridge along my collarbone. It was a surgical scar from three months in the Highlands, where an eighteen-ton oak truss in a sixteenth-century cathedral vault had shifted during emergency stabilization. The steel pin beneath my skin still throbbed when humidity changed, but in Scotland’s icy mist, pain was simply another metric to log and ignore.

I had been back in America for under forty-eight hours. My flight from Edinburgh had been delayed twice, my body was locked into twelve-hour night shifts beneath rain-soaked scaffolding, and my eyes burned from staring at strain telemetry. Yet as I pushed open the mahogany door of our ancestral brownstone, I told myself exhaustion was exaggerating the silence inside. For five years, I had served as the firm’s senior forensic conservator in Europe, stabilizing fire-damaged monuments that lesser engineering teams refused to touch. This building was my anchor, the place where my late mother had taught me how to read the hidden language of centuries-old mortar and structural timber.
