Timber Line Reckoning

Married for two months in an old coastal harbor town, Hannah Vance discovers her husband and his father are quietly arranging to leverage her privately owned mountain cabin for family debts. Her quiet defiance reshapes her marriage and her freedom.

Fictional story. Contains depictions of family financial conflict, emotional tension, and marital boundary struggles.

Chapter 1: The Intrusion at Blackwood Cove

The smell of cedar shavings and salt air had always given Hannah Vance a sense of order. In her small workshop down by the docks of Blackwood Cove, Oregon, she spent her days restoring the hulls of classic wooden fishing boats. Wood responded to patience and exact measurement; it did not lie about where it had been weathered or where it was weak. For five years, that clarity had sustained her. But over the past two months, since marrying Marcus Sterling, the quiet boundaries of her life had begun to fray under the weight of expectations she had never agreed to carry.

The trouble had moved in three weeks ago, disguised as a temporary arrangement. Marcus’s father, Arthur Sterling, ran a struggling civil engineering firm in town. When the firm’s downtown office lease expired without renewal, Arthur did not look for a smaller commercial space. Instead, he backed a flatbed trailer into the driveway of the coastal craftsman home where Hannah and Marcus lived, unloading steel filing cabinets, blueprint racks, and heavy oak desks into the detached carriage house.
