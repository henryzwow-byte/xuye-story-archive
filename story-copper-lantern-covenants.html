FICTIONAL STORY · A-162

The Copper Lantern Covenants

When architectural restorer Beatrice Thorne is ejected from her son's grand opening gala, she triggers emergency trust covenants that halt a fraudulent fifteen-million-dollar loan and demand true accountability.

Fictional story. Contains themes of family estrangement, financial betrayal, and workplace disrespect.

Chapter 1: The Wet Gravel of Oakhaven

The coastal rain had turned the long gravel driveway of Oakhaven Manor into a dark, gleaming ribbon by seven o'clock. Beatrice Thorne stepped out of her ancient pickup truck, pulling the collar of her oilskin slicker tight against the November wind. Her hands, calloused and stained with linseed oil from forty years of master timberwork, carried a heavy rectangular wooden box. Inside lay a fully restored 1840s copper cornerstone lantern—the original fixture of Oakhaven’s riverfront conservatory, which she had spent three months hand-soldering in her Savannah workshop as a gift for her son’s grand reopening gala.

Through the tall arched windows, the manor glowed with warm chandelier light. Valets in crisp black uniforms shuttled silver luxury sedans under the portico. Beatrice wiped her worn work boots on the brass mat before stepping into the marble foyer. The air inside smelled of expensive white lilies, champagne, and fresh varnish. She spotted her son, Julian, standing near the grand staircase in a tailored velvet tuxedo, laughing with a group of regional real estate investors. Beside him stood his fiancée and corporate partner, Olivia Vance, radiant in a champagne silk gown.
