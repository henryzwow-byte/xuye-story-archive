The Deed Filed at Eleven Forty-Five

A grieving archivist in coastal Maine protects her inherited tide-mill from a coercive land grab by documenting the threat and conveying the property to a conservation trust while reserving her lifetime right to remain.

Fictional story. Contains themes of emotional coercion, marital disputes, and legal conflict.

Chapter 1: The Tide-Gauge Ledger

The saltwater fog of Havenport always smelled of crushed pine needles, damp granite, and the faint metallic tang of old brass. Clara Sterling stood inside the upper loft of the tide-mill, her linen apron stained with iron-gall ink as the ocean tide filled the lower granite sluice below her feet.

The building had belonged to her grandfather, Captain Silas Vance Sterling, a hydrographer who spent fifty years recording the ebb and flow of the Maine coastline. Silas had left her the timber mill, three acres of jagged cliffside spruce, and a massive brass tide-gauge that ticked like a grandfather clock whenever water rushed through the submerged wheelhouse. To Clara, it was not merely real estate; it was an anchor. Every notch carved into the oak beams represented a winter storm her family had survived together.
