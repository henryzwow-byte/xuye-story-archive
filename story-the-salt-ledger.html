The Salt Ledger

Following his sister Clara's death, retired shipwright Silas Vance faces an unannounced visit from his estranged niece and her developer fiancé seeking prime waterfront property. But a final surveyor's ledger reveals hidden liabilities and truths they never anticipated.

Fictional story. Contains themes of grief, family conflict, legal disputes, and financial deception.

Chapter 1: The Timber and the Tide

The smell of cured cedar and boiled linseed oil always stayed in the rafters of the workshop, even when the Pacific fog pressed flat against the bay windows. For forty years, Silas Vance had spent his mornings in this timber yard at the edge of Tillamook Sound, shaping fir knees and planing oak ribs for local fishing boats. But on this overcast Tuesday morning, four days after his older sister Clara was laid to rest in the hillside cemetery above town, the familiar rasp of his hand plane brought no comfort.

Clara had spent thirty-five years as the chief archivist and dock conservator for the sound's historic waterfront. She was eighty-two when her heart finally gave out in her small cottage overlooking the lower tide flats. She had lived simply, surrounded by leather-bound harbor logbooks, brass navigational instruments, and water-stained soundings that dated back to the logging schooners of the late nineteenth century. To the town, she was the quiet guardian of the shoreline; to Silas, she was the steady anchor of a family that had otherwise drifted apart.
