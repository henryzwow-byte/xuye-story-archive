The Craftsman's Verdict

After years of self-funded labor restoring historic timber structures in coastal Maine, Nora wins a prestigious municipal preservation grant—only for her estranged family to demand her contract to cover her brother's failed business debts.

Fictional story. Contains themes of familial conflict, emotional coercion, financial boundary disputes, and public reputation confrontations.

Chapter 1: The Sawdust and the Seal

The early morning air inside the Northport Harbor shop always smelled of wet salt, white pine shavings, and cold iron. Long before the gulls began crying over the fish houses, Nora Bennett would strike a match, light the kerosene heater in the corner, and run her palms over the grain of oak joists salvaged from mid-nineteenth-century barns. She was twenty-six years old, and her thumbs bore thin, silver scars from five years of handling gouges, timber chisels, and heavy bandsaws.

Nora had built her preservation workshop out of scrap timber, midnight accounting, and sheer endurance. While completing her apprenticeship in structural timber restoration, she worked four-hour morning shifts loading crates at the commercial icehouse, followed by late nights repairing antique window sashes for local contractors. She paid her own rent on the drafty brick shed behind Pier 9, bought her machinery secondhand at estate auctions, and survived winter months on canned soup and thick wool socks.
