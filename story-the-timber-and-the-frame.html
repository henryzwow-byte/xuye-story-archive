The Timber and the Frame

When architectural conservator Nora Vance refuses to co-sign a ruinous commercial loan for her brother, her family retaliates by filing a false felony theft report against her. Cornered by county deputies at a stormy maritime loading dock, Nora relies on precise archival records, a dedicated surveyor, and her own resilience to expose the fraud and reclaim her independence.

Fictional story. Contains themes of family conflict, financial extortion, false reports, and emotional boundary setting.

Chapter 1: The Midnight Rain at Astoria

The rain came off the Pacific in heavy, rhythmic sheets, hammering against the corrugated tin roof of Nora Vance’s timber restoration studio in Astoria, Oregon. Inside the converted 19th-century boat-barn, the air smelled of linseed oil, aged heart pine, and damp cedar. Nora stood at her drafting bench beneath a hanging industrial lamp, carefully inspecting a restored leaded glass transom from a historic coastal lighthouse. Her hands were stained with dark putty, her shoulders stiff from fourteen hours of continuous labor, but there was a deep, quiet order to her work. Every grain of wood in her shop had a documented origin, every pane of glass a preserved history. She believed in restoring things that had proven their resilience against time.

Her phone vibrated violently against the zinc countertop, rattling among her wood chisels. The screen displayed her older brother Julian’s name. Nora wiped her hands on a grease rag and picked up the call, bracing herself. "Julian, it's almost eleven o'clock."
