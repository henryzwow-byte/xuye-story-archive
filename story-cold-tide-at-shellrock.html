Cold Tide at Shellrock

When Julian Frost rescues an emaciated Redbone search hound locked inside a condemned coastal shed, he discovers a stamped harness rivet that unravels a corporate fraud scheme hiding behind fake death certificates.

Fictional story. Contains themes of animal neglect recovery, institutional fraud, and emotional healing.

Chapter 1: The Cold Tide at Shellrock

Julian Frost pulled his battered Ford flatbed into the gravel lot of the Shellrock Cove ferry landing just as the afternoon fog began to thicken over the Pacific tide flats. The engine spat a final shudder of dieseled exhaust before dying, leaving only the rhythm of rain drumming against the cracked windshield. His lower back flared with that familiar, grinding ache—the residual souvenir of a steel gantry cable snapping at the Astoria dry docks four years prior. The doctor had called it permanent spinal nerve compression; Julian simply called it the price of staying under heavy machinery too long. He stepped out onto the wet gravel, adjusting his oilskin coat, his left boot dragging slightly until his hip settled into its uneven groove.

He had stopped only to buy diesel fuel and a roll of industrial electrical tape from the salt-crusted marine supply store. But as he walked past the abandoned cedar salt-house behind the dock—a leaning structure slated for county demolition—he heard a low, metallic clinking sound. It was not the loose tin siding flapping in the sea gale. It was the distinct rhythm of an iron chain dragging against concrete.
