The Timber Ledger

When a wealthy Maine family publicly humiliates her children at a gala, an architectural archivist unleashes the verified timber compliance records that expose her stepbrother's dangerous structural fraud.

Fictional story. Contains themes of family conflict, financial deception, and emotional boundary setting.

Chapter 1: The Midsummer Gala

The salt wind off Penobscot Bay carried the scent of wet cedar timber and pine needles into the grand ballroom of the Vance Harbor House. Constructed in 1888 with heavy granite masonry and hand-carved white oak, the historic estate stood as a symbol of coastal Maine wealth. Outside, white silk banners snapped against stone pillars under the warm glow of brass lanterns. Inside, more than a hundred guests gathered under soaring timber rafters for the annual maritime restoration gala. Fresh oysters rested on mounds of crushed ice, while crystal flutes clicked together in quiet, wealthy toasts.

Clara Vance stood near the outer edge of the terrace, her hands resting gently on the shoulders of nine-year-old Leo and eleven-year-old Nora. Nora wore a simple navy cotton dress, and Leo had carefully polished his dark leather shoes before leaving their cottage. They had spent three hours helping Clara arrange historical photograph displays, their small fingers stained with black ink from archival catalogs. Leo was Clara’s nephew, adopted two years ago after her sister passed away, while Nora was her daughter. Clara raised both children on her earnings as an independent architectural archivist.
