FICTIONAL STORY · A-216

The Coastal Deed

Returning home from emergency hospital care, a master glassblower finds her seaside workshop padlocked under a fabricated municipal notice by her ambitious husband and his uncle—unaware that she holds a historic maritime covenant capable of reclaiming her life.

Fictional story. Contains themes of marital betrayal, financial disputes, attempted real estate fraud, and emotional recovery after medical hospitalization.

Chapter 1: The Salt and the Iron

The rain along Cape Sterling did not fall in clean vertical lines; it blew sideways off the Pacific, carrying the harsh tang of crushed kelp and wet pine. Nora Vance gripped the steering wheel of her battered station wagon with raw, bruised knuckles. Six days had passed since her emergency appendectomy, followed by forty-eight grueling hours of post-operative fever in the county hospital. Her body was still weak, her lower abdominal incision aching under the pressure of her seatbelt, but the urge to return to Herring Point had overridden every doctor's order for bed rest.

Herring Point was not merely a residential address; it was five acres of black granite, wind-sculpted spruce, and a timber-framed artisan workshop overlooking the tide pools. Nora's grandfather had constructed the glassblowing studio in 1954, firing custom architectural panels long before the small fishing village transformed into an affluent haven for summer tourists and coastal developers. Nora had spent twelve years restoring the heavy stone hearths, installing modern annealing ovens, and refining her craft until her blown-glass sculptures anchored corporate lobbies from Seattle to San Francisco.
