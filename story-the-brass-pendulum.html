FICTIONAL STORY · A-355

The Brass Pendulum

In a mountain town in Oregon, sixty-five-year-old Margaret Vance struggles to maintain her independence against her son's well-meaning control. A mysterious ledger hidden inside an antique clock unleashes a forgotten land dispute, a budding friendship, and a dramatic public revelation.

Fictional story. Contains themes of family conflict, grief, and elder autonomy.

Chapter 1: The Silence in the Brass

The workshop smelled of mineral spirits, dry cedar, and the faint metallic tang of aged clock oil. For thirty-two years, Margaret Vance had spent her mornings at the heavy oak workbench overlooking the main street of Laurel Falls, Oregon. At sixty-five, her hands were still steady enough to set a hairspring with brass tweezers, though her spine complained whenever the rain rolled down off the Cascade ridge. Since her husband Arthur passed four years ago, the ticking of forty restored pendulum clocks was the only steady rhythm left in her life.

Her son, Julian, arrived at eight-fifteen with a paper cup of black coffee and a leather briefcase tucked under his arm. At thirty-eight, Julian was a senior surveyor for the county, a man who viewed the world through parcel boundaries, elevation markers, and risk assessments. He set the coffee near her magnifying lens and laid out a stack of glossy pamphlets.
