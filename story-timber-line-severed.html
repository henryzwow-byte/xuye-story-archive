FICTIONAL STORY · A-218

The Cedar Foundation

For four years, structural engineer Hannah Vance privately guaranteed mortgages and structural permits for her parents' coastal orchard estate. But a routine inspection of storm damage exposes a hidden trust amendment that transfers all assets to her favored younger brother while shifting all debt liabilities onto her.

Fictional story. Contains themes of family financial exploitation, emotional manipulation, and boundary setting.

Chapter 1: The Surveyor’s Binder

The late afternoon drizzle off Puget Sound smelled of crushed hemlock and saltwater, clinging to the salt-weathered cedar shingles of the Vance Family Orchard. Hannah Vance unhitched her laser transit level from its tripod and wiped the damp lens with the sleeve of her waxed canvas jacket. At thirty-one, she ran a thriving structural engineering firm in downtown Seattle, specializing in historic timber restorations across the Pacific Northwest. For the past four years, however, her weekend hours had been claimed by Bainbridge Island. She had poured her personal savings and technical skill into stabilizing her parents’ failing stone barn, retrofitting its hand-hewn trusses, and covering the recurring commercial property taxes that her father always insisted were temporary cash-flow hiccups.

Her father, Richard Vance, had spent his sixties cultivating heirloom cider apples with more artistic sentiment than business logic. Her younger brother, Julian, twenty-seven, occupied the converted guest cottage on the ridge—a modern cedar structure Hannah had personally cosigned to help him establish an artisanal cider tasting venture. Julian had big ideas about organic branding and bespoke copper stills, but his interest rarely extended past hosting invite-only tasting events for his inner circle. When the bills arrived, Richard quietly forwarded the lender notices to Hannah’s inbox with brief notes asking if she could plug the leak just until the autumn harvest.
