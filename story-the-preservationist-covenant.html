FICTIONAL STORY · A-277

The Preservationist Covenant

After five years of quiet condescension from her former in-laws, Clara Vance steps out from their shadow to enforce a centuries-old maritime trust.

Fictional story. Contains themes of divorce, family arrogance, financial disputes, and corporate negotiation.

Chapter 1: The Municipal Stairs

On a brisk October morning in Portsmouth, New Hampshire, Clara Vance adjusted the lapels of her dark woolen coat as she descended the granite steps of the municipal courthouse. The final decree dissolving her five-year marriage to Julian Sterling was tucked safely inside her leather briefcase, signed and stamped at precisely ten fifteen. Behind her stood Julian and his mother, Victoria Sterling, flanked by two high-priced corporate attorneys carrying leather portfolios. Victoria’s pearls gleamed against her silk collar, her expression carrying the crisp, practiced condescension she had cultivated over decades as a prominent Boston socialite.

"You are returning to that cramped paper restoration studio down by the fish piers, I assume," Victoria said, her voice smooth and pitched just loud enough for the passing court reporters and municipal clerks to hear. "Without Julian’s line of credit and our family's civic connections, Clara, I frankly doubt your little bookbinding business will survive past the first winter frost. It requires substantial capital and family standing to anchor oneself in a town like Portsmouth, my dear."
