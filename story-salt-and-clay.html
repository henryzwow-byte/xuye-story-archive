FICTIONAL STORY · A-294

Salt and Clay

A structural restorer discovers that her entitlement-driven family left her teenage daughter outside in the rain during a town banquet held in her own property. Rather than throw a loud scene, she systematically collects financial records and reclaims her boundary.

Fictional story. Contains realistic themes of emotional neglect, family financial conflict, and boundary enforcement.

Chapter 1: Salt and Clay

Elena Vance stepped through the back mudroom of her farmhouse in coastal Maine, her work boots heavy with dried mortar and wet salt grass from a late-evening timber restoration project in Orfordville. The autumn wind whistled through the bayberry bushes outside, carrying the raw bite of early November. It was past nine o'clock, and she expected the house to be quiet, with her fifteen-year-old daughter Chloe asleep upstairs after attending the town’s annual Maritime Guild Gala.

Instead, a single yellow lamp glowed in the corner of the woodworking studio adjacent to the kitchen. Elena found Chloe sitting on a stool beside the workbench, still wearing her dark green velvet dress, her denim jacket zipped tight up to her chin. On the table beside her sat a wooden crate holding twelve handmade porcelain leaf dishes—salt-glazed pieces Chloe had spent three weeks carving for the gala’s artisan showcase.
