Currents of the Valley

When environmental engineer Julian Vance moves his family to the Cascade River Basin, a powerful local industrialist attempts to frame him for a river spill. Only calibrated telemetry and an unexpected aerial recording can reveal the truth.

Fictional story. Depicts false accusations, industrial sabotage, and law enforcement response. All characters, organizations, and events are entirely fictional.

Chapter 1: The New Registry

The wooden floorboards of the Cascade River Field Office smelled of aged pine, copper polish, and damp mountain air. Julian Vance adjusted the brass desk lamp and pressed his hand against the crisp leather surface of the district registry. Dated that very morning, Friday, August 14, the document bore the official seal of the Water Resources Board and his freshly inked signature as Chief Hydroelectric Inspection Engineer for the Pine Crest Basin.

Outside the wide bay windows, mist rolled through the cedar canopy, blanketing the public salmon sanctuary and the stone spillway downstream. Inside the adjacent staff lodge, cardboard moving boxes still stood stacked in the hallway. Julian’s wife, Clara, was arranging garden shears and sketchpads on the porch table, while their twelve-year-old son, Toby, assembled a lightweight environmental drone kit beside a stack of unpacked dishware. After years of demanding municipal consulting jobs in the coastal cities, this appointment was supposed to be a fresh start—a tranquil mountain post where Julian could steward the watershed and spend quiet weekends with his family.
