The Tide Line Ledger

When marine inspector Caleb Thorne returns to Alder Bay, he finds his home abandoned and his wife critically injured in an ICU. Unraveling a scheme by a powerful maritime corporation, Caleb and federal investigators rely on hidden acoustic telemetry to bring the conspirators to justice.

Fictional story. Depicts themes of corporate conspiracy, physical harm, hospital hospitalization, and legal investigations.

Chapter 1: The Cold Wharf

The salt fog off Alder Bay always had a sharp, metallic edge, but on the evening of October fourteenth, it smelled of diesel and turned milk. I stepped off the coastal inspection vessel Sentinel at six twenty p.m., hauling my duffel bag across the wet planks of the commercial dock. My three-week rotation evaluating lighthouse power grids along the Oregon shoreline was finished, and all I wanted was to sit at our kitchen table while my wife, Moira, drank tea and walked me through her latest hydrographic mapping charts.

Our cedar-shingled cottage sat two miles up North Breakwater Road, tucked behind a stand of Sitka spruce. As I turned onto the gravel driveway, the house was dark save for a single amber porch light swinging in the gusts coming off the Pacific. That was the first anomaly. Moira never left the interior dark when she knew I was returning. She was an eco-system hydrographer for the state board—methodical to a fault, accustomed to keeping strict logs for every tidal cycle.
