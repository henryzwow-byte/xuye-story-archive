FICTIONAL STORY · A-466

The Cedar and Brass

When an estranged relative sends an antique nautical compass to an architectural restorer's son, a careful inspection uncovers hidden motives embedded deep within the brass.

Fictional story. Contains themes of family estrangement, covert surveillance, and legal disputes.

Chapter 1: The Courier on the Dock

The rain sweeping over Tillamook Bay had a heavy, relentless quality to it, pouring off the Pacific Ocean in cold gray sheets that rattled against the corrugated tin roof of the timber conservation barn. Inside the workshop, however, the atmosphere was warm and dry, filled with the rich, comforting scents of freshly planed western red cedar, cooked linseed oil, and heated copper solder. Julian Vance stood at his long oak workbench, carefully wiping fine cedar sawdust from the intricate gear teeth of a clockwork brass automaton that his eight-year-old son, Silas, had finished assembling earlier that afternoon.

Silas was perched on a tall wooden stool near the cast-iron woodstove, his damp canvas sneakers tucked neatly behind the wooden rungs. He was staring proudly at the blue satin ribbons laid out on the bench—awards he had earned at the regional youth engineering exhibit in town. Hannah, Julian’s wife and co-director of their architectural preservation studio, leaned against the heavy timber doorway with two steaming ceramic mugs of black tea, watching Silas enthusiastically explain the gear ratio between the mainspring barrel and the escapement wheel to an imaginary panel of judges.
