FICTIONAL STORY · A-460

Tide and Timber

When his daughter and her corporate husband attempt to seize his century-old coastal boatyard, an aging shipwright reveals a quiet legal shield created by his late wife.

Fictional story. Contains themes of family conflict, emotional betrayal, and financial disputes.

Chapter 1: Salt and Saltpetre

The salt-crusted lantern above the slipway flickered against the Maine twilight as crystal champagne flutes clinked inside the glass-walled VIP pavilion. For over one hundred years, the Vance Boatyard had shaped white oak, larch, and yellow pine into sea-worthy hull frames along the granite edge of Blackwood Cove. But on this humid August evening, the familiar scents of cedar sawdust, turpentine, and sea kelp were completely swallowed by catered smoked salmon, imported cigars, and expensive floral perfume.

At seventy-one, Silas Vance stood near the outer timber railing of the main wharf, his calloused thumbs tucked into the belt loops of his canvas work trousers. His heavy hands were permanently stained with copper naphthenate, tar, and linseed oil, marked by five decades of hand-carving keels alongside his late wife, Clara. Across the manicured lawn, his daughter, Evelyn, adjusted her diamond necklace while her husband, Julian Sterling, gestured broadly toward the deep-water moorings for a circle of private equity investors who had traveled up from Boston.
