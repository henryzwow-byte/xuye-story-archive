The Curator's Watermark

When archival conservator Eleanor Vance is assigned to authenticate a rare 1782 maritime atlas for a prestigious summer auction, she uncovers a microscopic forged watermark linked to her husband's secret printing press—dismantling both his high-stakes antiquities fraud and his hidden affair before a room of high-society patrons.

Fictional story. Contains themes of infidelity, emotional deception, and white-collar fraud.

Chapter 1: The Preservation Room

Eleanor Vance worked in the climate-controlled sanctuary beneath the Blackwood Historical Foundation in Providence, Rhode Island. The basement restoration laboratory smelled of aged parchment, ethanol, dry oak gall ink, and gentle humidity. For twelve years, her professional life had been defined by quiet precision. She was a master conservator, skilled at humidifying brittle leaves of eighteenth-century manuscript paper, neutralizing iron-gall ink corrosion, and reading the subtle physical clues embedded inside ancient rag fibers.

Her husband, Julian Vance, moved through the art and antiquities trade with a completely different momentum. He was the founder of Sterling Brokerage, an elite advisory firm that liquidated historic estates for wealthy families along the New England coast. Julian was tall, effortlessly charismatic, and favored tailored Harris tweed jackets. He possessed a theatrical appreciation for rare objects, speaking of early American cartography and colonial silver as if they were personal friends. He spent nearly half of every month away from their home on what he called high-level sourcing trips to private collectors in New York and Boston.
