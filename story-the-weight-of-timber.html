The Weight of Timber

When a catastrophic loft collapse exposes years of silent family scapegoating, Hannah Vance must rely on external evidence and medical truth to dismantle her brother's long-standing lies.

Fictional story. Contains depictions of family emotional abuse, gaslighting, accidental physical injury, and medical settings.

Chapter 1: The Quiet Archive

Hannah Vance had learned early in life that silence was her safest refuge. At twenty-two, working as a junior botanical illustrator, she possessed a quiet grace that her family often mistook for weakness. Growing up in the shadow of her older brother Caleb, she had been trained to yield space, soften her tone, and absorb blame whenever friction arose within the household. Caleb was twenty-six, a sharp-suited land developer with a polished public smile, loud confidence, and an uncanny ability to turn his own missteps into entertaining anecdotes at Hannah’s expense.

The family had gathered at the old Vance Timber Orchard, an expansive cedar compound on the rainy coast of western Washington. The estate, built by their grandfather decades ago, smelled permanently of wet pine, damp soil, linseed oil, and aging fir. It was supposed to be a weekend dedicated to cataloging the estate's historical workshop equipment before selling the property. Their parents, Arthur and Eleanor Vance, walked through the high-ceilinged rooms discussing real estate estimates, clearly viewing Caleb as the natural heir to every major family decision.
