The Iron Locker at Blackwood Cove

After her husband dies leaving a strict warning never to open an iron locker at a remote tide-mill, Clara Vance uncovers a decades-old secret radio relay and the silent guilt that fractured her family.

Fictional story. Contains mild themes of family grief, historical maritime accidents, and estrangement.

Chapter 1: The Silence at Tide's End

In the final hour before the cold fog rolled off Puget Sound, my husband did not ask for my forgiveness, nor did he ask me to light a candle at St. Jude’s on the anniversary of his passing. He lay beneath the stiff hospital linens of the Bellingham medical center, his breath catching like a dry gear in an unoiled clock.

Arthur Vance had spent forty-two years as a master electrician and navigational technician. He was a man who measured his life in copper gauges, solder joints, and tidal charts. In all our decades together in our gray-shingled house overlooking the water, I knew the cadence of his boots on the porch, the smell of ozone and pipe tobacco that clung to his canvas coats, and the exact tone of voice he used when a transformer had blown down in the harbor.
