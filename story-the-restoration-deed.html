The Restoration Deed

In a tense municipal arbitration hall, historic timber restorer Maeve Miller faces an injunction that threatens to bankrupt her workshop and strip away her lifetime of work. But a forgotten heritage trust holds a revelation that turns the tide.

Fictional story. Contains themes of corporate betrayal, contract dispute, and emotional coercion.

Chapter 1: The Cold Marble Hall

The municipal arbitration chamber in downtown Portland smelled of stale radiator heat, damp sea air, and polished mahogany. Outside the tall arched windows, fog rolled over Casco Bay, muting the morning sun into a pale gray haze. Maeve Miller sat at the wooden defense table, her rough, calloused hands resting flat against her linen trousers. Beneath her fingernails were faint traces of walnut stain and cedar dust—the honest residue of fifteen years spent restoring historic timber structures across coastal Maine.

Across the narrow aisle sat Trent Sterling. His tailored navy suit was crisp, his silver cuff links catching the harsh fluorescent light. Beside him, his high-priced corporate attorney, Arthur Vance, systematically organized a mountain of heavy leather binders. For two straight hours, Vance had laid out a meticulous timeline designed to dismantle Maeve’s career. According to their documents, every custom joinery technique, every proprietary wood-preservation resin formula, and every client contract Maeve had developed was the exclusive intellectual property of Sterling Restorations—the firm Trent had convinced her to co-found four years ago.
