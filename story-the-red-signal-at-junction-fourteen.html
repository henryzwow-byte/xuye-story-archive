The Red Signal at Junction Fourteen

A rail safety inspector triggers emergency signals to save a heavy freight train from a collapsing trestle bridge. Fired for disrupting shipping schedules, she and her surveyor husband uncover telemetry evidence and a crew member's secret recording that expose corporate negligence.

Chapter 1: The Red Notice on the Kitchen Counter

Nora was sitting at the kitchen island when I pulled my truck into the driveway, and the heavy silence radiating through the porch window stopped me before I even turned off the ignition. Rain beat against the metal roof in that relentless, late-autumn Pacific Northwest cadence that makes a timber cabin feel small and isolated. The kitchen smelled of stale roast coffee, damp canvas coats, and citrus disinfectant. Nora always cleaned meticulously when she was fighting back tears. But tonight, she wasn't moving at all.

Her hands were resting flat against the laminate counter, fingers interlaced so tightly her knuckles were white. Her ceramic coffee mug sat untouched beside her, the steam long gone. The overhead pendant lamp cast a single harsh circle of warm light over a single sheet of heavy bond paper. I had spent enough years reading disaster reports and forestry incident files to recognize bad news before reading the header. The sharpest blows in working life rarely arrive with shouting; they arrive on smooth letterhead with margins, dates, and formal seals.
