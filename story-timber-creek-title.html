Timber Creek Title

After years of investing her separate inheritance into a remote Oregon workshop, a mother faces a sudden lockout by her overbearing sister-in-law. Guided by quiet legal facts and an uncle's steady support, she draws a line that forces her husband to choose between passive fear and domestic truth.

Fictional story. Contains themes of family property disputes, domestic boundary violations, and emotional pressure.

Chapter 1: The Lock on the Timber Studio

Nora Finch knew that independence was not something handed down through goodwill; it was built piece by piece from documented labor. For three years, she had spent her late evenings turning the weathered cedar granary at Timber Creek into a climate-controlled botanical preservation studio. She had personally financed the standing-seam zinc roof, the industrial dehumidification systems, and the custom quarter-sawn oak drying presses using the lump-sum payout from her late mother’s life insurance policy. Every invoice was cataloged, every payment receipt cleared through her separate personal checking account, and every building permit bore her own name as the primary applicant.

On a cool Tuesday afternoon in late October, Nora returned to the orchard from a forty-mile trip to the regional archival supply warehouse. Her four-year-old daughter, Maya, was fast asleep in her car seat, her thumb hooked into her soft cotton sweater. Rain clouds hung heavy over the coastal ridge, threatening to turn into a downpour before nightfall. Nora pulled her station wagon alongside the studio's gravel turnout, unloaded two heavy rolls of acid-free rag paper, and walked toward the broad sliding doors.
