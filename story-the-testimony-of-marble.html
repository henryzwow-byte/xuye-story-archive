FICTIONAL STORY · A-078

The Testimony of Marble

Pregnant Elena is told she has no place in the family home, and a confrontation on the polished marble floor leaves every relative clinging to a different version of events. Reflections, a red recording light and a carefully preserved timeline force the house itself to testify.

Fiction. Contains pregnancy-related danger, family conflict, coercion, physical confrontation and emotional distress.

Chapter One: The Shadow of Confrontation

Elena’s fingers slipped from Vivian’s wrist. “You mean I should leave.”

“What I’m saying is, it’s time for you to leave before you turn this home into a nursery and turn other people into transients.”

The words “other people” caused an instant silence somewhere deep in Elena’s heart.

There is never any “other” here. Rather, it’s just Julian and Margaret, and their intimacy can sometimes feel suffocating, as neither wants to let the other down. And here’s Vivian, who grew up in this home and treats every room as proof that she rightfully belongs here. And Elena always comes late, always appearing after everything has settled.

She once firmly believed that as long as she had enough patience, there would eventually be a place for her here. It wasn’t until she got pregnant that this patience started to feel like a slow loss.

“I didn’t ask Margaret to tell you to move away,” Elena said.

Vivian’s expression only changed less than a millimeter, but Elena caught it. “No. You made this have to happen.”

“This is not the same thing as that.”

“That’s one thing when you’re the one that gets pushed out.”

“You’re not pushed away. You’re given time. You’re given help.”

“Got help,” Vivian snorted, “You make it sound like I’m a stray cat they picked up on the porch.”

Elena’s arm ached under Vivian’s restraint. She tried to break free again. Vivian adjusted her body slightly as she moved, blocking her way, causing Elena’s high heels to slip half an inch on the floor.

“Vivian,” Elena said, “that’s enough.”

“Why? Because he can hear it?”

“He’s outside.”

“That’s right.”

This answer made Elena feel an inexplicable chill. For a while, neither of them moved. Above their heads, the crystal chandelier gave out a faint metallic sigh, the tiny sound the whole house made as it bore its own weight. The sound was so subtle that Elena almost thought it was her own breathing.

Vivian looked toward the double doors, then back to Elena.

“He will always come back to you,” she said. “You know what? Even if he is angry. Even if it is you who is wrong. He will still come back because you have turned yourself into the softest harbor in every room.”

Elena looked at her, really looked at her. Vivian’s face was filled with anger, yes. But beneath the rage lies something older. An exhausting thing. A humiliating thing.

“Did you think this was just about Julian?” Elena asked.

“This is about Julian.”

“No. It’s about not knowing who you really are after you leave this house.”

Vivian’s grip became painful. Elena regretted it as soon as she said it. It’s not because there’s any truth to it. But because for Vivian, the truth has never been a door. It’s a match.

“You are not entitled to say that to me,” Vivian said.

“You’re holding on to me.”

“You don’t have the right to stand in my grandmother’s foyer and tell me where I belong.”

Elena winced at the word “grandmother.” Vivian only uses this word when she wants to weaponize the past. Margaret had inherited the house long before Elena met Julian. Vivian spent most of her adult life here, walking in and out of various rooms, as if the wall was built to suit her mood.

“I’m not telling you where you belong,” Elena said, “I’m just telling you that you can’t take it out on me out of fear.”

Vivian stared at her.

Then, she said very softly: “You have not grasped what I’m afraid of.”

Elena didn’t answer. The silence between them was big enough to hold a lot of things: old arguments, unspoken apologies, a hastily ended dinner, and the moments when Julian should have spoken but chose to look away. She wasn’t looking for a fight when she walked into the foyer. She just wanted to get through tonight. She wore the ivory dress because it was comfortable. She wears low heels because her feet hurt. She tucked her hair behind her ears before going downstairs because she wanted to look less tired.

Vivian, on the other hand, clearly dressed up to fight.

“This baby is going to be born into a home where everyone is pretending,” Vivian said. “You know that, right?”

Elena’s hands protected her abdomen more firmly. “Don’t talk like that about my kid.”

“Your children will learn all this. Julian is used to hiding. Margaret is used to controlling. You only cry when you need something. And I am the only one who tells the reality in blood.”

“No,” Elena said, “you just think being cruel makes you appear honest.”

Vivian’s eyes flickered. The heavy wooden structure of the double doors remained silent. The room outside the door remains hidden. Elena couldn’t hear Julian’s voice. She couldn’t hear Margaret’s voice. The absence of sound made the foyer seem extra large and cold.

Vivian finally let go of Elena’s arm. There was a dull pain where her fingers had scratched her. Elena took a step back, putting distance between the two of them. She should have headed for the door. She should have called Julian. She should have done something simple and decisive.

Instead, she stood by the stairs, gasping cautiously, because she felt dangerous leaving her back to Vivian, and because some stubborn part of her heart still hoped that this conversation would lead to another ending.
