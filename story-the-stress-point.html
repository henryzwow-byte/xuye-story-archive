The Stress Point

When a compromised waterfront construction project threatens a developer with bankruptcy, he turns to an elite forensic engineering firm—unaware that the principal engineer tasked with auditing his failure is the daughter he left behind decades ago.

Fictional story. Contains themes of parental abandonment, historic family conflict, and financial distress. All characters, businesses, and engineering events are purely fictional.

Chapter 1: The Weight of Timber

Claire Vance still remembered the specific hollow bang of a heavy iron bolt dropping against wet cedar planks. She was twelve years old when that single metallic thud marked the permanent rupture of her family's life on the Puget Sound inlet. Her mother, Eleanor Vance, had been an independent landscape architect whose spine was crushed during a sudden structural failure at a waterfront timber drying shed. In the agonizing months that followed, the fragrant smell of fresh-cut cedar inside their small rented cottage was replaced by the sharp, medicinal tang of antiseptic liniments, damp floorboards, and cold tea left on nightstands.

Her father, Arthur Vance, was a mid-level framing contractor who spent his evenings calculating profit margins on grease-stained graph paper. When Eleanor’s medical bills mounted and the insurance claim ran into administrative impasses, Arthur showed no patience for fragile things. He systematically organized his silver draft precision instruments, packed two heavy hard-shell tool trunks, and locked his truck cab without looking back at the house. Claire's nine-year-old brother, Julian, stood barefoot beside the porch pillar holding a tarnished brass tide gauge their uncle had given him. Julian had asked softly if Dad was going to fetch more timber for the shed. Arthur had merely scoffed, declaring to the open yard that he refused to let his life be dragged down by dead weight, before driving south into the coastal fog.
