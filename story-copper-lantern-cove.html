The Copper Lantern Cove

Caught in a Pacific gale and driven by hunger, Clara Vance trespasses on a reclusive founder's sea-wall workshop, only to unlock a secret legacy left by her late uncle.

Fictional story. Depicts themes of economic hardship, grief, and emotional recovery without graphic content.

Chapter 1: The Scraped Sea-Wall

It was the wet grinding of boots over barnacled timber after midnight. It was the sharp, shallow breath Clara Vance could not quiet as the Pacific wind cut through her oilskin coat. By eleven o'clock, the gale had driven every commercial trawler into the sheltered sound, leaving the harbor dark except for the distant pulse of the north headland beacon. Clara stood shivering on the access dock behind Ross Maritime Foundry, clutching a rusted iron lever she had found in the tide trash.

Her stomach had been empty since Tuesday morning, when the last dockyard cannery shut down its winter line. Hunger came in distinct layers. First came the quiet embarrassment of declining lunch with the deckhands. Then came the heavy lethargy that made every staircase feel steep. Finally came the cold clarity of midnight, when pride ceased to be a shield and became merely an obstacle to remaining alive.
