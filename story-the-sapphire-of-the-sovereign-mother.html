FICTIONAL STORY · A-108

The Sapphire of the Sovereign Mother

A luxury director humiliates an elderly visitor, only to learn that dignity—not status—will decide the future of the company she serves.

Fictional story. Contains class prejudice, workplace misconduct, a non-graphic slap, a heated confrontation, dismissal, and emotional distress.

Chapter 1: The White Marble Gauntlet

Valois Luxury's Rodeo Drive boutique gleamed with white Italian marble, crystal chandeliers, and display cases bright enough to make every gemstone look untouchable. Victoria Vance, the managing director, treated the showroom as her private court. She measured customers by their clothes, their watches, and the confidence with which they crossed the threshold.

Late one afternoon, Clara Montgomery entered in a weathered coat after walking several blocks from a bus stop. A little dust clung to her shoes. Victoria intercepted her before she reached the sapphire pendant in the front window and ordered her to leave, insisting that someone who looked like her could not afford anything in the store.

Clara explained that she only wanted to see the pendant because her son had asked her to choose something meaningful. Victoria laughed at the idea. When Clara quietly asked to speak with another associate, Victoria lost control and slapped her. Clara stumbled against a display case but remained standing, one hand pressed to her cheek.

The glass doors opened before Victoria could raise her hand again. Julian Montgomery crossed the showroom, caught Victoria's wrist, and placed himself between the two women. He did not strike her. He called the store's security supervisor, asked that the doors remain open, and demanded that every camera recording be preserved. Then he turned to his mother and asked whether she needed medical care.
