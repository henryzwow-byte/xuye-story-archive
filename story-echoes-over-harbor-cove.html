Echoes Over Harbor Cove

When a timber restoration specialist discovers his estranged former partner quietly raising their hearing-impaired daughter along the Maine coast, a suppressed past unravels, forcing him to rebuild trust from the ground up.

Fictional story. Contains emotional themes of past family estrangement, pediatric medical debt, and interpersonal conflict.

Chapter 1: The Driftwood Market

Marcus Vance built his career on timber that had survived centuries of salt spray, ice storms, and neglected harbors. As the principal conservator of Vance Historic Preservation in Portland, Maine, he spent his cold winter mornings sifting through salvaged barn beams, abandoned mill timbers, and shipwright wreckage. He was known among maritime contractors and city historical commissions as a relentless, methodical man who trusted grain density over empty promises and structural integrity over aesthetic charm. His personal life, however, was a quiet, bolted door he had shut six years prior when his partnership with marine research technician Clara Sterling collapsed under the weight of financial exhaustion, grueling work hours, and bitter, lingering silences.

On a biting Tuesday morning in late February, Marcus walked through the outdoor salvage docks at Harbor Cove. The damp Atlantic air tasted of diesel exhaust, frozen mud, and wet cedar shavings. Seagulls wheeled low beneath a ceiling of iron-gray clouds as dockworkers hauled crate after heavy crate of weathered pine off the barges. Marcus was searching for heart-pine joists to restore a historic 18th-century customs house along the waterfront, but his steps slowed near a makeshift vendor stall selling discounted sound-dampening acoustic tiles and reclaimed cypress wall panels.
