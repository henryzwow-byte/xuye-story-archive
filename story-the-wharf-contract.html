FICTIONAL STORY · A-167

The Wharf Contract

When Nora Mercer and her eight-year-old son are humiliated at the annual coastal maritime banquet, she stops holding back the quiet financial access her late husband left in her hands.

Fictional story. Contains themes of family conflict, emotional grief, and financial independence.

Chapter 1: The Guild Table

The maritime hall at Oakhaven Harbor smelled of cedar oil polish, cold ocean salt, and slow-roasted prime rib. High above the parquet floor, brass lamps glowed along the exposed fir rafters, casting warm golden circles over fifty white linen tablecloths. At the center table, positioned directly beneath the guild’s antique iron chandelier, sat the executive committee of Mercer Freight & Haulage. Aunt Beatrice adjusted her layered pearl necklace with calculated grace, her eyes scanning the ballroom to ensure every prominent shipper, harbor commissioner, and timber buyer in the county noticed her family's prime seating arrangement. Beside her, my older brother Julian cut into his steak with methodical precision, occasionally offering a practiced nod toward local council members while entirely ignoring his eight-year-old nephew sitting opposite him.

My son, Leo, sat on my left in a dark navy blazer that was slightly broad in the shoulders. He had drawn three tiny tugboats on the edge of his cardstock place card with a graphite pencil, his small thumbs smudged with grey lead. He was counting the decorative brass rivets on the massive wooden ship wheel mounted behind Beatrice’s high-backed chair. Eighteen rivets. He liked numbers because numbers remained predictable and steady, regardless of who entered the room or how cold the air became.
