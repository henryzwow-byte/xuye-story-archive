Timber, Tide, and Title

Recovering from a severe leg injury in her coastal Oregon home during a torrential storm, Clara Vance discovers her son has forged land title documents to pledge her orchard as loan collateral. Facing darkness and betrayal, she takes decisive legal action to reclaim her property and autonomy.

Chapter 1: The Weight of Cedar

The salt-heavy wind off Cape Rowan rattled the double-hung windows of the old orchard house, carrying the smell of crushed pine needles and wet granite. Clara Vance sat at the heavy oak kitchen table, her right leg encased in a rigid orthopedic boot after a stone retaining wall in the upper gardens had given way during the autumn rains three weeks ago. The house was cold despite the roaring stove, its floorboards grooving under sixty years of coastal moisture.

Across the room, her thirty-four-year-old son, Julian, leaned against the kitchen counter, his eyes fixed on the illuminated screen of his smartphone. He wore a sharply tailored wool coat that looked entirely out of place against the weathered cedar walls. For three days, he had been staying in the upstairs guest room, claiming he had taken time off from his boutique investment consultancy in Portland to oversee her recovery. But he spent most hours pacing the porch, speaking in hushed, urgent tones to callers she never heard him name.
