The Archival Impression

When letterpress artisan Corinne Miller delivers a custom registry to a high-profile timber lodge dedication, she unexpectedly encounters Julian Vance—the ambitious architect who abandoned their twin daughters five years ago. As Julian's wealthy fiancée discovers the deceitful narrative he spun to cover his past, Corinne stands firm on public accountability and self-respect.

Fictional story. Contains themes of emotional deception, family secrets, and parental estrangement.

Chapter 1: The Cedar and the Cast Iron

The crisp smell of cedar wood smoke and river mist always settled early over Pine Creek, a quiet timber town nestled deep in the Columbia River Gorge. Inside a renovated 1920s brick cannery overlooking the water, Corinne Miller ran Gorge Letterpress & Bindery. For seven years, her days had been measured by the rhythmic clack of heavy iron machinery, the rich tang of linseed oil ink, and the meticulous hand-setting of lead type. She took pride in crafting things built to last—durable cotton invitations, archival books, and deep, tactile impressions pressed into heavy paper.

Five years ago, her life had been abruptly dismantled and reassembled. Julian Vance, a brilliant preservation architect expanding his family’s prominent Chicago firm into the Pacific Northwest, had walked out of her world. When Corinne broke the news of her high-risk pregnancy with twin girls, Julian had panicked. Sitting at her kitchen counter with rain lashing the window, he had spoken with the polished detachment of an executive delivering an unfavorable quarterly report. He claimed that fatherhood would compromise his career trajectory, split his financial focus, and disrupt his firm’s high-stakes corporate expansion. He wrote a cashier’s check for half their remaining studio lease, packed his leather luggage, and drove off into the coastal fog.
