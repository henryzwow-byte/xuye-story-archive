FICTIONAL STORY · A-344

The Timber Seal

A decorated structural engineer returns home to find his brother and mother using his credentials to cover up hazardous construction on a high-stakes heritage project, forcing a public reckoning atop the rain-swept bluffs.

Fictional story. Contains themes of familial deception, emotional conflict, and professional ethics disputes.

Chapter 1: The Rain on Oakhaven Bluffs

The scent of freshly sawed red cedar, polished granite, and damp sea air clung to the grand portico of the Vance ancestral estate on Oakhaven Bluffs. Down along the rocky cove, rain flickered through the pine canopy, but on the driveway, valet drivers in crisp black coats guided slick luxury sedans into place. Silk banners fluttered from the timber gables, announcing the evening's premier event: the unveiling of the Pacific Heritage Pavilion, a twenty-million-dollar timber-frame resort development led by my elder half-brother, Julian Vance.

I had been back on home soil for less than twenty minutes after three straight years in the northern Cascades, leading alpine bridge stabilization crews after seasonal landslides. My work boots were clean, but the heavy leather satchel across my shoulder held the scars of rough mountain field sites, containing my certified master engineer seal, digital stress logs, and federal civil restoration medals.
