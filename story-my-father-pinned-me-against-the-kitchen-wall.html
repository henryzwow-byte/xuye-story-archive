FICTIONAL STORY · A-032

My Father Pinned Me Against the Kitchen Wall

Twenty-nine-year-old Claire is fighting cancer when her father pins her against the kitchen wall and tries to force her to sign away the sixty-five thousand dollars reserved for surgery. Her phone recording and a financial investigation expose years of family coercion, identity theft and the gambling crisis they had always expected her to absorb.

Fiction. Contains attempted strangulation, family violence, cancer treatment, coercive control, financial abuse, gambling addiction, identity theft, police response and courtroom proceedings.

Chapter One: The Phone Remembered

The first time my father tried to strangle me, it happened beneath a photograph of our whole family smiling at Disney World. I was already twenty-nine years old. After months of chemotherapy, I weighed only eighty-eight pounds.

My hair was starting to grow into thin, uneven stubble, but I still wore a soft gray hat because the skin on my scalp was still sensitive and fragile. Before I could react, he moved his position, and Richard’s rough fingers were already tightly grasping my throat.

One second, he was standing across from the kitchen forcing me to sign. The next second, my back hit the wall hard. The family photo overhead shook violently. My mother screamed my name. Not because she wanted him to stop.

“Claire, don’t resist him!”

My vision turned pale. I scratched Richard’s wrist desperately. His face was inches from mine, twisted with rage.

“You’ve been taking from this family your whole life.”

I can’t answer. The envelope containing my medical documents was wedged tightly between his arm and my chest. Across the room, my brother, Brandon, stood at the dining room table. The expensive watch on his wrist was shaking slightly.

He didn’t come over to help me.

“Dad,” he said weakly, “just get her to sign it.”

Richard increased the strength on his hands.

“Your brother needs this money more than you do to save his life.”

The words cut through the roar of my tinnitus. Something hard awoke deep in my consciousness. Not courage. But the program. Three weeks ago, my lawyer told me that fear becomes more deadly when it erases logic.

Remember what happened. Save what you can. Get out before you prove anything else. I shoved Richard’s forearms with both hands, tilting his face to the side. His grip relaxed slightly, and air finally squeezed into his trachea.

“Take your hands away.”

Elaine rushed to his side. For a second, I held out hope that my mother would pull him away. Instead, she slapped me hard on the shoulder with the asset transfer document.

“Stop struggling and sign quickly!”

Richard still held me down. Brandon moved closer. His eyes moved from my face to the letter.

“Give me the documents quickly before they come to trouble me.”

I yanked the envelope away from his reach.

“You brought the debt on yourself.”

“You cannot imagine what they would do to me.”

“You said the same thing last time.”

“This time is different.”

“Every time you need money, it’s the same excuse.”

His expression hardened.

“You’re going to die anyway.”

My heart fell into a deathly silence. Richard’s hand was still around my throat. Elaine held the papers. Brandon reached out and tried to take away the deposit that was supposed to pay for my surgery.

None of the three of them were shocked by what he said. My shoulder hit the wall again as Richard tried to force me down. I used what little strength I had left to slam sideways.

He let go for a second. My knees buckled and I fell to the ground. Severe pain shot through my skull and abdomen. The envelope is still stuck to my chest. Richard lunged at me again.

Elaine grabbed his arm.

“Wait.”

Her eyes were fixed on my face. I stopped crying. My calmness frightened her more than my resistance. I reached into my sweater pocket and pulled out my phone. A tiny recording light lit up on the screen.

Richard’s hand stopped inches from mine. Brandon backed away in fear. Elaine’s mouth opened in horror.

“Keep talking,” I whispered. “My phone has every word down.”

The rage on Richard’s face disappeared.

“What did you record?”

“All.”

Elaine rushed over to grab the phone. I raised my phone high and avoided her hand.

“Claire, give me that.”

“No.”

“This is a private family matter for us.”

“You just turned this into a deliberate attack.”

Richard glanced at the window in alarm.

“Delete it.”

“No.”

His voice deepened.

“You have not grasped what you’re doing.”

“I know exactly what’s in this recording.”

Brandon walked toward the back door in a panic.

“Where are you going?” Richard asked.

“I don’t want to be here when the police come.”

I didn’t mention the police at all. His fear revealed that he knew the consequences of this recording would be far more than just humiliating. Elaine rushed to block him.

“You can’t just leave us here.”

I stood up unsteadily. The wall behind him seemed to be shaking. My throat burned.

“You entered this door together,” I said. “You can explain to the police together.”

Richard took a step forward. I raised my phone.

“The files have been automatically backed up to a place you can’t reach.”

It’s true. My attorney, Naomi Chen, helped me set up automatic cloud backups since receiving the threat message at home. This recording doesn’t even need to be on your phone to be saved. Richard understood.

He slowly lowered his hands.

“Your mother invited you here to discuss Brandon’s safety.”

“She asked me to sign the transfer of property.”

“You refused to help your brother.”

“I refuse to hand over my surgery fee.”

“He could have died.”

“Me too.”

Elaine’s face twisted.

“Don’t compare your cancer to the dangers Brandon faces.”

This sentence completely exposed the value I was given in that family. Brandon’s mess was an urgent emergency. And my terminal illness is just an expense that can be cut at any time. I stepped back into the front hallway.

Richard approaches again. I pressed the emergency button on my phone. The device began calling 911 and sent my location to Naomi and my friend Hannah in real time. Elaine heard the busy signal when the phone was connected.

“You actually called the police to arrest your biological father for such a thing?”

Richard yelled:

“Tell them it was just an accident!”

I finally retreated to the front door. The cold air slapped my skin. My neighbor, Mrs. Delgado, heard the quarrel and was standing by the mailbox looking into it. She watched me stumble onto the porch.

I saw the red marks gradually appearing on my neck. Then she ran towards me. Richard remained in the house. He didn’t chase him out.

Just a few minutes ago, he had been convinced that the walls of his so-called “home” could conceal what was going on inside.

And now, the door is open. The recording continues. And the first witness, who had never been trained to make excuses for him, was walking quickly towards me.
