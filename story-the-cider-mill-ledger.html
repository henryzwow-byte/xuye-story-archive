The Cider Mill Ledger

A night of betrayal at an historic Hudson Valley cider mill forces an architectural restorer to flee with forged documents. Her father, a retired surveyor, untangles a high-stakes corporate land trap with the help of his estranged sister.

Fictional story. Depicts corporate coercion, financial fraud, and family conflict. All characters, locations, and events are purely fictional products of creative imagination.

Chapter 1: The Midnight Signal

The autumn frost had already settled over the valley when the rattle of an old diesel engine cut through the silence of Pine Ridge. Arthur Vance stood by his kitchen workbench, wiping sawdust from his hands, as headlights swept across the frosted windows of his timber cottage. It was past eleven on a Tuesday night. In a town where roads grew dark by eight, an unexpected vehicle was rarely a harbinger of good news. When he stepped onto the porch, he found his daughter, Hannah, leaning against the fender of her work truck, her jacket soaked with freezing rain and her breathing fast and shallow.

She was holding a water-stained leather messenger bag tightly against her ribs. Under the amber glare of the porch lamp, her face was pale, and dark purple pressure marks ringed both of her wrists. When Arthur reached for her, she stumbled forward, her work boots slipping on the slick wooden steps. Real panic rarely arrives with dramatic speeches; it comes in jagged gasps and cold hands that refuse to let go. He helped her inside, shutting the heavy oak door against the rising wind, and guided her to the stone fireplace where the embers still glowed softly.
