Sanctuary on the Canyon Ridge

After three years of quiet isolation on a powerful family's estate, a young mother and her son are ousted into a mountain blizzard—only to uncover a surveyor's locked case that alters everything they knew about a missing husband.

Fictional story. Depicts family conflict, financial coercion, and memory loss themes in a dramatic context.

Chapter 1: The Eviction at Frost Hollow

The mountain fog settled heavy over the High Desert basin of Frost Hollow, wrapping the stone caretaker's lodge in a damp, icy sheath. For three long years following her husband Lucian’s sudden disappearance, Claire Mercer had survived in that drafty cottage on the outskirts of the Holloway Timber Estate. Her seven-year-old son, Leo, sat near the cold iron stove, drawing quiet spirals into the frost on the windowpane. When Lucian’s lightweight surveying plane vanished over the sharp crags of the Cascade Range, his father, Garrison Holloway, had wasted no time shifting the narrative. Garrison painted Lucian as a reckless gambler who had run off to escape corporate debt, while treating Claire and Leo like unwelcome charity cases allowed to stay only out of grudging familial duty.

Every month was a campaign of subtle humiliation. Garrison’s estate manager audited Claire’s modest wages from her part-time work at the local seed nursery, questioning every grocery line item while keeping her under constant threat of removal. Yet Claire endured the isolation because the lodge held the last remnants of Lucian’s touch—his hand-carved cedar wooden blocks in Leo’s room, his leather jacket lingering with the scent of pine pitch in the closet, and the quiet hope that one day a definitive answer would find its way down the mountain road.
