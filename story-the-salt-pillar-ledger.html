FICTIONAL STORY · A-197

The Salt-Pillar Ledger

In a fog-bound coastal Maine town, historical timber surveyor Clara Vance uncovers a falsified registry mark on an ancestral lighthouse beam, forcing her to confront a buried legacy of family betrayal and commercial fraud.

Fictional story. Contains depictions of family conflict, financial disputes, and corporate fraud.

Chapter 1: The Cold Gauge

The Atlantic tide hit the granite footings of Cape Point Light with a hollow, rhythmic roar, throwing fine salt spray against the high fogged windows of the lower keeper’s quarters. Clara Vance unbuckled her canvas tool roll across the worn pine trestle table, the familiar metallic clink of brass calipers, iron moisture probes, and timber scrapers breaking the heavy damp silence of the station. Outside, the bleak November wind whistled through the wrought-iron railings of the lantern gallery high above, but down in the sub-level timber cribbing, the air smelled only of wet hemlock, cured pitch, age-old lime wash, and ocean brine.

At thirty-four, Clara was the senior timber structural auditor retained by the Lincoln County Historical Conservancy. She had spent twelve years evaluating historical coastal structures across northern New England, building an unquestioned reputation for precision and uncompromising accuracy. While commercial developers viewed old lighthouses as prospective luxury acreage or deep-water shipping terminals, Clara saw them as living mechanical puzzles of joint work, grain density, tension beams, and load distribution.
