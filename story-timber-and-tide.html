Timber and Tide

When developer Lyle Vance tries to expropriate Maeve Holloway's historic timber mill using a dubious land claim, an ancient craftsman's secret embedded within the mill's heartwood beam changes everything.

Fictional story. Contains depictions of civil property disputes, corporate deceit, and emotional tension.

Chapter 1: The Echo of Ironwood

The salt fog hanging over Cedar Reach smelled of cedar shavings, damp shale, and the bitter tang of low tide. In the quiet sanctuary of the timber shop, Maeve Holloway rested her calloused palm against the massive hemlock post that anchored the building to the shore. For three generations, the Holloway Tide Mill had stood at the estuary’s edge, its heavy wooden gears turning with the steady rhythm of the water. Maeve pulled her flannel coat tighter against the morning draft, listening to the familiar groans of timber seasoned by a century of sea rain.

The heavy oak door creaked open, breaking the silence. Lyle Vance walked in without wiping his boots, his polished leather shoes leaving muddy tracks across the swept pine floor. Behind him stood a young associate clutching a leather briefcase and an aluminum measuring tape. Lyle was the executive director of Vance Maritime Logistics, a regional developer whose steel container terminals had gradually replaced the traditional wooden boathouses along the bay. He looked out of place among the iron clamps and hand saws, his tailored wool coat smelling faintly of dry cleaning and cologne.
