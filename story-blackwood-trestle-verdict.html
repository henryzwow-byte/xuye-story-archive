The Gravity of Timber and Steel

When a runaway ballast car threatens a mountain rail ceremony, retired structural engineer Sarah Vance intervenes to save her son, sparking a confrontation that uncovers structural negligence.

Fictional story. Contains themes of workplace safety hazards, physical injury recovery, and tense verbal confrontation.

Chapter 1: The Weight of Steel

Long before the rain began to fall over the mountain pass, Sarah Vance had learned to measure life by structural integrity. For fifteen years, she had worked as a senior rigger and heavy infrastructure inspector across the Pacific Northwest, climbing concrete piers and assessing stress fractures in steel girders. She knew the exact pitch a cable made before it frayed, and she could gauge the load-bearing capacity of a Douglas fir timber by tapping it with her knuckle. But twelve years ago, inside a damp rail tunnel near the Montana border, an unmapped shale collapse buried her lower right leg under three tons of shattered timber shoring.

Survival had not been clean or majestic. It was four agonizing surgeries, titanium pins, a steel leg brace that hissed softly under her trousers, and months of grueling physical therapy where every step felt like pressing shattered glass into cold clay. When well-meaning neighbors called her recovery miraculous, Sarah remained silent. She knew that courage wasn't a sudden flash of light; it was the quiet, grueling decision to pull yourself out of bed at dawn when your hip joint felt like rusted iron scraping against stone.
