FICTIONAL STORY · A-326

Tide and Iron

When nine-year-old Liam brings a tin of scrap brass to Silas Thorne's salvage shop to save his mother's cottage from night intimidation, an old maritime arbitrator decides to enforce his own rules on the harbor.

Fictional story. Contains mild thematic elements involving property intimidation and domestic stress. All characters, places, and events are entirely fictitious.

Chapter 1: The Scrap Tin Contract

The heavy iron door of the barge office swung inward with a metallic groan that cut through the thunder of the Slate Harbor tide. Heavy rain rattled against the corrugated steel roof like handfuls of loose rivets, and the smell of creosote, motor oil, and salt spray filled the cramped workspace. Silas Thorne did not look up immediately from his workbench. He was oiling the teeth of a heavy winch gear, his thick hands stained with graphite and grease. In his line of work, sudden visits after sundown rarely promised quiet news, but men who survived four decades along the northern Maine coastline learned to read the rhythm of a footstep before reaching for a wrench.

The steps were too light to belong to a harbor deputy or a waterfront creditor. They stopped just past the threshold, right on the edge of the ribbed rubber floor mat where the salt water pooled. Silas laid his grease rag across the vise and turned his shoulders slowly.
