The Tillamook Trust

A tension-filled tale of family resilience, hidden financial fraud, and structural truth set in the dense timberlands of the Pacific Northwest.

Fictional story. Depicts themes of elder financial exploitation, deceptive legal coercion, and emotional strain. All characters, places, and events are purely fictional.

Chapter 1: The Cold Timber

The coastal highway leading into Tillamook Ridge was choked with evening fog when Julian Vance pulled his rusty pickup onto the gravel driveway of the family homestead. For three grueling weeks, he had been away in northern California restoring the heavy timber joists of a nineteenth-century courthouse. He was accustomed to the bone-deep ache of long labor, but he had looked forward to the scent of cedar shavings and his Aunt Eleanor’s stovetop cider waiting at the end of the road. Instead, the sprawling cedar-clad house sat dark against the pine canopy, its high gables swallowing the faint twilight.

The front porch lamp, usually left burning night and day, was cold. As Julian killed the engine, he noticed deep, fresh tire ruts etched into the damp gravel—treads far wider than his own truck or his sister Nora’s station wagon. A strange, metallic silence hung over the yard, broken only by the rhythmic drip of mist from the overhang. Inside, there was no hum of the radio, no rattle of teacups, and no greeting from his fifteen-year-old nephew, Oliver, who usually spent his late afternoons tuning ham radios in the sunroom.
