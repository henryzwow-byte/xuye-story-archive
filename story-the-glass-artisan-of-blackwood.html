FICTIONAL STORY · A-293

The Glass Artisan of Blackwood

An artisan dedicated to restoring coastal heritage finds her quiet masterwork recognized by national curators after her family abandons her milestone exhibition.

Fictional story. Themes of emotional neglect, family conflict, professional validation, and personal independence.

Chapter 1: The Dust on the Workbench

The damp salt air of Blackwood Cove always settled heavy in the basement workshop beneath Vance Contracting. It smelled of boiled linseed oil, beeswax, lead came ribbon, and the bitter copper sting of soldering flux. Clara Vance stood over her slate workbench at six in the morning, her thumb tracing the restored bevel of an 1888 cobalt glass prism. For eighteen months, she had lived inside this subterranean silence, painstakingly cleaning sea-salt corrosion from the bronze framework of the St. Jude’s Maritime Chapel rose window and fitting replacement panes ground from historic mid-nineteenth-century stock.

Upstairs, heavy work boots stomped across the subfloor. Her father, Richard Vance, was yelling over the rumble of his commercial diesel truck while her half-brother, Brandon, clattered frying pans in the kitchen. Clara wiped her hands on her canvas apron, carefully covered the glass panel with a felt drop-cloth, and walked up the creaking wooden stairs.
