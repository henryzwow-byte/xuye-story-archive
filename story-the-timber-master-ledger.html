The Timber Master's Ledger

When a prestigious architectural family hides their master-craftsman uncle in a utility pantry to impress rich investors, a young architect takes a stand—uncovering a web of structural fraud and reclaimed legacy.

Fictional story. Contains depictions of family emotional conflict, verbal mistreatment, and corporate disputes.

Chapter 1: The Cold Welcome

The misty gale blowing off the Oregon coast rattled the stained-glass transom above the grand ballroom of Falcon Crest Lodge. Inside, four hundred guests in tailored tuxedos and evening gowns sipped aged pinot noir beneath soaring Douglas fir trusses. Outside the kitchen service entrance, down a narrow, unheated hallway where stainless steel food carts rattled and wet floor mops dripped into zinc buckets, sat my Uncle Samuel.

Samuel was seventy-six years old. He had spent eight hours on a milk-run coastal bus, carrying his wooden carpenter’s toolbox and a weathered canvas satchel, just to celebrate the fifty-year restoration gala of the lodge he had spent four decades physically rebuilding. His hands were thick, scarred, and stained with walnut stain and boiled linseed oil. His tweed coat was clean but worn thin at the elbows, smelled faintly of cedar shavings, and lacked the polished sheen of the luxury fabrics moving through the main hall.
