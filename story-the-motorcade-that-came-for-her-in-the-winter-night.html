FICTIONAL STORY · A-022

My Husband Stripped Me and Cast Me Into the Freezing Night—Thirty Minutes Later, a Rolls-Royce Motorcade Arrived

After five years of control and humiliation, Sophia is assaulted and abandoned in a freezing Manhattan alley by the family she tried to please. One desperate phone call brings her back to the Sterling empire she once left—and gives her the power to rebuild her life and hold her abusers accountable.

Fiction. Contains domestic abuse, coercive control, forced undressing and exposure, cold exposure, infertility stigma, abduction, threats, attempted murder and emotional distress.

Chapter One: The Night the Mask Broke

The slap was so hard that before I even felt the ground, I saw the afterimage of the crystal chandelier above me.

One second, I was standing in the marble kitchen of the luxurious penthouse that I had personally cleaned, decorated, cooked for him for five years, and painstakingly maintained a false peace. The next second, I had fallen on the tiles, my mouth full of blood, my cheeks burning with pain, staring at the broken light and shadow above, and my husband stood condescendingly above me, like a stranger with the face of Ethan Hayes.

“Sign,” he said.

His voice is not loud. That’s why it’s even more despairing. Outside the floor-to-ceiling windows, the winter rain in Manhattan merged with the cold yellow traffic. The interior is bright, luxurious and dead silent. The divorce agreement lay quietly on the island countertop - where I had arranged plates and folded napkins countless times, trying to make an ordinary life worthy of the love I thought we had.

My mother-in-law, Carol, was leaning against the doorframe, wearing a beige sweater I gave her for Christmas. Her lipstick is impeccable. But her smile said otherwise. It was a small, contented smile with surgical precision.

“Don’t delay, Sophia,” she said. “Dignity is not for you.”

Behind her, Ethan’s little sister, Khloe, was holding up her phone, the camera pointed directly at me, her thumb hovering over the screen with the kind of excitement one only gets when watching fireworks or public executions.

“Oh my gosh,” she said, laughing under her breath, “this angle is amazing. Ethan, move a little to the left. We need to get her face.”

Dizzy, I propped myself up with one hand. “What are you doing?”

Chloe tilted her head. “Record the truth.”

“The truth?” I murmured.

Ethan grabbed the agreement, squatted down, and slammed it against my chest. “The truth is I wasted five years on you.”

My hands were gripping the edge of the document and shaking. It is full of dense terms, legal provisions, signature lines and financial figures. My name was printed neatly at the bottom, just waiting to be shredded.

“I don’t understand,” I said. “You kissed me when you left the house this morning.”

“This morning,” Carroll interjected, “he was just being polite.”

Chloe scoffed. I looked at Ethan. Really look at him. Look at the corners of his cold mouth. Look at the disgust in his eyes. Look at his calmness. No guilt. No confusion. No pain. Just extreme impatience, like being disgusted with a late delivery.

Then, he uttered those words, which completely split my life into “before” and “after”.

“You can’t bear children for me, it won’t help my career at all, and you are of no use to me anymore.”

For years, I’d reassured myself that his coldness was due to work pressure, that Carol’s meanness was due to disappointment, that Chloe’s teasing was due to youth and maturity, that every humiliation had its reasons, its bad day, its rational explanation. Love will turn the person who gives the most sincerity into a complete liar.

I stood up slowly, holding on to the island with one hand. “If it’s because of pregnancy preparation, I told you that the doctor said we have other treatment options-”

Ethan’s hand suddenly stretched out and grabbed a fistful of my hair. My scream was slowed down by half a beat.

“Cut the nonsense,” he said.

He dragged me across the polished floor while Chloe held up her phone to film the process while Carol opened the door diligently and efficiently. The cold winter wind blows in like a knife. I grabbed Ethan’s wrist desperately. I begged. I cried bitterly. I promised we could talk tomorrow, see a psychiatrist, start over, anything.

However, he just pulled it roughly and tore the thin silk nightgown I was wearing into pieces. The sound of tearing fabric was as sharp as a sentence. I heard Chloe let out an excited cheer.

I heard Carol laughing wildly. Then, Ethan pushed me into the cold darkness. When I fell heavily on the cement steps outside, the divorce papers were flying behind me like white birds with broken wings.

Then, Carol crossed the threshold, pointed at a pile of stinking garbage in the alley, and said in the cold and pleasant tone of a person who finally spoke his mind:

“That’s where you belong. Let’s see which beggar picks you up.”

Half an hour later, the man who came to pick me up arrived at the alley with a black fleet of Rolls-Royces and a power that could make the entire family kneel down and beg for mercy.

But to understand how I was thrown naked into a cold alley in winter, abandoned like garbage by the man I was willing to die for, you must first know how I got to this point.

You have to know who I really was before I was Sophia Hayes. You have to know the warning I should have heeded five years ago when my grandfather looked Ethan Hayes in the eyes and said those words with fearful certainty:

“That man doesn’t love you. He’s looking at something else through you.”
