The Tideland Charter

Inheriting a weather-beaten Oregon tide observatory while her brother claimed the family's lucrative Seattle marina, a marine restoration engineer uncovers a century-old hydrographic charter concealed within a brass master clockwork that redefines their entire legacy.

Fictional story. Contains family inheritance conflict and mild emotional tension.

Chapter 1: The Discarded Station

The air inside the glass-walled conference room on the thirty-second floor of the Seattle high-rise smelled of polished mahogany, expensive espresso, and quiet dismissiveness. Outside, Puget Sound gleamed under a thin silver veil of morning fog, dotted with container ships and private yachts carving white wakes through the cold water. Inside, the formal reading of Uncle Arthur Vance’s last will and testament had just concluded, leaving a sharp, heavy stillness in the room.

My older brother, Julian, leaned back in his leather chair, adjusting the gold links at his cuffs. A smirk worked its way onto his lips, unhidden and deliberate. He had inherited the crown jewel of the Vance estate: the full controlling share of the waterfront commercial holdings in downtown Seattle, including the lucrative North Harbor Marina complex. It was a portfolio evaluated in the tens of millions, promising endless dividends and political prestige along the coast.
