The Tide-Water Ledger

When a retired marine engineer is publicly humiliated at his own boathouse by his ambitious stepson, he uses four decades of physical paper records to dismantle a covert financial scheme and reclaim his legacy.

Fictional story. Depicts themes of familial betrayal, financial deceit, and psychological conflict. All characters, places, and events are entirely fictional.

Chapter 1: The Rust on the Ledger

Silas Thorne spent forty-two years listening to the sound of oak hulls shifting against the cedar pilings of Cape Cliff, Maine. He was a marine engineer by training and a boatbuilder by habit, the sort of man who kept calipers in his shirt pocket and recorded every bolt replacement, timber shipment, and tide variance in leather-bound ledger books. His boathouse stood on a narrow spit of granite overlooking Heron Cove, built with reclaimed white pine thirty years ago alongside his wife, Clara. When Clara died after a quiet illness, the workshop remained his anchor—a place where wood grain and precise measurements governed the day.

Trouble did not arrive in Silas's life with a loud crack; it accumulated slowly, like salt crusting over iron fittings. Two years after Clara passed, Silas's stepson, Derek, pulled into the gravel driveway in a sleek rental sedan. Beside him was Genevieve, a sharp-eyed consultant with polished speech and expensive boots. Derek claimed he wanted to honor his late mother’s memory by founding a heritage boatbuilding sanctuary. He spoke of preserving traditional craftsmanship and taking the heavy physical burdens off Silas's aging shoulders. Silas, missing the echo of footsteps in the drafty house, unlocked the loft room and let them move in.
