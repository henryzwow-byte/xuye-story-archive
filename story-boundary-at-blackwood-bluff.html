Boundary at Blackwood Bluff

When a maritime engineer returns to her Oregon cliffside workshop only to find her half-brother hosting an unpermitted corporate gala on her property, she uses legal documentation and municipal enforcement to reclaim her sanctuary.

Fictional story. Depicts themes of family conflict, real estate trespass, and legal boundary enforcement.

Chapter 1: The Return to Blackwood Bluff

After forty-two days surveying deep-water concrete caissons along the icy swells of the Alaskan shelf, all Nora Sterling wanted was forty-eight hours of quiet coastal fog, the smell of damp pine needles, and the solitary comfort of her restored timber workshop. She had spent three years turning the old cedar orchard barn at Blackwood Bluff into her personal sanctuary, rebuilding every beam, sanding down every floorboard, and fitting the stone fireplace with her own hands between long maritime inspection contracts. The twelve-acre property sitting high above the Tillamook coastline was the only place on earth where her mind could drop its hyper-vigilant gear.

Her old green pickup hummed as it climbed the winding gravel driveway, the ocean mist hanging low in the fir canopy. Nora looked forward to simple things: a scalding shower with unscented soap, black coffee brewed in her heavy stoneware mug, and an evening spent quiet by the woodstove, listening to the tide wash against the basalt rocks below.
