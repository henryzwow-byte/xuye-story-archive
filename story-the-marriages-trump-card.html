FICTIONAL STORY · A-067

The Marriage's Trump Card

On the second morning of her marriage, Serena Vale's new husband slaps her in front of his family. He does not know that Serena's private holding company owns the debt, property and financial lifelines behind his family's empire—or that the agreement he signed can freeze them the moment he becomes violent.

Fiction. Contains domestic violence, coercive control, family conflict, emotional abuse, financial consequences, divorce and legal proceedings.

Chapter One: Coffee on the Marble Floor

My name is Serena Vale, and I saw my husband for who he really is before the wedding flowers even began to wither. That slap happened on the morning after the wedding. Not after years of resentment.

Not after a long marriage has rotted away. Just forty-eight hours after Daniel Cole stood under the white rose tree, held my hand, and promised to always respect me. We were at his family’s lakefront estate in Connecticut, a house so large it was more wings than hallways. Daniel insisted that we put the first month of marriage there.

“Our family is old-school,” he told me before the wedding, “but they love deeply. Give them some time.”

Controlling families always start like this. They first ask you for some time. Then, they use this time to teach you what the rules are. That morning, I walked into the marble-lined kitchen and found Vanessa Cole sitting at the island in silk pajamas, scrolling through her phone. Dirty dishes, coffee cups and half-eaten snacks were piled around her. It looked like a crime scene left behind by a group of royals after a party.

Daniel’s mother, Margaret, was sitting at the breakfast table drinking tea and reading the newspaper. His father, Arthur, was browsing the stock market on a tablet. At first, no one paid any attention to me.

I poured myself a cup of coffee. Vanessa snapped her fingers without looking up.

“Since you’re standing, flush those plates.”

I looked at the dishes in the sink. He looked at her again.

“That’s what you used. You can wash it yourself.”

The kitchen fell into dead silence for a moment. Margaret slowly put down her teacup. Vanessa laughed, as if I had told a funny joke.

“What did you say?”

“I said, you can wash your own dishes.”

Arthur closed the tablet’s leather case. The corners of Margaret’s mouth pursed into a straight line.

“You just got here, Serena. We don’t talk to people like that in our family.”

“I was talking to her like an adult.”

The smile on Vanessa’s face disappeared. She picked up the coffee cup, tilted it deliberately, and poured the remaining coffee directly onto the smooth marble floor. The brown liquid quickly spread on the white ground.

“Keep this clean too, redneck.”

For a moment, all I could hear in my ears was the faint sound of coffee dripping down the edge of the island. Then Daniel walked in. He wore a navy blue cardigan and wore that charming smile reserved only for investors. His eyes passed from the coffee on the ground to Vanessa’s proud expression, and finally stopped at my empty hands.

“What’s wrong?”

Vanessa’s eyes turned red.

“She was telling me what to do.”

Daniel turned to me.

“You asked my sister to clean up?”

“I just told his sister not to treat me like a servant.”

His jaw tensed momentarily. That’s an small change. But I noticed. Daniel has always been impeccable in front of others. He raved about my career at dinner parties, he would hold doors open for me, and he was full of great things to say about relationships. In front of those with status, he would even praise me as a talented woman.

But in the private room, the mask of public camouflage can always be torn off. He grabbed my wrist firmly.

“Daniel, let go.”

He dragged me forcefully from the kitchen to the living room, where his relatives were gathering for breakfast. His strength was so strong that my arms hurt.

“How dare you yell at my sister!” he roared, “Find out your identity!”

Then, he slapped me in the face. My body hit the carpet hard. The lip collided with the teeth and split on the spot. While his family was watching all this in stunned silence, Vanessa stood at the door of the kitchen and sneered slightly.

That smile marked the end of my marriage at this second.
