The Saltmill Covenant

A dedicated restoration woodworker in coastal Oregon discovers her mother secretly pledged her workshop's historic land deed as mortgage collateral for her older brother's speculative resort enterprise. Unwilling to let her life's work be demolished, she uncovers a hidden preservation covenant left by her late mentor.

Fictional story. Contains themes of familial financial betrayal, emotional conflict, and property disputes.

Chapter 1: The Eviction on the Tide

The morning fog over Port Mercer hung low against the shoreline, smelling of damp cedar, salt spray, and yellow pine pitch. Nora Vane stood over her workbench in the Saltmill, a restored nineteenth-century tide-driven timber shop on the edge of the harbor. Her hands were grey with fine oak dust as she planed the curvature of a transom beam for a local fishing schooner. The rhythmic scrape of the iron blade was the only sound besides the lap of water under the floorboards. At twenty-four, Nora had spent six years rebuilding this shop plank by plank, learning traditional boatbuilding and timber restoration from its previous owner, old Caleb Thorne.

The quiet broke when a heavy man in a dark trench coat walked down the damp gravel ramp holding a thick Manila envelope. He didn't ask for a quote or admire the hand-hewn douglas fir rafters. He handed Nora a notice printed on crisp blue bond paper with a red court seal across the header. It was a formal Notice of Foreclosure and Intent to Seize Property, issued by Pacific Tide Capital. The document declared that the parcel of land housing the Saltmill was encumbered by a delinquent three-hundred-and-twenty-thousand-dollar commercial mortgage, and public auction proceedings were scheduled to begin in thirty days.
