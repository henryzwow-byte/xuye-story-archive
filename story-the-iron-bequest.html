The Iron Bequest

An architectural restorer in a small coastal town defends her historic timber studio against her brother's predatory redevelopment scheme after discovering an iron ledger vault beneath the workshop floor.

Fictional story. Contains themes of family conflict, financial deception, and contested property rights.

Chapter 1: The Notice on the Pier

The morning mist off Pine Cove had turned the cedar shingles of the old harbor workshop a dark, saturated gray. Clara Vance stood at her heavy cast-iron bandsaw, tracing the fine annual rings of a reclaimed Douglas fir beam. Salt air drifted through the cracked transom window above her workbench, mixing with the sharp, resinous smell of freshly shaved timber. At thirty-two, Clara had spent seven years breathing life back into this building, replacing rotted sill plates, hand-planing floorboards, and restoring the historic waterfront studio her late uncle, Arthur Vance, had established fifty years before.

The heavy oak double doors creaked open, breaking the rhythmic hum of Clara’s hand plane. Julian walked in first, his polished leather boots clicking crisply against the dust-dusted floorboards. He wore a tailored charcoal overcoat that looked entirely out of place among the iron clamps and stacks of cured timber. Beside him stood Aunt Beatrice, her silver hair styled into an immovable helmet, her gold ring catching the cold light filtering through the dock windows. Behind them lingered a young man wearing a high-visibility vest and clutching a heavy brass surveyor’s tripod.
