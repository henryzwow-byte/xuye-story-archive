FICTIONAL STORY · A-180

The Timber Ledger

Estranged boatbuilder Callum Vance faces his stepmother's corporate takeover of his late father's maritime estate, only to discover an ancient tide-land trust that alters everything.

Fictional story. Contains themes of corporate manipulation, family estate conflict, and emotional estrangement.

Chapter 1: The Salt and the Steel

Callum Vance stood on the wet timber dock of Oakhaven Harbor, watching thick morning fog roll across the gray, churned waters of Cascade Sound. Five days had passed since his father, Arthur Vance—the formidable founder of Vance Maritime & Timber—was laid to rest in the small coastal chapel above the bay. For twelve long years, Callum had worked independently three miles down the shoreline, earning a modest living restoring vintage wooden working craft in a rented berth. As a young man, he had stood beside his father learning how to steam oak ribs and lay cedar planking for heavy tugboats. Yet he had been effectively banished from the corporate boardrooms by Victoria Vance, the sharp, elegant woman Arthur had married five years after Callum’s mother passed away. Victoria had spent a decade quietly recasting Arthur’s legacy, transforming a gritty timber-and-tug fleet built on hard physical labor into a polished private equity holding firm operating from urban glass towers.

Inside the harbor estate’s newly renovated glass overlook, Victoria sat with controlled poise behind a polished rosewood conference table. She wore a tailored black wool coat and dark pearl earrings that caught the pale coastal light. Beside her sat her thirty-four-year-old son, Julian Vance, dressed in bespoke Italian wool. Julian leaned back in his leather chair, casually scrolling through luxury vehicle listings on a glowing tablet while tapping his polished dress shoe against the baseboard. Positioned at the far end of the table was Gavin Ward, a slick corporate receiver brought in by Victoria’s legal team, who methodically arranged stacks of digital asset summaries and notarized balance sheets. When Callum walked into the room wearing his rain-dampened work jacket and steel-toed boots, Julian did not look up, while Victoria offered a thin, practiced smile that carried no genuine warmth.
