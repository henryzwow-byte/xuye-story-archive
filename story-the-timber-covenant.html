The Timber Covenant

An aged Oregon shipbuilder faces public humiliation by his estranged daughter at a corporate investor gala. Rather than retaliating with anger, he relies on a forgotten land trust covenant to reclaim his workshop and teach a decisive lesson in integrity.

Fictional story. Contains themes of family estrangement, emotional betrayal, and commercial property disputes.

Chapter 1: The Gathering at the Estuary

The smell of cured white oak, damp river silt, and oil-rubbed brass had lived in the rafters of Cedar Reach Workshop for more than forty years. Arthur Vance knew every seam and timber pin of the building. He had framed the main barn himself in the summer of 1984, driving heavy iron bolts through heartwood Douglas fir while the Columbia River tide turned cold and grey outside the double bay doors.

At sixty-six, Arthur was a man of few words and slow, deliberate movements. His hands were thick and heavily calloused, mapped with faded white scars from chisels, hand planes, and wire ropes—the hands of a master shipwright who had spent four decades restoring historic wooden craft for Pacific Northwest fisheries and private collectors. He lived his life by simple, unyielding measurements: plumb, square, and true.
