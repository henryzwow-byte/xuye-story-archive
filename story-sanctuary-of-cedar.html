Sanctuary of Cedar

After fourteen years of disciplined joinery work, Nora Vance purchases a historic timber workshop in the Cascade Range. But when her stepmother attempts to commandeer the sanctuary for her son, Nora defends her space with legal authority and quiet resilience.

Fictional story. Depicts family boundary conflict and emotional displacement without violence.

Chapter 1: The Mountain Sanctuary

The rain over the Cascade Range fell in steady, resin-scented sheets, drumming against the heavy cedar beams of the workshop lodge Nora Vance had bought with fourteen years of hard-won savings. Inside, the air smelled of sawdust, linseed oil, dried pine needles, and the faint warmth of cast-iron stove embers. For the first time in her adult life, Nora stood beneath a roof where every tenoned joint and mortised timber belonged entirely to her.

She was thirty-four, with hands calloused from timber-frame restoration and custom joinery. She had spent over a decade turning down social weekends, living in drafty rented rooms, and banking every stipend from her historic preservation contracts into an untouched reserve. The closing attorney in the mountain county seat of Pine Ridge had handed her the thick envelope two weeks prior, noting with dry admiration that few independent artisans secured a commercial timber property without a bank mortgage or family guarantor.
