FICTIONAL STORY · A-240

The Brass Chronometer

When restorer Silas Thorne returns to liquidate his late grandfather's clock tower workshop in Highland Creek, he discovers a runaway teenager hiding in the loft with a mechanical music box containing secret land surveys.

Fictional story. Contains mild themes of past family grief, corporate pressure, and property trespassing. All characters, locations, and events are entirely fictional products of creative imagination.

Chapter 1: The Dust on the Brass

Silas Thorne pulled his coat tight against the cold drizzle blowing off the valley ridge. The iron key to the Highland Creek Horology Workshop turned in the lock with a dry, grinding resistance that felt like breaking bone. For three years, he had avoided this three-story timber building nestled beneath the shadow of the mountain. His grandfather, Arthur Thorne, had died here in solitude, leaving behind a legendary collection of tower clocks, brass gears, and unfulfilled promises. Silas, now thirty-four and working as a quiet museum conservator in Seattle, had convinced himself that liquidating the inventory was purely a business matter. But stepping over the threshold into the damp, cedar-scented dark, he knew the silence inside was heavier than any financial debt.

The ground floor was a cathedral of halted time. Dozens of pendulum clocks lined the heartwood shelves, their pendulum bobs suspended like frozen raindrops. Tall grandfather cabinets stood like solemn guards against the walls, while workbench drawers lay half-open, spilling copper springs, oiled rags, and glass loupes across the workbench. Silas switched on his flashlight, the yellow beam cutting through years of settled sawdust and dried machine oil. In the center of the room sat his grandfather’s masterwork: a six-foot tall astronomical clock wrapped in hand-carved mahogany. Its glass panels were dark, its chimes muted by decades of grease.
