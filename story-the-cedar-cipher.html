The Cedar Cipher

Returning to his Maine boatyard after a salvage trip, Julian Vance discovers his stroke-recovering father working under grueling secret quotas in an unheated barn for his wife's design business. A carved cipher in the timber reveals the truth, forcing Julian to confront the betrayal.

Fictional story. Depicts elder exploitation, emotional mistreatment, family conflict, and physical strain.

Chapter 1: The Hidden Mark

The damp salt breeze off Penobscot Bay had always smelled like home, but on the rainy Tuesday Julian Vance returned to his coastal boatyard, the air inside the workshop felt heavy and cold. For three weeks, Julian had been up in northern Maine negotiating log salvages for a maritime museum project. His boots were still caked with river mud as he unlocked the heavy oak doors of Vance Joinery, expecting the quiet stillness of an idle shop. Instead, his gaze immediately landed on a mahogany console table sitting on the center workbench, waiting for its final coats of hand-rubbed wax.

The table was a piece of extraordinary craftsmanship—slender tapered legs, intricate dovetail joinery, and a hand-planed top with a grain pattern that rippled like dark water. Attached to the corner was a shipping tag from one of Portland’s most exclusive interior design boutiques, owned by Julian's wife, Evelyn. The tag listed a sale price of nine thousand dollars, with Evelyn’s company logo stamped across the cardstock. Julian smiled faintly, assuming Evelyn had hired one of the regional master joiners while he was away to keep up with her showroom inventory.
