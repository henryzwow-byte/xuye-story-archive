The Tide Mill Ledger

When a widowed carpenter in an Oregon coastal town risks everything to care for a homeless former draftsman, a forgotten set of architectural tracings unravels a town dispute and restores two broken families.

Fictional story. Depicts grief, economic hardship, and minor accidental danger.

Chapter 1: The Timber at the Salvage Yard

The rain along the southern coast of Oregon rarely fell in gentle drops; it drove sideways off the Pacific, carrying the heavy scent of crushed spruce and saltwater. Clara Finch stood in the muddy yard of Oakhaven Marine Salvage, clutching a wet clipboard under her canvas work coat. Her late husband, Paul, had spent twelve years building Finch Joinery, a modest timber-framing workshop built over the tidal mudflats of the bay. Since his heart failed him two winters ago, Clara had tried to keep the saws running, balancing heavy timber contracts with the relentless demands of three growing children.

That afternoon, everything was slipping. Her middle son, Ethan, ten years old and prone to sudden asthmatic wheezing in the damp cold, had accompanied her to inspect a stack of salvaged reclaimed Douglas fir posts. Lucas, fourteen and burdened with a quiet sense of premature adult responsibility, was home keeping six-year-old Daisy away from the woodstove.
